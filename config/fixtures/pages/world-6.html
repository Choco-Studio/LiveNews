Japan's bullet trains mark a record year for punctuality

Rail operators in Japan say their bullet trains ran with an average delay of under one minute last year, a record. More than 160 million passengers used the network. The fastest trains on the line reach 320 kilometres an hour. Operators credit new signalling and longer maintenance windows at night. The network carries passengers between Tokyo and Osaka in about two and a half hours.

The figure includes delays caused by weather, such as snow in the mountains. Operators say careful maintenance at night, when no trains run, keeps the lines in good condition. The first bullet train line opened in 1964, linking Tokyo and Osaka.
