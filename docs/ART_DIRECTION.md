# GLOBIT 24 — Art direction for the studio and graphics

What real newscasts do, and the rules we apply to the 384x216 pixel-art studio. Written on 2026-10-02 from set photos, design-agency case studies and on-air frames of the channels below. NewscastStudio (NCS) pages refuse automated fetching, so their claims here come from search summaries of those pages. Everything else was read or looked at directly.

## 1. The essence

**Shared by credible news sets:**

- **There is one hero per shot.** BBC Studio B has one portrait LED tower, CNN London a desk in front of one LED backing, ABC one curved map wall and NBC one curved headline segment. Sets can be large, but each camera shot frames a single focal element next to the presenter.
- **The presenter is the brightest and warmest thing in the frame.** In every frame we looked at, faces are warm and lit evenly from the front. The background is cooler, darker and out of focus: navy maps at ABC/NBC, a blue LED texture at Al Jazeera English, a night skyline at the BBC. Studios put colour on the scenery and white light on people.
- **Each set has one brand colour plus neutrals.** BBC uses red with white and near-black, NHK red with grey and glass, Sky and DW blue with white, and CNN London blue and white with one red logo block. The brand colour appears as a few flat, deliberate blocks. It is never a glow spread over everything.
- **Negative space is designed in.** The zone around a presenter's head is calm. Logos and graphics sit in the empty third beside the presenter: NHK's logo panel, ABC's title next to Muir, NBC's "Nightly" lock-up.
- **Materials are real.** BBC wanted a set that was "real, that you could touch" ([TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/live-production/we-wanted-a-studio-that-was-physical-not-virtual-behind-the-design-and-technology-of-bbc-news-studio-b)). CNN London has brick, I-beams and a parquet floor. Al Jazeera's newsroom has white curved surfaces. Glossy dark floors are near-universal.
- **Graphics are calm, low and consistent.** There is a small bug, one strap system, one clock and a ticker. Breaking news is shown by a colour change, not by fireworks.

**What makes a set look cheap or "AI-made":**

- Screens everywhere. Devlin Design Group says many sets look alike because they are "either completely virtual or just covered in LED monitors" ([SVG, 2026](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/05/19/inside-studio-design-trends-and-how-to-make-the-right-moves-with-devlin-design-group/)). At 384 px each extra screen becomes noise.
- Everything glowing at the same intensity, so the eye has no light direction to follow and nowhere to rest.
- Background as bright or as saturated as the faces, which turns presenters into silhouettes.
- No empty areas. Every corner holds a gadget: windows, clocks, charts, beams.
- Random sci-fi interface: fake data readouts, rings and scanlines that carry no information.
- Many unrelated motions at once: blinking LEDs, traffic, aircraft, beams, pulsing lights.

## 2. Reference notes

Frames marked "frames" are YouTube auto-thumbnails of official uploads. The local files are listed in the report.

**BBC News (Studio B 2022, Studio E/C, 2023 channel)**
- Jago Design's brief: "memorable, modern, uncluttered and instantly recognisable as BBC News" ([Jago](https://jagodesign.co.uk/projects/bbc-national-news-2)). The palette is a near-black glossy floor, white walls and flat red slabs. The portrait LED tower is the hero. Each bulletin's identity is a single numeral on the wall ("6", "10").
- Studio E (2013) used the live newsroom as its backdrop ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2013/03/21/bbc-news-debuts-studio-e-at-broadcasting-house/)). In 2023 frames, the newsroom sits behind the MCU out of focus and darker than the presenter.
- On-air frames: the wide shot has a standing presenter in the right third and a big video wall on the left. The MCU has a soft London skyline behind. Titles use a white field, a red/white globe and thin city-network lines ([frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5DvEeP0RAp8), [frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sfyl01wS8eI)).
- Graphics: the 2019 Reith redesign has high-contrast straps, and the ticker was replaced by headlines shown "individually" (a flipper). Breaking news turns the strap red. The countdown carries the BBC pips; its extended version runs 87 s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News_presentation), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News_(British_TV_channel))).

**Sky News (Studio 21 "glass box")**
- A minimal set with glass on three sides and one LED explainer wall. The desk rotates and changes height but keeps "a refined and minimal aesthetic" ([Jago](https://jagodesign.co.uk/projects/sky-news-2), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News)).
- On air: a top-left bug ("sky" plus a red "news" box) and a small LIVE / time / location tag. The bottom ticker is white with a skynews.com plate, turning yellow with black text for top stories. Programme titles are bold condensed white caps on a deep blue band ([frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JnmYgbYazV0), [frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J27Pe31QCDo)).

**CNN International (London, Old Street)**
- One large central desk rotates for three backings. The desk is a white lightbox with a single red CNN block. Brick, I-beams and parquet add texture ([Jago](https://jagodesign.co.uk/projects/cnn-london)).
- Lighting is soft, "relatively flat" ambient light at about 5000 K, ellipsoidals for facial definition, and coloured pars only as "pools" on the architecture ([TM Broadcast](https://tmbroadcast.com/cnn-london-lighting-workflow/)).
- 2023 graphics: white banners, the bug at bottom right and a red flag at the far left. Over-light type was criticised and thickened in August 2023 ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2023/06/01/cnn-new-graphics-chryons-lower-thirds/), [NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2023/08/15/cnn-graphics-update-august-2023/)). Legibility beats elegance.

**Al Jazeera English (Doha)**
- Curvilinear white architecture and long curved LED walls, with the working newsroom as the set ([designboom](https://www.designboom.com/architecture/veech-x-veech-al-jazeera-network-studio-building-doha-05-10-2017/), [NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2022/04/21/al-jazeera-studio-redesign/)).
- The 2019 MCU frames show a centred presenter in front of an out-of-focus blue LED texture. The gold logo plus an orange ticker sit at bottom left ([frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BX8-wu03pjo)).
- The 2022 package uses one device, a square taken from the logo, as the "conductive" element across all screens ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2022/04/25/al-jazeera-branding-broadcast-design/)).

**NHK World-Japan (NY bureau)**
- NHK red runs through the anchor background, a floor stripe and small accents. The studio is glass-walled onto the newsroom ([Provost Studio](https://provost-studio.com/work/nhk-world-japan-news-headquarters-new-york-bureau/)).
- The desk is L-shaped with a glass top and a lit front panel carrying the logo. In the MCU render, the presenter is offset right and a red logo panel fills the empty left third.

**DW News (Berlin)**
- A 24 m² curved LED wall set to 3200 K to match the studio light. It is one wall, integrated into the set ([PresseBox](https://www.pressebox.com/pressrelease/eyevis-gmbh/DW-News-Studio-with-High-Resolution-eyevis-LED-Video-Wall/boxid/769838)).
- Frames: a glossy desk, a pale image behind the anchor, and the "DW NEWS" wordmark in the empty half. The 2024 refresh adds yellow to DW blue and the Pangea typeface ([frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwaqfok-pNA), [German Design Award](https://www.german-design-award.com/en/gallery/detail/corporate-identity/dw-corporate-design-refresh-2024)).

**France 24**
- Blue-grey glass-and-steel sets. The 2013 package used a black tickerbar plus a white "FRANCE24.COM" bar ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_24)). The 2024 Gédéon package is built from news images, "not one point of view but multiple" ([France Médias Monde](https://www.francemediasmonde.com/fr/actualites/france-24-change-de-visage-lhumain-et-la-confiance-au-coeur-de-la-nouvelle-offre-editoriale-et-du-nouvel-habillage-de-la-chaine/)).
- An older promo shows world times as a single graphic strip (Washington / London / Cairo / New Delhi), not as clocks on the wall ([frame](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=StY4EmXUqEo)).

**Euronews**
- Euronews works pictures-first: "No Comment" and voice-over bulletins, and in-vision anchors were phased out of weekday bulletins by 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews)).
- The 2023 look is a mobile-centric square layout with centred captions ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/commercial/news/unveiling-the-new-euronews-2)). Frames show a small top-right bug, a small blue topic tag and a big white condensed headline set straight over the picture ([frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=apiaIgKLobQ)).

**ABC World News Tonight**
- The Hudson Square studio (2025) kept the formula: an L-shaped desk, a large curved LED behind the anchor, a world map and a moody skyline ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2025/03/10/abc-world-news-tonight-studio-hudson-square/)).
- Frames: the anchor sits left of centre in front of a dark navy map, with the show title in the right third. The lower third is a small red tag ("LIVE ET" or "BREAKING NEWS"), then a big white caps line on navy, then a smaller line, with the round bug at right. The whole block covers about 16% of frame height ([frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-Ez_cARboo)).
- Sound: a four-note signature used since 1978 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_World_News_Tonight)).

**NBC Nightly News (Studio 3A, May 2026)**
- The anchor is seated at the corner of an L-desk. A curved wall segment beside him carries the headline and picture, so the over-the-shoulder graphic is physical. A U-shaped LED ribbon frames the studio ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/05/19/nbc-nightly-news-top-story-studio-3a/)).
- Frames: the anchor sits in the right third, with a red "BREAKING NEWS" tag and a white condensed headline on the wall at left. The 2023 "N" emblem was designed "to create fewer distractions". The theme has been John Williams' "The Mission" since 1985 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Nightly_News), [frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZMJtrc0Sf0g)).

**Retro: BBC Nine O'Clock News 1985/88, ITN News at Ten 1988, NBC 1985**
- BBC 1985 titles: chunky bevelled gold type over Earth from orbit, with a "darker overall style" for the late bulletin ([Ravensbourne](https://www.ravensbourne.ac.uk/bbc-motion-graphics-archive/nine-oclock-news-1985)).
- BBC 1988 and ITN 1988 sets ([frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ox-i25kSdHw), [frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PFWT2oPhf1w)): the presenter MCU sits left of centre on a flat graded blue background with one bevelled picture box top right. Nothing else is in frame. News at Ten separated its headlines with Big Ben's bongs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_News_at_Ten)).
- NBC 1985 ([frames](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=THtyqTek-3Y)): a dim warm newsroom with soft CRT glows behind a brightly lit anchor.
- Lesson: one person, one box, a graded backdrop. This is the most pixel-art-friendly grammar there is.

## 3. Rules for our 384x216 studio

**Hero and screens (strict)**
- **The video wall (x 140–244, y 18–80) is the only screen on set.** There are no side monitors, no city windows and no wall clocks. World time belongs in the graphics package.
- **Each shot shows at most one active image area.** That is the wall in studio shots, or the full-screen picture.
- **Non-screen accents are limited to three:** the desk-front logo plate, one accent line (desk LED or wall bezel) and a pair of symmetric practical lights. They are static.

**Wide shot (two presenters at x≈118 and x≈266)**
- **Bands:**
  - y 0–10 is ceiling, black to ink, with at most one static 1 px grid line.
  - y 18–80 is the wall, centred on x=192.
  - y 118 is the desk top, with a 1 px silver highlight.
  - y 150–216 is the darkest, quietest zone, because the lower third (166–197) and the ticker (202–216) sit over it.
- **Head zones:** x 80–140 and 244–304, y 40–110, behind the heads. Keep them calm and mid-dark (ink to slate, with one soft light pool from above). Put no edges, text or high-contrast seams within 6 px of a head outline.
- **Sides:** x 0–60 and 324–384 fall off to black. At most one vertical architectural line per side, mirrored.
- **Symmetry:** the set is symmetric about x=192. Asymmetry comes only from the presenters and the wall content.
- **Solo shot at x=192:** the head overlaps the wall's bottom edge. Wall content must keep its bottom 16 px dark (black to ink, no text or logo), and the presenter gets the rim light described below. Put wall titles and logos in the upper half or in the third away from the head (the ABC/NBC/NHK pattern).

**Palette discipline**
- **Per frame:** neutrals (black, ink, slate, steel, fog, silver, white), skin tones, brand red `#e43b44` (with `#a22633` for shadow), one programme accent and the logo's yellow bit.
- **Saturated colour** (red, accent, cyan, yellow) covers at most about 10% of set pixels in the wide shot. Wall content and clothing are excluded.
- **Brand red** appears only in flat blocks: the bug, LIVE/BREAKING tags, the desk logo plate and at most one slab or line on the set. It never fills a gradient or a glow.
- **Navy `#124e89`** is a wall-content base colour, not a set colour. The set's neutrals are ink, slate and steel.

**Lighting recipe (pixel art)**
- **Key light** comes from upper front, slightly camera-left, and is the same for every presenter and close-up. Faces are flat-lit: one shadow tone (skinShade or tanShade), 1–2 px wide, on the side away from the key. Never tint faces.
- **Rim light** is 1 px of silver `#c0cbdc` along the top of the hair and the right shoulder line. It is mandatory on dark suits (navy and ink suits otherwise vanish into slate). Cream `#ead4aa` is allowed for warm programmes.
- **Value ranges (CIE L\*):**

  | Area | L\* range | Palette |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Head-zone background | 18–45 | ink to steel |
  | Wall average | ≤ 45 | highlights up to 85 on at most 10% of the wall |
  | Faces | 55–73 | skin, tan |
  | Pure white | — | collars, eye glints, logo and text only |
  | Floor | ≤ 18 | plus reflections ≤ 30 |

- **Gradients are vertical:** the top of the set is darker than the head zone, which is darker than the faces. There is one soft pool of light per presenter on the wall behind them. Light falls off to the frame edges.
- **The wall is dimmed and cool**, as real LED walls are balanced to camera (DW's is matched to 3200 K). The wall throws at most a 10–15% alpha wash on the desk top. It never lights faces.

**Materials via limited dithering**
- **Bayer 4x4** is the only dither. It mixes two adjacent ramp steps (black↔ink, ink↔slate, slate↔steel) and is used only for light falloff on walls, wall glow and floor gradients.
- **Never dither** faces, text, logos, desk edges or anything smaller than 8 px. No random noise.
- **Material vocabulary:**
  - Matte panels are flat colour with seams.
  - Glossy floor is horizontal 1 px reflection streaks at 25–35% of the source.
  - Metal is a 1 px silver highlight over steel.
  - Glass is a 1 px edge highlight plus a 10–15% overlay.

**Motion budget**
- **May move:** presenters (blink, lips, gestures), wall content, ticker and clock, plus one ambient set effect at most (a light sweep or ring pulse no more often than every 20 s, or a practical light "breathing" with a period of at least 6 s and a change of at most one palette step).
- **Must stay still:** architecture, rig, beams (delete moving beams), desk, logos on set and the camera. Pixel-art camera drift jitters. The only camera-like move is the slow pan on full-screen photos.
- **Squint test:** in a still frame of a presenter talking, at most three zones move: presenters, wall, graphics.

**Re-dressing by programme (accent, light tint and wall content only)**

| Programme | Accent | Tint on scenery (not faces) | Wall idle | Wall in story |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WORLD NOW | red `#e43b44` | none or navy ≤ 10% (the home look) | slow globe or world map with London marker | story picture or locator map |
| TECH BYTES | cyan `#2ce8f5` (thin lines only) | blue `#0099db` ≤ 10% | static two-tone chip/grid motif | story picture |
| COSMOS DESK | magenta `#b55088` / pink text | purple ≤ 12%, set one step darker | starfield + planet, near-still | picture or diagram |
| MONEY MINUTE | green `#63c74d` | warm yellow ≤ 8% | index line chart (changes per story, never jitters) | chart or picture |
| NEWS IN 60 | yellow `#feae34` (black text on it) | orange ≤ 8% | 60-second ring that ticks once per second | picture |

Real precedents for this approach are BBC's per-bulletin numeral and Devlin's backlit panels that change colour "without complete redesigns". The tech accent moves from magenta to cyan so it no longer collides with COSMOS purple.

## 4. Graphics package

- **Margins:** use EBU action-safe (13 px sides, 8 px top/bottom) as the minimum for the bug, clock and text. Use graphics-safe (19 / 11 px) for straps ([EBU R95](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r095.pdf)).
- **Bug:** top left at (13, 8), 13 px high, at most 48 px wide. It is static. The glint plays only at programme start and on return from a break. The LIVE tag sits next to it with a static red square (on-air LIVE tags don't blink). The programme name appears beside the bug for about 8 s after the open and after breaks, then hides.
- **Clock:** one clock, top right, studio time (London) as HH:MM with a static colon. No rotating world cities. World times appear only as a graphic strip in WORLD NOW, France 24 style.
- **Lower third:**
  - The tag row is 11 px (category, source or BREAKING; accent or red).
  - The headline bar is 17 px: white 5x7 caps on ink or black, or black on white. It starts at x=19 and ends at x≤365.
  - An optional sub-line is 10 px in micro type.
  - Headlines are at most about 45 characters.
  - The presenter's name super appears only the first time each presenter speaks in an episode, held 5 s. It does not repeat every story.
- **Lower-third timing:** the bar wipes in left to right in 0.35 s (ease-out, integer pixels), and the text appears 0.1 s after the bar. The headline stays while the story is on air. On a story change, flip the text in 0.3 s without removing the bar. Exit in 0.25 s. No bounce.
- **Ticker:** a 14 px band at y 202. Prefer a BBC-style flipper (one item at a time, 0.3 s push) held for 1.5 s plus one word per 0.4 s. That is slower than the BBC subtitle rate of about 3 words/s, because pixel type is harder to read. If it scrolls, keep it at 30–40 px/s. Keep the yellow "LATEST" plate.
- **Breaking:** the tag turns red and reads "BREAKING". There is an optional full-screen card of at most 3 s with a sting. The banner may pulse at most twice on entry, at least 0.36 s apart (Ofcom's 9-frame rule; transitions to and from saturated red are flagged as risky, see [Ofcom guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/16248/gn_flash.pdf)). After that it stays steady. Never flash continuously.
- **Stingers (0.8 s):** only for the programme open and close, going into and out of breaks, and breaking news. Between stories and between shots, cut.
- **Programme open:** real title animations run 5–15 s, and some flagships add a 30–60 s headline sequence before them. Ours is 4 s. Keep these elements:
  1. A 3–5 note sonic signature shared by all programmes and re-orchestrated per show (BBC pips/Lowe, ABC four notes, NBC "The Mission").
  2. One motion idea in the programme accent.
  3. A logo and title lock-up that resolves by 3.2 s and holds still until 4.0 s.
  4. A hard cut on the downbeat to the wide two-shot.

  Drop particles, multiple text moves and fades to grey.
- **Typography:** three levels at most, and at most two per screen.
  - Display is 5x7 at 2x (14 px), for titles and full-screen cards.
  - Body is 5x7 at 1x, for straps and the ticker.
  - Micro is 3x5, for source, time and metadata only, never for essential information.
  - Accent colours go on bars, not on text. Allowed text pairs are yellow on black, black on yellow, and white or cream on dark.

## 5. Camera and directing grammar (one bulletin)

1. **Cold open:** headline montage over the presenter's voice, 2.6–4 s per headline. Cut, don't wipe, between headlines.
2. **Open:** 4 s, then a hard cut to the **wide two-shot**. "Good evening, I'm Paco / and I'm Lola", held 4–8 s.
3. **Story link:** a **single** of the speaking presenter for the first one or two sentences (at least 3 s, typically 5–12 s). The lower third enters about 1 s after the cut.
4. **Pictures:** cut to the full-screen picture, 4–8 s per image, while the voice continues. Show a **map** (3–5 s) at the top of foreign stories to establish place. Use at most one fact card per story, held for its word count ÷ 3 + 2 s. Never cut away during the first sentence of a link.
5. **Back to presenter:** a single for the closing line or the next link. No shot shorter than 3 s, and no studio shot longer than 15 s without a reason.
6. **Hand-over:** when presenter B takes the next story, cut to B's single on B's first word. For a "toss" ("Lola?") or banter, stay on the wide. In the wide shot the listener looks at the speaker. In singles, presenters look at the lens.
7. **Close:** the wide two-shot, then the end card.

Solo programmes (MONEY MINUTE, NEWS IN 60) alternate the solo single with pictures. NEWS IN 60 may cut faster (3–6 s shots) but still has no stinger between items.

## 6. Do / don't checklist

**Do**
- [ ] Use one hero screen (the wall) and nothing else that glows.
- [ ] Make faces the brightest, warmest area. The background reads at least two palette steps darker.
- [ ] Give dark suits a 1 px rim light.
- [ ] Keep empty, calm zones around the heads and in the bottom 50 px.
- [ ] Keep the set symmetric about x=192, static and cool. Colour goes on the scenery; light on faces is white.
- [ ] Use brand red only in flat blocks, plus one programme accent.
- [ ] Keep text inside safe margins, with three type levels at most.
- [ ] Cut between shots and stories, and keep stingers for open, breaks and breaking news.
- [ ] Pass the squint test: only presenters, wall and graphics move.

**Don't**
- [ ] Use windows, side monitors, wall clocks, moving beams, airliners, traffic, bokeh or blinking LEDs.
- [ ] Put fake data UI, scanlines or decorative HUD rings on set.
- [ ] Let the wall be brighter than the faces, or put text behind a head.
- [ ] Use gradients or glows in brand red, or dither faces and text.
- [ ] Rotate clocks, blink the LIVE tag, or flash continuously.
- [ ] Show a name super on every story, or let a headline exceed about 45 characters.
- [ ] Animate the camera on the set.
