# GLOBIT 24 — Art direction for the studio and graphics

What real newscasts do, and the rules for our 384x216 pixel-art studio. Written on 2026-10-02 from set photos, design-agency case studies and on-air frames. NewscastStudio (NCS) pages block automated fetching, so their claims come from search summaries. Everything else was read or viewed directly.

## 1. The essence

**What credible news sets share:**

- **There is one hero per shot.** BBC Studio B has a portrait LED tower, CNN London one LED backing, ABC one curved map wall and NBC one curved headline segment. A set may be large, but each shot frames one focal element next to the presenter.
- **The face is the brightest, warmest thing in the frame.** In every frame we looked at, the background is cooler, darker and softer. Studios put colour on the scenery and white light on people.
- **There is one brand colour plus neutrals,** used as a few flat blocks, never as a glow everywhere.
- **Negative space is designed in.** Around the head stays calm, and logos sit in the empty third beside the presenter.
- **Materials are real.** BBC wanted a set that was "real, that you could touch" ([TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/live-production/we-wanted-a-studio-that-was-physical-not-virtual-behind-the-design-and-technology-of-bbc-news-studio-b)).
- **Graphics are calm and consistent.** Breaking news is a colour change, not fireworks.

**What looks cheap or "AI-made":** screens everywhere. Devlin Design Group says many sets look alike because they are "either completely virtual or just covered in LED monitors" ([SVG, 2026](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/05/19/inside-studio-design-trends-and-how-to-make-the-right-moves-with-devlin-design-group/)). Other failures:

- everything glowing equally, so there is no light direction;
- a background as bright or saturated as the faces;
- no empty areas;
- decorative sci-fi interface that carries no information;
- many unrelated motions at once.

## 3. Rules for our studio

**Hero and screens (strict)**
- **The video wall (x 140–244, y 18–80) is the only screen.** No side monitors, city windows or wall clocks. World time belongs in the graphics.
- **Each shot shows at most one active image area:** the wall, or the full-screen picture.
- **Non-screen accents are limited to three, all static:** the desk-front logo plate, one accent line (desk LED or wall bezel) and one symmetric pair of practical lights.

**Wide shot (presenters at x≈118 and x≈266, heads y≈80, desk top y=118)**
- **Bands:**
  - y 0–10 is dark ceiling with at most one static grid line.
  - y 18–80 is the wall, centred on x=192.
  - y 118 is the desk top, with a 1 px silver highlight.
  - y 150–216 is the darkest, quietest zone, because the lower third (166–197) and the ticker (202–216) sit there.
- **Head zones** (x 80–140 and 244–304, y 40–110): calm ink to slate with one soft light pool from above. No edges, text or high-contrast seams within 6 px of a head.
- **Sides** (x 0–60, 324–384): fall off to black, with at most one mirrored vertical line per side.
- **Symmetry:** the set is symmetric about x=192. Only presenters and wall content break it.
- **Solo at x=192:** the head overlaps the wall's bottom edge. Keep the wall's bottom 16 px dark (no text or logo there) and put wall titles in the upper half or the third away from the head.

**Palette discipline**
- **Per frame:** neutrals (black, ink, slate, steel, fog, silver, white), skin tones, brand red `#e43b44` (shadow `#a22633`), one programme accent and the logo's yellow bit.
- **Saturated colour** covers at most about 10% of set pixels in the wide shot. Wall content and clothing are excluded.
- **Brand red** appears only as flat blocks: the bug, LIVE/BREAKING tags, the desk plate and at most one line on the set. Never as a gradient or glow.
- **Navy `#124e89`** is a wall-content colour. The set's neutrals are ink, slate and steel.

**Lighting recipe**
- **Key light** comes from upper front, slightly camera-left, and is the same for every presenter and close-up. Faces are flat-lit, with one shadow tone 1–2 px wide on the far side. Never tint faces.
- **Rim light:** 1 px of silver `#c0cbdc` on top of the hair and on the right shoulder. It is mandatory on navy and ink suits, which otherwise vanish into slate.
- **Values (CIE L\*):**

  | Area | Target |
  | --- | --- |
  | Head-zone background | 18–45 (ink to steel) |
  | Wall average | ≤ 45 for idles, plates and figures; a story picture ≤ 56 (COSMOS ≤ 50), highlights rolled off under 88 |
  | Faces | 55–73 (skin, tan) |
  | White | collars, eye glints, logo and text only |
  | Floor | ≤ 18; reflections ≤ 30 |

- **The wall is dimmed and cool,** as real LED walls are balanced to camera. Its spill on the desk is at most 10–15% alpha and never reaches faces.
- **Story pictures on the wall (owner, 3 Oct):** a picture shows the photo in its own colours, quantised to its own palette with an ordered dither (the full-screen shot's look), never forced onto the set palette or a cool grade; it covers the whole wall screen, the presenter in front of it.

**Materials via limited dithering**
- **Bayer 4x4 only,** mixing two adjacent ramp steps (black↔ink, ink↔slate, slate↔steel), and only for wall light falloff, wall glow and floor gradients.
- **Never dither** faces, text, logos, edges or anything under 8 px. No random noise.
- **Vocabulary:**
  - Matte panels are flat colour with seams.
  - Glossy floor is 1 px horizontal reflections at 25–35%.
  - Metal is a 1 px silver highlight over steel.
  - Glass is an edge highlight plus a 10–15% overlay.

**Motion budget**
- **May move:** presenters, wall content, ticker and clock, plus one ambient set effect at most (a sweep or pulse no more than once every 20 s, or practical lights "breathing" with a period of at least 6 s and a change of at most one palette step).
- **Must stay still:** architecture, rig, beams, desk, logos on set and the camera. Pixel-art camera drift jitters. Only full-screen photos pan.
- **Squint test:** in any frame, at most three zones move: presenters, wall, graphics.

**Re-dressing by programme (accent, scenery tint, wall content only)**

| Programme | Accent | Tint on scenery | Wall idle | Wall in story |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WORLD NOW | red `#e43b44` | none or navy ≤ 10% (home look) | slow globe or map, London marker | picture or locator map |
| TECH BYTES | cyan `#2ce8f5` (thin lines) | blue `#0099db` ≤ 10% | static two-tone chip/grid | picture |
| COSMOS DESK | magenta `#b55088` | purple ≤ 12%, set one step darker | near-still starfield + planet | picture or diagram |
| MONEY MINUTE | green `#63c74d` | warm yellow ≤ 8% | index chart, changes per story only | chart or picture |
| NEWS IN 60 | yellow `#feae34` (black text on it) | orange ≤ 8% | 60 s ring ticking once per second | picture |

Precedents: BBC's per-bulletin numeral, and Devlin's backlit panels that change colour "without complete redesigns". The tech accent moves from magenta to cyan so it no longer clashes with COSMOS purple.

## 4. Graphics package

- **Margins:** EBU action-safe (13 px sides, 8 px top/bottom) is the minimum for the bug, clock and text. Straps sit inside graphics-safe (19 / 11 px) ([EBU R95](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r095.pdf)).
- **Bug:** top left at (13, 8), 13 px high, at most 48 px wide. It is static, and the glint plays only at the open and on return from breaks. The LIVE tag sits beside it with a static red square. The programme name shows beside the bug for about 8 s after the open and after breaks, then hides.
- **Clock:** one clock, top right, studio (London) time as HH:MM with a static colon. No rotating world cities. In WORLD NOW, world times may appear as a graphic strip, France 24 style.
- **Lower third:**
  - The tag row is 11 px (category or source in the accent; BREAKING in red).
  - The headline bar is 17 px: white 5x7 caps on ink, from x=19 to x≤365. Headlines are at most about 45 characters.
  - An optional sub-line is 10 px in micro type.
  - The presenter's name super appears only the first time each presenter speaks, held 5 s.
- **Lower-third timing:** the bar wipes in left to right in 0.35 s (ease-out, integer pixels), and the text appears 0.1 s later. The headline stays for the whole story. On a story change, flip the text in 0.3 s and keep the bar. Exit in 0.25 s. No bounce.
- **Ticker:** a 14 px band at y 202. Prefer a BBC-style flipper (one item at a time, 0.3 s push) held for 1.5 s plus 0.4 s per word. If it scrolls, keep it at 30–40 px/s. Keep the yellow "LATEST" plate.
- **Breaking:**
  - The tag turns red and reads BREAKING.
  - An optional full-screen card lasts at most 3 s, with a sting.
  - The banner may pulse at most twice on entry, at least 0.36 s apart (Ofcom's 9-frame rule, which also flags saturated-red transitions; see [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/16248/gn_flash.pdf)). Then it stays steady.
- **Stingers (0.8 s):** only for the open and close, breaks and breaking news. Between shots and stories, cut.
- **Programme open:** real title animations run 5–15 s, and flagships often add a 30–60 s headline sequence before them. Ours is 4 s. Keep these four things:
  1. A 3–5 note sonic signature shared by all shows and re-orchestrated per programme (BBC pips, ABC's four notes, NBC's "The Mission").
  2. One motion idea in the programme accent.
  3. A logo and title lock-up that is still from 3.2 s to 4.0 s.
  4. A hard cut on the downbeat to the wide shot.

  Drop particles and stacked text moves.
- **Type hierarchy:** three levels at most, two per screen.
  - Display is 5x7 at 2x, for titles and full-screen cards.
  - Body is 5x7 at 1x, for straps and the ticker.
  - Micro is 3x5, for source and time only.
  - Accents go on bars, not text. The only coloured text pairs are yellow on black and black on yellow.

## 5. Camera and directing grammar

1. **Cold open:** headline montage under the presenter's voice, 2.6–4 s per headline, with cuts between them.
2. **Open:** 4 s, then a hard cut to the **wide two-shot** ("Good evening, I'm Paco / and I'm Lola"), held 4–8 s.
3. **Story link:** a **single** of the speaker for the first one or two sentences (at least 3 s, usually 5–12 s). The lower third enters about 1 s after the cut.
4. **Pictures:**
   - Full-screen pictures run 4–8 s each.
   - A **map** (3–5 s) opens foreign stories.
   - At most one fact card per story, held for its word count ÷ 3 + 2 s.
   - Never cut away during a link's first sentence.
5. **Shot length:** no shot under 3 s, and no studio shot over 15 s without reason.
6. **Hand-over:** cut to B's single on B's first word. For a "toss" ("Lola?") or banter, stay on the wide, with the listener looking at the speaker. In singles, presenters look at the lens.
7. **Close:** the wide two-shot, then the end card.

Solo shows alternate the single with pictures. NEWS IN 60 may cut at 3–6 s but still uses no stingers between items.

## 6. Do / don't checklist

**Do**
- [ ] Use one hero screen. Nothing else glows.
- [ ] Make faces the brightest, warmest area, with the background at least two palette steps darker.
- [ ] Give dark suits a 1 px silver rim light.
- [ ] Keep empty zones around heads and in the bottom 50 px.
- [ ] Keep the set symmetric, still and cool. Colour goes on the scenery, white light on faces.
- [ ] Use brand red only as flat blocks, plus one programme accent.
- [ ] Keep text inside safe margins, with three type levels at most.
- [ ] Cut between shots and stories, with stingers only for open, breaks and breaking news.
- [ ] Pass the squint test: only presenters, wall and graphics move.

**Don't**
- [ ] Use windows, side monitors, wall clocks, moving beams, airliners, traffic, bokeh or blinking LEDs.
- [ ] Put fake data UI, scanlines or decorative HUD rings on set.
- [ ] Let the wall outshine faces, or put text behind a head.
- [ ] Use red gradients or glows, or dither faces and text.
- [ ] Rotate clocks, blink the LIVE tag, or flash continuously.
- [ ] Show a name super on every story, or let headlines exceed about 45 characters.
- [ ] Move the camera on the set.

## Set dressing per programme (round 4, 4 Oct)

The owner rated round 3 at 5-5.5 ("Todavía no los veo a un buen nivel… Puedes añadir animaciones o lo que sea. Pero quiero platós realmente bien hechos"). Round 3 hung small decorations on a dark, flat wall; half the wide was black. Round 4 rebuilds the back of each set as its own world and makes the studio read as lit and alive.

The network's architecture, shared by all five sets:
- the hero screen in a black mount;
- a soffit along the top with the programme's LED cove, glowing down;
- pillars at the sides (the set flats) with a light inlay in the programme's colour;
- the desk as a lit object: a thicker top slab with a shadow under it, the LED line's light falling on the front, the screen's reflection on the top, and a light line at its foot with its reflection on the floor;
- the GLOBIT 24 red plate.

**What moves** (`live.js`): a layer drawn every frame over the cached set and under the presenters. It costs a few hundred pixel writes.
- WORLD NOW: windows switching on and off, aviation lights, a plane crossing, cars on the avenues.
- TECH BYTES: data pulses along the traces; the cursor blinks.
- COSMOS DESK: stars twinkle, a satellite crosses, a meteor now and then.
- MONEY MINUTE: the ticker runs.
- NEWS IN 60: the clock's ring of sixty LEDs fills as the minute runs; the snow and the waveform move; the tallies breathe.
- Every desk line but MONEY MINUTE's: a slow sweep of light now and then.
- The wall's own content keeps its rules: the NEWS IN 60 dial never ticks.

**Out of focus** (singles, `cam.soft`):
- the city is the same city, its lights a step dimmer and its fine edges gone (owner, 4 Oct: bokeh discs read as circles, not as a city);
- the circuit as glowing lines;
- the brightest stars as dimmer points and the Earth's limb as a soft band;
- the gallery's monitors as soft squares.

**Rules**, checked by `test/v2-set.test.js`:
- every pixel is a palette colour;
- nothing is behind a head;
- the values around the heads stay inside each bible's range;
- no 4x4 patch is brighter than the NEWS IN 60 or COSMOS faces;
- text is shown whole or not at all, and never under the graphics' top row;
- the set shows no figure the channel did not report;
- the graphics zone (y 150-216) is black and ink, except the desk's foot light (a line and its reflection, never an area);
- camera moves never shimmer: nothing is re-picked per pixel under a move. Lights are fixed world points, adjacent towers share edges, and the reflections are flat rows;
- the camera checks run with the live layer off (`setLive(false)`);
- flat light shapes and flat light lines, not Bayer fringes (a dithered fringe on black reads as a dotted line).

`setDressing(false)` shows the bare architecture, which the older census tests use.
