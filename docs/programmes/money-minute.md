# MONEY MINUTE: style bible

A short round-up of the markets and your money, presented by Penny Sterling alone. The server builds each episode live from business feeds: 3 stories, no chats, and the `number` feature. This file adds to `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md` and does not repeat it.

**Sources (2026-10-02):** the egress proxy blocked every direct fetch (NewscastStudio, Variety, Wikipedia, Marketplace, YouTube thumbnails), so no frames were viewed; claims come from search summaries of the linked pages.

## 1. Identity

The channel's "after the close" desk, for adults with a pension, a mortgage or a shopping bill, not traders. In under three minutes they should feel briefed, as after a good FT chart or a Marketplace numbers segment. Figure first, then what it means for your money. Precise, calm, warm under cool light, never hyped; honest data, sparse motion, one dry line per episode at most.

## 2. Real references

**Bloomberg TV (Surveillance, The Close)**
- **Graphics:** the 2021 package added "chart and data visuals in a nod to the Bloomberg Terminal" to the titles ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2021/05/07/bloomberg-redesign/)).
- **Set:** the 2024 Surveillance studio uses its LED walls for "market deep dive visualizations" ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2024/01/17/bloomberg-updates-television-schedule-adds-new-studio-for-surveillance/)).
- **Pacing:** The Close covers "the final minutes and seconds before and after the closing bell" ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2024/06/10/bloomberg-the-close-preview/)).
- **Colour:** the Terminal is amber on black. It was chosen "to be distinctive since everyone else used green" ([Ted Merz](https://ted-merz.com/2021/06/26/amber-on-black/)).
- **Music:** more than 50 themes hang on one **two-note sonic logo** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/reelworld--reel2media-create-new-sonic-brand-for-bloomberg-television-and-radio-301376292.html)).
- **Warning:** the old "J screen" stacked figures around the picture. The redesign brief was to clean it up "without compromising the live action" ([Mark Porter](https://markporter.com/work/bloomberg-television)).
- **What we take:** a tiny musical motif, data as the scenery, and restraint.

**CNBC (Squawk Box, Closing Bell)**
- **Graphics:** the 2023 Troika package aims for a "clean, minimal overall aesthetic… so they can engage comfortably 24/7".
  - It sits on a square grid.
  - It drops bevels, flares, gradient text and glassy 3D.
  - Most of the time a single strap element is on screen, with the main topic boldest.
- **Ticker:** its lower tier became a colour-coded **"bottom line"** of market indices ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2023/12/12/cnbc-new-graphics-logo-ticker/), [Variety](https://variety.com/2023/tv/news/cnbc-ticker-overhaul-screen-graphics-tv-news-1235832629/)).
  - Green means up and red means down, and the arrows were redrawn to point north-east and south-east ([summary](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/29260)).
- **Light:** the Squawk Box studio's LED light changes "based on season or time of day" ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2017/11/27/squawk-box-gains-flexible-new-home-nasdaq/)).
- **Ritual:** Closing Bell is built around the 4 pm bell ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closing_Bell)).
- **What we take:** flat squares, one strap, direction shown by shape as well as colour, and a recurring ritual.

**BBC World Business Report / Business Today**
- **Format:** 25-minute editions from Studio C for a global audience, 1995–2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Business_Report)). Business Today replaced it, presented from London, New York and Singapore ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Today_(BBC_News_programme))).
- **Data:** the BBC dropped its web market-data feeds in 2024 but kept market headlines on air ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/bbc-market-data-news/)).
- **What we take:** one spoken market line beats a wall of quotes.
- **What we avoid:** relief presenter Aaron Heslehurst is billed as "very energized" ([London Speaker Bureau](https://londonspeakerbureau.com/speaker-profile/aaron-heslehurst/)). Penny sits at the opposite, calm end.

**Financial Times**
- **Charts:** John Burn-Murdoch's best chart had "a strong narrative title and multiple explanatory annotations". His rule is to "minimise distraction, maximise contrast": highlight one or two series and grey the rest ([GIJN](https://gijn.org/stories/data-visualization-storytelling-tips-john-burn-murdoch/)).
- **Colour:** salmon paper since 1893 ([Quartz](https://qz.com/462285/why-the-financial-times-is-pink)).
- **What we take:** warm "paper" cards whose titles state the takeaway.

**Marketplace (APM radio)**
- **The ritual:** in "Let's do the numbers", the host reads the indices over music ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2009/06/02/lets-do-numbers)). The tune follows the market: "We're in the Money" when up, "Stormy Weather" when down, "It Don't Mean a Thing" when mixed ([Houston Chronicle](https://www.houstonchronicle.com/local/gray-matters/article/Let-s-do-the-numbers-5751441.php)).
- **What we take:** a music choice driven by data, which our runtime can compute.

**Wall Street Week (Louis Rukeyser)**
- **Delivery:** a self-written "wry, pun-jammed" opener in jargon-free language ([AHBJ](https://ahbj.sabew.org/lives/0330201louis-rukeyser-1933-2006-wall-treet-week/)). After the 1987 crash he said "it's just your money, not your life" ([Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1995/nov/26/rukeyser-paved-way-for-financial-news-on-tv/)).
- **What we take:** the wit lives in the framing lines, never inside facts.

**Reuters:** all I could confirm is that *Morning Bid* is a podcast and YouTube series from the markets desk ([Apple](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/reuters-morning-bid/id1854858291)). I could not check its visuals, so I have not used it.

**Pace:** in Rodero's 2015 study, 150 words per minute felt slow and 170–190 felt normal. The ideal was **170 wpm for dense news** ([Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/media/radio-news-pace-words/)). Writing guides advise "ideally one number per sentence" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@ErinWrites_/how-to-write-a-radio-script-10-tips-for-clear-confident-broadcast-writing-bc7affdfc9d2)).

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (writer prompt and voice prosody)

- **Figures:**
  - The figure that matters comes in sentence 1 or 2.
  - One figure per sentence, two per story at most.
  - Copy figures exactly from the summary, for grounding. The voice layer expands "$", "%" and "bn".
  - Never read ticker symbols aloud.
- **Sentences:** 8–18 words each, 22 at most. One idea per sentence, no parentheses.
- **Verbs:** rose, fell, held, edged up, slipped. Use "soared", "plunged" or "crashed" only if the source does.
- **Money line:** the last sentence says what the story means for people's money only when the summary does. Otherwise, stop.
- **Jargon:** give one gloss of 3–6 words ("bond yields, the cost of government borrowing"). The gloss defines the term and never adds a fact.
- **Humour:**
  - At most one dry line per episode, in the teaser or the sign-off.
  - Use understatement or a mock-formal tone ("Spend it wisely. Or at least slowly.").
  - Never near layoffs, collapses, poverty or deaths.
  - No puns in straps and no exclamation marks.
- **Hand-overs (no banter):**
  - In: "This is Money Minute. I'm Penny Sterling."
  - Out: "That's your Money Minute. Next on GLOBIT 24: <next title>."
- **Places and currencies:** say the place first ("In Tokyo, the Nikkei…"). Say currencies as words ("the pound").
- **Prosody:**
  - 165–175 wpm. Measure the current `rate: 1.05` against this target.
  - Pause 150–250 ms before the key figure, end it on a falling final syllable, and never use upspeak.
  - Leave 350–450 ms between sentences and 700–900 ms between stories.

### Structure (produced live)

1. **Open** (template): 4 s.
2. **Intro:** the greeting plus "the number that matters today", 8–12 s. The number is the top story's grounded figure, or its headline if there is none.
3. **Lead story:** 20–30 s. **Stories 2–3:** 18–25 s each.
4. **Number of the day:** the `feature:'number'` story, 10–14 s. The server moves it to the end, and skips it if no story qualifies.
5. **Outro and end card:** 6–9 s, naming the next programme in the rotation.

A 360-character story is about 60 words, or about 21 s at 170 wpm. The whole show runs about 1:45–2:40.

**Episode "tape":** computed from the sentences that hold grounded figures. The tape drives the bed mode and the glyph colour.

| Tape | When |
|---|---|
| `up` or `down` | the direction verbs agree |
| `mixed` | the direction verbs conflict |
| `neutral` | no signal, any `isGrave` story, or any doubt |

### Set and light: "after the close"

- **Key and rim:** the channel key and silver rim, untinted.
- **Scenery:** one step darker than WORLD NOW (black and ink, slate panels).
- **Practicals:** warm `P.cream` at low alpha.
- **Desk line:** one steady `P.darkGreen` line. In `set.js` → `money`, drop the `P.yellow` LED peak and `glow: P.green`.
- **Idle wall:**
  - An ink field with a 1 px slate grid every 12 px.
  - One seeded abstract line in `P.steel`. It has no axes, labels, numbers or green/red, because colour means data.
  - No LIVE pill.
- **Wall in story:** the grounded figure (white, Display 2x, micro label) in the third away from Penny's head, or the picture. Wall content changes only under a cut.

### Camera and directing (seeded, from segment data)

**Shots**

| Shot | What it shows |
|---|---|
| WIDE | Solo desk |
| MCU-R | Penny in the right third, the wall figure in the empty left third |
| CARD | Full-frame figure card |
| PIC | Full-frame picture, when `hasImage` |
| MAP | A market city with no image; at most once per episode |

**Moves**
- **Open:** hard cut on the open's downbeat to WIDE, held 3–5 s.
- **The only on-set move:** a WIDE→MCU-R push on the intro's second sentence (2.5–3 s, ease in-out, integer zoom steps).
- **Everything else:** cuts, placed in the pauses between sentences (use the word timings).

**Sequence**
- **Story:**
  1. MCU-R for sentence 1, at least 3 s.
  2. CARD for the figure sentence when there are numbers, held for words ÷ 2.8 + 1.5 s (4–8 s). Otherwise PIC for 4–7 s.
  3. MCU-R for the money line.
- **Number of the day:** starts on CARD and returns to MCU-R after 8 s.
- **Outro:** MCU-R, then cut to WIDE on "I'm Penny Sterling", then the end card.
- **Shot length:** 3–12 s.

**Gestures**
- **Allowed:** `steeple`, `glasses`, `papers` (between stories), `nod`, and `lean_in` (once per episode, on a money line).
- **`point_screen`:** only while the wall shows the current figure.
- **Never:** `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up`, `facepalm`, `laugh`, `shrug`, `wave`. Use a nod at the sign-off instead.
- **Limits:** at most one per sentence and two per story, and none within 0.8 s after a cut.
- **On the figure word:** the arms stay still and a brow lift does the work.
- **Eyeline:** Penny looks at the lens.

### Graphics

- **Accent:** a `P.green` tag plate with black text, and `P.darkGreen` bars.
- **Kickers:** MARKETS, YOUR MONEY, NUMBER OF THE DAY.
- **Strap:**
  - Channel timings: in 0.35 s, flip 0.3 s, out 0.25 s.
  - The source goes on the micro line.
  - "PENNY STERLING · MARKETS" appears on her first story only, for 5 s.
- **Direction glyphs:** 5 px arrows: NE ▲ for up, SE ▼ for down, = for flat. The figures stay white.
  - `P.green` and `P.red` only for market prices: shares, indices, currencies, commodities.
  - `P.fog` for economic statistics, where "up" is not "good".
- **"Paper" CARD:** a `P.cream` panel (x 19–365, y 30–134) on ink, with all text in `P.ink`.
  - Title: a narrative title (the grounded headline) in Body.
  - Figure: Display 2x. The numeral for the number of the day alone may use 3x (21 px).
  - Label: Body, with "SOURCE: <feed>" in micro.
  - Content comes only from grounded `numbers[]`:
    - one value: the figure alone;
    - two non-negative values with the same unit: proportional bars, `P.ink` for before and `P.darkGreen` or `P.darkRed` for after;
    - anything else: the two figures with an arrow between them.
  - Never draw a trend line, because we have no series data.
- **Card motion:**
  - The panel wipes in from the left (0.3 s, ease-out).
  - The figure rises 0.25 s later and settles 0.4 s before it is spoken.
  - Bars grow over 0.5 s, starting on the second value's word.
  - Then everything holds and leaves on a cut. No rolling digits or count-ups.
- **Ticker "bottom line":**
  - It flips through this episode's grounded figures ("OIL  $82 ▲"), holding each for 1.5 s + 0.4 s per word.
  - With fewer than two figures, it shows headlines instead.

### Music and sound

- **Rework `audio/themes.js` `'money-minute'`.** It currently reads as a game show: "Strut… swing, 'cha-ching' sparkle", 128 bpm, swing 0.18, a brass lead and bell arpeggios.
- **Target theme:**
  - 100–112 bpm in straight time (swing ≤ 0.05).
  - The shared signature, voiced minor or Dorian.
  - Lead: 25% pulse with a soft attack and late vibrato.
  - Comping: 12.5% pulse off-beat plucks, standing in for an electric piano.
  - Bass: triangle, root and fifth in half notes.
  - Pad: filtered square.
  - Texture: very quiet noise-hat 16ths.
- **Banned:** brass stabs, bells above C6, coin or register effects.
- **Beds:**
  - Under the intro teaser.
  - **Silence under stories.**
  - Under the number of the day, a **tape bed**: the same groove voiced Ionian (`up`), Aeolian (`down`), Dorian (`mixed`) or plain (`neutral`). Original writing only, never song quotes.
  - Under the outro, the theme tail with a button on the end card's downbeat.
  - Silence for grave stories and breaking news.
- **Stings:** the signature (≤ 1.2 s) only into the number of the day and at the end. No card whooshes.
- **Mix:**
  - Beds sit 18–22 dB under the voice (duck attack 120 ms, release 500 ms).
  - In gaps of 0.6 s or more, beds rise to −10 dB relative to the voice.
  - Full level only in the open and on the end card.

### Transitions

- **Into the show:** stinger (0.8 s), open (4 s), then a hard cut on the downbeat.
- **Between stories:** cut to MCU-R and flip the strap. A `papers` gesture is optional. No stinger.
- **Into the number of the day:** the sting plus the card wipe. This is the only graphic transition inside the show.
- **Out:** WIDE, then the end card ("NEXT: <programme>"), then the stinger.

### Do / Don't

**Do**
- Lead with the exact figure.
- Show direction by both shape and colour.
- Keep to one accent, paper cards and a dark set.
- Cut on sentence boundaries.
- Keep silence under stories.
- Allow one dry line at most.

**Don't (childish or cheap)**
- Coins or dollar signs with faces, piggy banks, money rain, rockets, bull or bear mascots, sparkles.
- Cha-ching sounds, strutting brass, bells.
- Rolling digits, green glows, blinking LIVE pills, fake candlesticks or trend lines.
- Hype verbs, exclamation marks, puns in straps, jokes near layoffs.
- `wow`, thumbs-up or fist-pump gestures.
- A Bloomberg "J screen" wall of numbers.

## 4. Acceptance checklist

1. Every on-screen figure is in its story's source summary; no trend lines or invented series.
2. Each story speaks its first grounded figure within two sentences.
3. 160–180 wpm; sentences average ≤ 18 words; no sentence holds two figures.
4. ≤ 1 humorous line per episode, none in a grave story; no exclamation marks.
5. Shots 3–12 s; the intro push is the only on-set move; every cut falls in an inter-sentence pause.
6. A card's figure is legible ≥ 0.3 s before it is spoken; nothing appears in < 0.2 s except at a cut.
7. Green/red only on market-price glyphs; economic statistics use fog glyphs.
8. Idle wall: no numbers, labels, green/red or LIVE pill; wall content never changes on screen.
9. Saturated colour ≤ 10% of any studio frame; no green/red glow or gradient.
10. Only allowed gestures, ≤ 2 per story, none within 0.8 s of a cut.
11. Silence under stories; tape bed matches the derived tape (neutral if any story is grave); no coin, bell or brass; beds 18–22 dB under voice.
12. Open lock-up still from 3.2 s; cut to WIDE on the downbeat.
13. A first-time adult viewer calls it "business news", never a kids' or game show (blocker).
14. A fixture episode with no figures airs cleanly: no cards, wall shows the kicker, ticker shows headlines.
