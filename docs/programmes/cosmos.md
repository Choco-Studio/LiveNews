Science, space and our planet, with Dr Nova Reyes and UNIT-8. This builds on `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md` and does not repeat it. Revision 2, 2026-10-02.

## 1. Identity

A late-evening observatory: dark, quiet, exact. It is for adults who read the science pages and want the real story.

- Wonder comes from scale, specifics and silence, never from adjectives.
- Nova is the calm expert, and she leads. UNIT-8 is a literal-minded instrument. Its precision is the only joke needed.
- The model is a public-service science desk with documentary restraint. It is never a cartoon explainer.

**What only COSMOS does** (our rules, detailed in section 3):
1. **The Reading.** NUMBER OF THE DAY is shown like an instrument reading: one white figure above a powers-of-ten ruler with a magenta measurement line, then 1.5 s of true silence.
2. **Music plays under pictures, never under faces.** The bed fades in on picture shots and out when we cut back to people.
3. **The slowest voice on the channel.** Nova reads at 145 wpm, against 165–185 elsewhere. The pauses carry the slowness, not a drawl.
4. **The darkest set.** The wall shows a still planet, and only its terminator drifts across it, over 45 s.
5. **Lights down.** A 0.5 s dip to black comes before the end card. It is the only dip in the programme.

### Live and news-format broadcasts

**NASA mission broadcasts (live)**

  - Rob Navias is described as "authoritative, prepared… calm and stylish" ([JSC Roundup](https://roundupreads.jsc.nasa.gov/roundup/2244)) (unconfirmed).

**BBC Stargazing Live (live science programme)**
- A scientist (Brian Cox) and a comedian who is also an amateur astronomer (Dara Ó Briain) presented live from the Jodrell Bank control room, with telescope links abroad ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargazing_Live), [University of Manchester](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/brian-cox-to-host-live-astronomy-show-from-jodrell-bank/)).

**BBC The Sky at Night (monthly magazine)**
- It is a monthly 30-minute programme presented by Maggie Aderin-Pocock and Chris Lintott, with viewer "Question Time" specials ([memorabletv](https://www.memorabletv.com/news/sky-night-question-time/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sky_at_Night)).
- Its theme has been Sibelius's "At the Castle Gate" since 1957 ([SecondHandSongs](https://secondhandsongs.com/performance/1303749)), which is grave and unhurried.
- Patrick Moore is remembered for fluent "rapid diction" ([Royal Society memoir](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsbm.2019.0029)). His authority came from fluency, not slowness. That is why our slow pace comes from pauses (section 3).
- One thread on the British Astronomical Association forum disliked the 2013 "flashy new graphics" ([BAA forum](https://britastro.org/forums/topic/bbc-sky-at-night)). This is a single anecdote, not evidence about the audience.
- **Our reading:** fixed segments, plain experts and a serious theme.

**General newscasts** (the same evidence the sibling bibles cite, with the same caveats)
- **Pace:** in Rodero's 2015 study, listeners heard 150 wpm as slow and 170–190 wpm as normal. Recall peaked at 170 wpm for dense news ([Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/media/radio-news-pace-words/), as cited in `money-minute.md`). This is the main risk of our slow pace (section 3).
- **Shots:** ITV bulletins have a median shot of 6.3 s and a minimum of 2.7 s ([Redfern](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263129239_The_structure_of_ITV_news_bulletins)).
- **"And finally":** a crisply written item that ends upbeat. It is harder to script than hard news ([Broadcast](https://www.broadcastnow.co.uk/behind-the-scenes/the-importance-of-an-and-finally/5153349.article)).
- **Short bulletins with pictures:** BBC Three's "60 Seconds" ran five stories of about 10 s each, with pictures under each one ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Seconds)). Euronews' "No Comment" runs pictures with no voice at all ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/nocomment)).
- **Register:** NHK's normal register is detached and factual. Urgency is kept for emergencies ([Verification Handbook](https://datajournalism.com/read/handbook/verification-1/preparing-for-disaster-coverage/8-preparing-for-disaster-coverage/8-1-how-nhk-news-covered-and-learned-from-the-2011-japan-earthquake)).

**Coverage gap.** No dedicated short science or space news bulletin has been analysed, because no page could be opened. These are leads for the next pass, and no claims are drawn from them here: "This Week @NASA", Euronews' "Space" magazine, Sky News' "The Climate Show", BBC World Service's "Science in Action" and DW's "Tomorrow Today".

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (writer prompt and voice prosody)

**Pace (our rule: deliberately slow)**
- **How it is measured:** `overall_wpm` in `tools/voice/measure.py` is words ÷ reply duration × 60, with planned pauses included.
- **Targets:** Nova 140–150 wpm (target 145) and UNIT-8 125–135 wpm (target 130). These match `TARGET_WPM` (`measure.py:69`).
- **Why:** COSMOS is the channel's slow programme on purpose. It contrasts with WORLD NOW (165–185) and NEWS IN 60 (175), as the owner asked ("not every programme has to have the same style").
- **How it stays fluent:** the slowness lives in the pauses.
  - Take a 13-word sentence with a 350 ms full stop and one 180 ms comma. At 145 wpm overall, the speech itself runs at about 160 wpm. That is close to Rodero's normal range and in the spirit of Moore's fluent delivery.
  - UNIT-8's 130 wpm works out to about 140 wpm of speech: deliberate, like an instrument reading out.
- **Risk:** Rodero's listeners heard 150 wpm as slow, and this format has two presenters at a desk, not narration over footage. Mitigations:
  - The programme lasts only about 2 minutes, and stories are 3–4 sentences.
  - If critics say it drags, first shorten the full-stop pause to 280 ms. Then raise the targets to Nova 155 and UNIT-8 140 through the preset speed (`measure.py rates` suggests the speed).
  - Never let Nova's speech itself, with pauses removed, fall below 150 wpm.

**Pauses** (planned silence, through the phrase planner in `tools/voice/textnorm.py`)
- Comma 180 ms, full stop 350 ms, and 600 ms after a story (including the director's 0.3 s gap).
- Before the story's key figure (its `numbers[0].value` or `fact`), once per story: 250 ms.
- NUMBER OF THE DAY: 1.5 s after the first sentence ("Number of the day: 40,000.").
- The server passes both of these as `phrases` with `pauseAfter`, which `plan_phrases` already supports (`textnorm.py:484`).
- There are no speed changes within a segment. The earlier "slow to 115 wpm on the key sentence" is withdrawn, because the pause does that job.

**Sentences**
- Sentences average 10–16 words, with 22 at most and one idea each.
- Each story has one sentence of 3–6 words.

**Register**
- Units are said in full ("kilometres", "degrees Celsius").
- **Banned (script check):** "amazing", "incredible", "mind-blowing", "epic", "wow", "out of this world", "over the moon", "rocket science", "the sky's the limit", "light years ahead".
- **Reviewer rubric only**, because a script cannot tell the figurative use from the scientific one: "stellar" or "astronomical" used to mean "great" or "huge", and space puns.

**Analogies**
- At most one per story, from Nova only. Analogies are qualitative and number-free.
- Never compute conversions or comparisons. This is our rule, in line with Space Time's restraint.

**Who reads what (our rule)**
- **Intro:** Nova (anchor A).
- **Stories:** they alternate as the writer already requires (A, B, A). Nova reads the lead and story 3, and UNIT-8 reads story 2.
- **NUMBER OF THE DAY:** story 2 is NUMBER OF THE DAY whenever a non-grave story qualifies, and the writer places that story second. Otherwise UNIT-8 reads story 2 as a plain story.
- **Chats:** at most 3, matching `maxChats: 3` for cosmos in `config/channel.json`. That is one two-line exchange after the lead (UNIT-8, then Nova) and one UNIT-8 line after AND FINALLY.
- **Outro:** Nova.

**Humour (our rule)**
- Humour appears only in chats and AND FINALLY. UNIT-8 is never wrong about a figure.
- **After the lead** (only if it is not grave): UNIT-8 restates one stated figure exactly, with a flat acknowledgement ("Forty thousand tonnes. Noted."). Nova then bridges to the next story. Neither line adds a fact.
- **The idiom gag:** at most once per episode, and only when AND FINALLY runs.
  - Nova's last AND FINALLY sentence may contain one common English idiom that is not about space ("a drop in the ocean").
  - UNIT-8's single chat line takes it literally, in at most 12 words, with no new facts or figures ("There is no ocean in this story, Dr Reyes.").
  - Nova does not reply. The wide shot holds on her neutral face for 1.2 s (the straight face), then the outro follows.
  - An idiom is wording, not a fact, so the accuracy rules still hold.
- **Nobody laughs.** `laugh` is banned for both presenters. Nova may use `happy` (a closed-mouth smile) on the last AND FINALLY line only.
- **Grave stories** (`isGrave` in `server/facts.js`, or emotion `serious`/`sad`) get no chat before or after, no joke, no light cue and no bed.

**Address**
- UNIT-8 says "Dr Reyes". Nova says "UNIT-8", spoken "unit eight".

**Numbers and names**
- Read figures as written: "1.3 million" is "one point three million". No scientific notation. Negative temperatures are "minus".
- Missions are spoken as words ("Artemis two").
- Use the full name first ("the James Webb Space Telescope", then "Webb"), and spell out acronyms on first use.
- Earth places are "city, country", from the gazetteer.

### Structure (built live from feeds)

Durations assume the target paces (Nova 2.4 words/s, UNIT-8 2.2 words/s) and the config change in section 4: stories of 3–4 sentences, 40–70 words.

| # | Segment | Built from | Voice | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open: one line over the lead picture | `intro.cold` (new, section 4) | Nova | 6–8 s |
| 2 | Open | template | — | 4 s |
| 3 | Greeting on the wide: Nova names both and gives one teaser | rest of `intro` | Nova | 7–11 s |
| 4 | Lead story | story 1 | Nova | 17–30 s |
| 5 | Optional exchange (never after a grave lead) | 2 chats | UNIT-8, then Nova | 6–11 s |
| 6 | NUMBER OF THE DAY, or a plain story | story 2 | UNIT-8 | 18–32 s |
| 7 | Story 3; AND FINALLY when supported | story 3 | Nova | 17–30 s |

| 9 | Close on the wide, dip, end card | `outro` | Nova | 8–10 s |

The director adds 0.3 s after each segment.

**Runtime**
- With no cold open, no chats and short stories, the programme runs about 72 s. With everything at maximum it runs about 145 s. A typical episode runs about 1:55.
- **Target:** 70–150 s, from the first frame of the cold open (or the open) to the end of the end card. The median should be about 2:00.
- The old 2.5–3.5 minute target is withdrawn, because three stories capped at 450 characters cannot reach it.

**Ordering rules**
- NUMBER OF THE DAY is never the lead, never grave, and appears at most once (the writer already enforces once).
- No chat after a grave story, and no exchange before the lead.
- AND FINALLY is only ever story 3, and it is dropped rather than following a grave story.
- An unsupported feature is dropped, never invented.

### Set and light

**Set** (our rule, within ART_DIRECTION's COSMOS row)
- One step darker than the home look: black, ink and slate, with a purple scenery tint of no more than 12%. The sides fall to black.
- The magenta desk line is the only programme accent on the set.
- The head-zone background is L\* 35 or less (ART_DIRECTION allows 45).

**Light**
- The key comes from upper front, camera-left, and is neutral. Skin is never tinted.
- Rim: 1 px of `P.silver` on hair, shoulders and UNIT-8's casing.

**Video wall at idle (our rule)**
- **Stars:** a starfield baked once, with no twinkle.
- **Planet:** the open's ringed planet, still, one ramp step darker so it never competes with faces.
  - The lit bands are `P.tan` (L\* 61) and `P.tanShade`.
  - The shadow side runs `P.brown` → `P.ink` → `P.black`.
  - The ring is `P.steel`/`P.fog` with a 1 px `P.silver` lit edge.
  - No pink, magenta or red, and no storm.
- **No rotation.** Bands that follow latitude would not show it anyway.
- **What moves is the light.**
  - The light's azimuth swings ±35° around the key direction on a 90 s sine.
  - At radius 12 the terminator travels about 7 px each way. It peaks near 0.5 px/s mid-sweep and eases to rest at each end.
  - The terminator is a curved edge with one intermediate tone, and each row steps on its own. So it reads as a slow roll, never as the whole edge ticking.
- **Wall average:** L\* 40 or less.

**Video wall in a story**
- The story picture (the 104×62 `SMALL` size in `director.js:16`). Without a picture, the kicker plate.
- Diagrams are dropped, because nothing in the episode data defines them.

**UNIT-8 is an instrument, not a toy (a redesign)**

Today's renderers draw a toy (section 4 gives the line numbers): a blinking red antenna, rainbow chest lights and a level meter, emoji-shaped LED eyes, cyan eyes with a scanning pupil, and an equaliser mouth. Replace them with:

| Element | Close-up (head about 50×46, visor 38×28, as in `portraits.js`) | Wide (head 22×17, visor 12×9, as in `anchors.js`) |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes | Two 6×2 px slits in `P.silver` with a `P.white` top row. Centres at x ±10 and y 21–22 from the top of the head. No pupils. | Two 3×1 px `P.silver` slits on visor row 4 |
| Eye states | Rest is 6×2 and "processing" is 6×1. To look somewhere, the slits and head shift 1 px toward the target. No curved, ^^, X or sad shapes. | Rest only; a look shifts 1 px |
| Instead of blinking | A dim to `P.fog` for 80 ms every 7–12 s (seeded) | Same |
| Speech indicator | One horizontal line at y 31, centred. Its odd width of 1–13 px follows the smoothed voice envelope. `P.silver`, with `P.fog` end pixels once it is 5 px or wider. | Odd width of 1–5 px, on visor row 7 |
| Antenna | A static steel rod with no light | Static, with no light |
| Chest | A plain plate with a 1 px silver seam. No lights and no meter. | Plain |

- **Indicator smoothing:**
  - The envelope comes from the speech timeline (`audio.speechFrame()`, `public/js/audio.js:382`), with a 30 ms attack and a 120 ms release.
  - The indicator redraws at most 12 times per second (8 in the wide), and its width changes by at most 4 px per update.
  - After 200 ms of silence it rests at 1 px in `P.steel`.
- **Motion:** eased (ease-in-out), with no overshoot, and slightly slower than a person (a nod is a 1 px tilt over 0.4 s). No stepped "servo" quantisation: `anchors.js:1217` rounds poses to thirds, which reads as jitter.
- **Listening:** this follows the owner's 17:47 note. At the start of Nova's turn, UNIT-8 looks at her (head and slits shift 1 px) for about 1.5 s, then returns to the lens. It never stares constantly.
- **Wave 2 (canvas25d)** builds UNIT-8 from this table and keeps its proportions: each slit is about 12% of the head's width, 2 px tall in close-ups and 1 px in the wide.
- **No cyan anywhere on UNIT-8.** Cyan is TECH BYTES' accent.

### Camera and directing (runtime rules)

**Shots**

| Shot | Used for | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Wide two-shot | Greeting, chats, the last AND FINALLY line with the idiom reply, close | 4–15 s |
| Single MCU | Story links | At least 4 s. Never cut away during the first sentence. |
| Full-screen picture | Story pictures, cold open | 6–10 s |
| Locator map | Stories with a `location` | 4–6 s, cutting in within ±0.3 s of the spoken place name |
| The Reading | NUMBER OF THE DAY | At least 4 s (see the choreography below) |
| Quote card | Stories with a `quote` | Words ÷ 3 + 2 s, and at least 4 s |

**Rhythm**
- The minimum shot is 4 s everywhere (the channel's is 3 s, `MIN_SHOT` in `director.js:19`).
- Singles average 6–9 s, and studio shots last at most 15 s.
- Hold 0.6 s of picture after the voice ends.

**Story beats** (seeded per episode)
1. A single for sentence 1.
2. The map, if there is a location.
3. The picture, if there is an image.
4. Back to the single for the last sentence, if at least 4 s of it remain.

**Pictures**
- Each source is quantised once, as `director.js:173–175` already does (416×234 px, 24 colours).
- It pans on whole pixels along the horizontal axis only, with ease-in-out over the shot and a peak of 2–4 px/s. That gives 8–27 px of travel within the 32 px margin.
- No zoom, because re-sampling a pixel image every frame shimmers.
- Today's pan (`studio.js:180`) is diagonal, with an ease-out over 16 s. COSMOS uses this one-axis version.

**Cut or move:** cut between people and between shots. The set camera never moves. Only pictures move.

**Gestures**
- At most one arm gesture per sentence, and at most one per 6 s per presenter. Each starts 0.3 s before the stressed word.
- Nova: `steeple`, `raise_hand`, `point_screen` (only when the wall has content), `glasses`, `chin`, `nod`.
- UNIT-8: `nod`, `look_partner`, `lean_in`, `count`.
- Banned in COSMOS: `wave`, `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up`, `facepalm` and `laugh`. The greeting uses `[B:nod]` on UNIT-8's name. Today's fallback for intro and outro is `wave` (`director.js:209`).
- `B:nod` only in the wide.

**NUMBER OF THE DAY choreography**

| When | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| UNIT-8's first word ("Number of the day:") | Cut to the Reading: the ruler is drawn and the figure slot is empty. The strap tag flips to NUMBER OF THE DAY in 0.3 s. | Voice only. The previous bed has finished fading out 0.5 s earlier. |
| Start of the figure word (word timings) | The figure wipes on in 0.25 s. The magenta line grows to the figure's magnitude in 0.6 s, eased out. | Voice says the figure |
| End of sentence 1 | The card holds. Nothing moves once the line settles. | 1.5 s of silence: a planned voice pause, no bed, no sting |
| First word of sentence 2, no earlier than 4 s after the cut | Cut to UNIT-8's single | Voice only |
| Later sentences | The picture (6–10 s) if the story has one, then back to the single for the last sentence if at least 4 s remain | A bed under the picture only |

No single comes before the card, so there is no 1–2 s shot. The hand-over needs no line, because the strap flip and the cut do the job. Nova may toss ("UNIT-8, the number.") only when the segment before is her own chat line.

**The Reading (COSMOS's own card; our rule)**

The card sits on `P.black`. Coordinates are cap lines at 1x.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Figure | `numbers[0].value` exactly as written, display 5×7 at 2× (14 px tall), `P.white`, at x 64, y 56 |
| Label | `numbers[0].label`, body 5×7 at 1×, `P.silver`, at x 64, y 80 |
| Ruler | A 1 px `P.steel` baseline at y 104, from x 64 to x 304, with one tick per power of ten from 10⁰ to 10¹², every 20 px. Minor ticks are 1 px at y 103. Major ticks are 3 px of `P.silver` at y 101–103, for one, thousand, million, billion and trillion. |
| Ruler labels | Micro 3×5, `P.fog`, cap line at y 108, centred under the major ticks: 1 · 1K · 1M · 1BN · 1TN |
| Measurement | A 1 px `P.magenta` line over the baseline, from x 64 to x = 64 + round(20 × log10(value)). Above its end sits a `P.magenta` caret (3×1 over 1×1) at y 99–100. |
| Source | Micro, `P.steel`, at x 64, y 128 |

- The value is `numbersIn(value)[0].scaled` from `server/facts.js:37`, which already handles "1.2 million", "$2.5bn" and "40,000".
- The ruler is drawn only for one positive value between 1 and 10¹² that is not a percentage, a year or a range. Otherwise the card shows the figure, label and source only.
- The ruler adds no claim. It only places the stated figure among powers of ten.
- There is no separate unit line, because `numbers[]` holds only `value` and `label`. The label carries the unit in the summary's words.
- **Layout:** everything stays inside y 26–136 (clear of the bug and the strap) and x 19–365. The left column (x 64) and the cap lines (56, 80, 104, 128) sit on the 8 px grid. The ruler's 20 px tick pitch is the one exception.
- **Never count up and never type on.** The figure is absent, then whole.
- It differs from TECH BYTES' card, which is a right-aligned ledger with a cyan rule and no ruler, and from MONEY MINUTE's.

**Quote card (our rule)**
- The quote is in body type, `P.white` on `P.ink`, with the attribution in micro `P.fog`.
- A 1 px `P.magenta` underline extends under each word as it is spoken, when the story text contains the quote word for word (matched through the word timings). Otherwise the underline draws in 0.4 s on entry.

**Motion:** cards enter in 0.4 s, eased out, on whole pixels, and leave in 0.3 s, with no overshoot.

### Music and sound

**Where music plays (our rule)**

| Moment | Music |
|---|---|
| Cold open | Bed. Bells are allowed only in the 1.0 s before Nova's first word. |
| Open | The open's own tune (`audio/themes.js`, cosmos). It is not a bed. |
| Greeting, chats and close (wide shots) | Silence |
| Singles | Silence |
| Picture and map shots in non-grave stories | Bed, fading in over 1.0 s from the cut and out over 1.5 s from the cut away |
| Picture shots in AND FINALLY | The light colour of the bed, 4 dB quieter: at least 22 LU under the voice |
| The Reading | Silence, including the 1.5 s pause |
| Grave segments, and the segment after one | Silence |
| End card | The network outro cue (`audio.sfx('outro')`, as the director plays it today) |

- The bed runs continuously through a segment, and only its bus gain fades, so the musical phrases carry on underneath.
- No bed on picture shots shorter than 6 s, and at most one fade-in per 10 s, so the music never pumps.

**Arrangement** (this applies to whichever bed engine is chosen; see section 4)
- E lydian. The colour note is ♯4 (`COLOURS.cosmos = 6` in `themes.js`).
- 68–84 BPM. Both bed proposals already fall inside this range.
- **Under any voice:** pad and sub only, low-passed at 1.2 kHz or lower. No bells, arpeggios, drums or sparkles.
  - This keeps the 1–4 kHz consonant band free for the voice.
  - The rule stands on intelligibility alone: loud beds are a leading cause of complaints that speech is hard to follow ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.14240)). The Planet Earth note above agrees but is unconfirmed.
- **With no voice:** bells (at most 4 notes per bar) with a long echo.

**Level**
- The bed sits at least 18 LU under the voice, and at least 22 LU in AND FINALLY. This is in line with the 18–20 dB guide cited in `world-now.md` ([guide](https://pureaudioinsight.com/blogs/content-production/background-music-volume-how-loud-should-it-be)).
- **Method:**
  1. Render the voice and bed stems separately (for example with `tools/render-audio.mjs` on a lab page).
  2. Take BS.1770 short-term loudness in 3 s windows, with `public/js/audio/loudness.js` or ffmpeg's `ebur128`, over each picture shot.
  3. Pass if the median of voice minus bed is at least 18 LU and no window falls under 15 LU.

**Stings**
- None inside the programme.
- The earlier 1.2 s NUMBER OF THE DAY sting is withdrawn. It contradicted ART_DIRECTION §4 and this file's own "no stingers between stories".
- No whooshes, lasers, theremin, beeps or "boing".

**UNIT-8's voice**
- `tools/voice/dsp.py` ROBOT (`dsp.py:423–430`) is the reference:
  - Below 2.4 kHz the voice is mostly a 100 Hz monotone vocoder carrying its formants.
  - Above 2.4 kHz it is mostly dry, for intelligibility.
  - It adds a 2.7 ms comb at g 0.22 and 6% bit-crush.
- It sounds robotic through monotone, not pitch shift. The earlier "10–15 ms comb at 15% wet" is withdrawn.
- `config/channel.json` still sets unit8 `pitch: 0.6`. That value feeds only the Web Speech fallback (`public/js/audio.js:656`). Set it to 1.0.

### Transitions

- **Into the programme:**
  1. The break's ident hard-cuts to the cold open, or to the open when there is no cold open.
  2. The cold open hard-cuts to the open 0.6 s after Nova's last word. Its bed has faded out over its last 0.5 s.
  3. The open hard-cuts on its downbeat to the wide shot.
- **Between stories:** a hard cut. The strap flips its text in 0.3 s, and the wall content changes on the cut.
- **Into NUMBER OF THE DAY:** the strap tag flips on the cut to the Reading. No sting.
- **Into AND FINALLY:** a 0.8 s pause, then a cut to Nova's single.
- **Breaks:** there are none inside COSMOS, because breaks are separate items in the schedule (`director.js:102`). The programme ends on its end card, and then `channel-and-breaks.md` takes over.
- **End:** the wide shot holds for at least 4 s, then a 0.5 s dip to black (0.2 s down, 0.1 s of black, 0.2 s up) leads into the end card. For COSMOS this replaces today's stinger into the end card (the outro case in `director.js`).

### Do / Don't

**Do**
- Make one white figure the hero, and leave silence after it.
- Put a deadpan instrument beside a straight-faced expert.
- Put music under pictures, and keep voices dry on faces.

**Don't (childish or cheap)**
- Planets with wavy pink or magenta bands, a storm or a visible spin (today's `drawPlanet` in `set.js`).
- Saturated nebula gradients, or twinkling or shooting stars (today's blinking stars at `set.js:899–902`).
- A robot with pupils, emoji eyes, an equaliser mouth, a blinking antenna or rainbow chest lights (today's `portraits.js` and `anchors.js`).
- A bouncing robot, "beep boop", or bleeps in the bed.
- Exclamation marks, "wow", puns, rockets with faces.
- Count-ups, type-ons, a spinning globe behind a speaking head, or music under faces.
- Kurzgesagt's bright palette, a second accent colour, or cyan on UNIT-8.

### Format round (owner 4–5 Oct): longer, never presenter after presenter

The owner's brief, as for TECH BYTES (`tech-bytes.md` §3.10): longer programmes that are more than one presenter after another. COSMOS DESK now carries up to 11 stories, and UNIT-8 does more than restate figures. Every line is still the source's, except UNIT-8's definitions, which are a fixed list of ours.

**UNIT-8 ASKS.** After a main story Dr Reyes read (never the lead, which keeps his restatement), UNIT-8 asks the literal question it leaves, and she answers with one of the story's own sentences:
- **The questions:** how far, how long ago, how big, how they know, what comes next, what it means.
- **Each type once a programme**, in several phrasings: "Dr Reyes, why does it matter?" / "Researchers say its unusual sulfur-based chemistry could point toward cheaper and more practical ways to turn sunlight into clean fuel."
- **A method is said, not just named.** "The study team retained control over the analysis" once answered "how was this measured?". Now the answer needs a measurement, an observation, a sample, a scan or a randomised trial.
- **An answer stands on its own after the story.** It never opens on "But", "So" or "This", never names someone the story did not introduce, and is never kept back before a grave story.

**UNIT-8 DEFINES** (`server/glossary.js`, set "science", programme `"terms": { "explainer": "unit8", "set": "science" }`). The science words a story used, read the way he reads a figure, logged and exact, with the definition card:
- **The phrasing:** "Definition filed: the Hubble tension is the clash between two measures of how fast the universe expands."
- **The terms:** about 40 (exoplanet, light year, black hole, Hubble tension, zircon, mass extinction, enzyme, microbiome, clinical trial, permafrost, El Niño...).
- **No figure in a definition:** the set never shows one the channel did not report.
- **The same placement rules as Ada's** (after a main story, never after grave news, at most two, never a definition heard lately). UNIT-8 may define right after his own story.

**IN BRIEF** (`roundup.kind: "pictures"`, kicker IN BRIEF, read by UNIT-8). The science stories that would be short anyway (one whose article was read keeps its full telling), one sentence each over its own picture. It only airs when there are three such stories: on 5 Oct every science story had its article, so none did, and the programme kept its depth.

**The boards, STILL TO COME, IN THEIR WORDS** air here too (pace `shots`). BY THE NUMBERS takes the stack panel; the Reading stays the number of the day's single figure on its ruler. WHAT WE KNOW covers hard science (health, climate; `"boards": ["known"]`). In the v2 planner a story with a board uses the generic beat order (board after the map).

**The first run on real news** (5 Oct, the fallback writer on the live science feeds):
- **A student robotics trip and a meteor crater read as serious.** "Blast off" counted as an explosion, and "the mysterious Ordovician bombardment" (370 million years ago) as a war. A launch and a good time are not grave; nor is anything said to have happened millions of years ago (deep time: fossils, dinosaurs, ice ages, the geological periods).
- **Pages that are not news.** A rover's mission blog ("Curiosity Blog, Sols 5022-5028", written in "we" with its date glued on), a staff profile ("..., featuring Richard Spolzino"), NASA's picture of the day (APOD, a page of menus around one photo), and "See the link here for more details".
- **Sentences.** "...the Gold Rush Trail, where miners[ and prospectors flocked...]" (a clause cut from its verb). "A UCLA study links faster brain aging to specific gut bacteria" followed by "A new UCLA study suggests that the pace of brain aging may be connected to bacteria in the gut" (the same thing twice). An "And finally" that opened on an unattributed quotation.
- **Openers.** A story whose summary leans ("It is hoped the prospective sale...") or opens on a detail ("The Shropshire Astronomical Society plans to install the dome...") now opens on the sentence that tells most of the headline's news. That is "Conservationists have said they are planning to buy a field to extend a 'green lung'", or "A group of amateur astronomers have been given permission to set up a mini-observatory". It is never one that follows on ("So it's only natural..."), nor a bracketed aside.
- **A fault every programme shared.** Only the lead was meant to give up its kept-back sentence when nothing else could open it, but every story that opened on its summary did too. So THE CATCH, UNIT-8 ASKS and WORLD NOW's added detail aired only where a headline opened.

**The second pass** (the voiced episode of 5 Oct, read line by line; `test/cosmos-text.test.js`):
- **Who is speaking.** "Scully said the organisation may have to accept..." came after "A National Trust manager says...": a surname nobody had heard. A person is now named whole the first time, as the article gives the name ("Justin Scully said..."). A role between commas goes from an over-long sentence only when the story has already said it ("Justin Scully[, manager of Fountains Abbey and Studley Royal,] said..."). A role the viewer has not heard stays, and the sentence with it.
- **What a story opens on.** "The students will head to Florida..." leans on its headline ("Students blast off to US for Nasa robotics competition"), which is on the strap and never said. A summary like that opens no more when a sentence says who: "A team, from Queen's University Belfast, are heading to Florida...". An acronym in brackets after its name is page furniture and goes. A question ("Could a robot designed by...?") and a definition ("Lunabotics is a university-level competition...") never open a story. One that tells the headline's news itself still opens as the outlet wrote it ("The central bank has kept interest rates unchanged...").
- **The same news twice.** The standfirst "a surprising second job" and the first paragraph "another unexpected role" said the same thing in other words. So did "Researchers have found that it also helps epithelial cells, which form..., engulf nearby dead cells", whose main clause was already told and whose only news was an aside. Both are now left out: the restatement test knows the words outlets swap (surprising/unexpected, job/role, second/another, found/discovered, linked/connected) and a word's inflections, and a sentence whose main clause the story has said is left out.
- **Rhythm.** "The findings suggest... The findings offer a new look..." Two sentences in a row never open on the same two words.
- **Cuts.** "Previous research has found that when the brain appears older than expected." The cut left a "that when" clause waiting for its main clause. That cut is refused, and so is one that ends on a compound adjective ("...one of the fast-growing, and best-paid.").
- **Lines.** Dr Reyes no longer hands over with "Our number of the day is yours." (it read as "the number belongs to you"). She says "You have our number of the day."
- **Cell biology as grave news.** "Swallow nearby dead cells" and "immune attack" made a protein discovery and a cancer drug grave, so the number of the day moved and UNIT-8 fell silent after the lead. Dead or dying cells, microbes and stars, a drug that kills cancer cells, an immune attack and the death of a star are not grave. The validator judges a story by its headline, its summary and its opening sentence, never a detail three sentences in.
- **Beats.** The broader HEALTH topic (cancer, tumours, chemotherapy, clinical trials, therapy) carried a vitamin D cancer drug, a forest experiment and a heritage climate story to TECH BYTES. Its science beat is now the tech topics only (space, energy, AI, robotics, chips, gadgets, software, connectivity, motoring, transport). Medicine and climate science are COSMOS DESK's.

**The third pass** (the real news of 9 Oct, every programme; `test/realnews-oct9.test.js`). New stories, new faults, all fixed in the shared writer:
- **Broken feed text.** "1 from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California." was what was left of a launch date after its "Nov.". A sentence that opens on a number before a preposition, or in lower case, is no sentence. A clock keeps its time zone ("3:30 p.m. EDT" was split in two), a quotation cut in two is left out, and so are a blockquote's ">", invisible joiners, a one-word gloss in brackets ("bacteriophages (phages)"), an editor's "Update ...: The article was updated" and a press release's news-conference logistics.
- **Names.** "The mission was the second for Meir and Fedyaev and the first for Hathaway and Adenot", "neither Lorenz nor Hamilton", "Hasan fatally shot...": people by surname alone are said whole when the article gives the whole name ("Nidal Hasan fatally shot..."), else the sentence is left out. "Meredith Whittaker" no longer goes out as "meredith Whittaker" after "And finally:".
- **What never opens a story.** A connective ("But Norwich Apex Data Centre has defended the scheme..."), a time that points back ("...that same year", "at the time"), "The other firms...". The sentence that tells the news opens even when it is a little long ("The Trump administration is suspending Microsoft, Adobe, and several other technology companies...", not "H-1B visas are designed for..."). An over-long opener loses its aside, not its news ("Margaret Hamilton[, who coined the term...,] died last week at the age of 90"), and a lede that ends on who said it reads whole ("..., experts have said."). A standfirst that dropped its article gets it back ("An intense flash of radio waves...").
- **What never airs in a story.** A "he" or "she" nobody introduced ("The plan was for her to work until he finished his law degree"), the reader addressed ("You might not have known..."), an aside the story has already said ("Hurricane Isaias[, the first hurricane of the 2026 Atlantic season,] is intensifying..."), and a second sentence opening the same way as any earlier one ("Scientists have uncovered... Researchers have uncovered...").
- **Headlines that are labels.** "BBC on Hurricane Isaias and its expected Gulf Coast landfall" (a video's page), "RIP Margaret Hamilton...", "'Careless use of AI is the real threat'": the intro and STILL TO COME say the story's own news instead ("Hurricane Isaias is intensifying as it heads toward the northern Gulf Coast"; "Meredith Whittaker says the tech has been misunderstood"), and the lead does not read that sentence again. A Title Case headline is said in sentence case ("NASA’s SSPICY mission to demonstrate in-space inspection technologies").
- **Figures.** "Crew-12" put "12 MISSION SAFELY" on a card: a number in a name (after a hyphen, or after a capitalised word: "Apollo 11") is no figure, and a person's age is no card. "80% OF COSMIC" keeps its noun. The number of the day may be deep time ("more than 10bn years"), and is the headline's figure when only the article gives it ("$20 billion" less, not the summary's earlier $70 billion). UNIT-8 echoes a figure that aired ("800,000 hours. Confirmed."), never "Wales." in its stead.
- **Grave or not.** Viral attacks and microbes killed are biology. A death notice ("RIP"), a shooting ("shot and wounded", "fatally", "gunman") and a stabbing are grave.
- **The desk.** A picture's caption ("...is seen moments before splashing down... in this Oct. 8, 2026, photograph") and an hourly newscast's page ("Trump says U.S. won't attack Iran before midterms. And, ICE...") are not reports. A quotation is said by "the company", not by "a move the company". A second report of the same affair airs right after the first, not three stories later.

**Length.** On 5 Oct's real news (12 science stories on the desk) the programme carried about 820 words, near 5 min 30 s; voiced, the endcard came at 6 min 38 s, inside the 6–8 minutes the LLM writer is asked for. The fallback writes no more than the reporting supports.

## 5. Acceptance checklist

1. **Script, automatic:** no word or phrase from the banned list and no "!". Sentences average 10–16 words, none is over 22, and each story has at least one sentence of 3–6 words.
2. **Script, reviewer:** a reviewer reads 3 seeded episodes. Any of these fails: wordplay on a science or space term, figurative "stellar" or "astronomical", a joke in story copy outside AND FINALLY, or more than one idiom.
3. **Pace:** measured with `overall_wpm` in `tools/voice/measure.py`, pauses included. Nova reads at 140–150 wpm and UNIT-8 at 125–135 wpm. Nova's speech rate with planned pauses removed is at least 150 wpm.
4. **Number of the day:** UNIT-8 (anchor B) reads it as story 2. Its first sentence contains `numbers[0].value` character for character and is followed by a 1.5 s planned pause.
5. **Grave stories:** wherever `isGrave` is true or the emotion is `serious`/`sad`, there is no chat next to the story, no light cue (the `parseCues` grave filter) and no bed in it or the next segment.
6. **Shots** (from the director's shot log):
   - Every shot lasts at least 4 s, and studio shots at most 15 s.
   - Pictures run 6–10 s.
   - Maps run 4–6 s and cut in within ±0.3 s of the place name.
   - The Reading lasts at least 4 s.
7. **Camera:** in studio shots, frame-to-frame differences appear only in the presenter, wall and graphics regions (checked by diffing lab captures).
8. **Faces:** in the wide, the mean L\* of each face box is at least 20 above the head-zone background mean and above the mean of the planet's lit side. The head-zone background is L\* 35 or less, and the wall average L\* 40 or less.
9. **Accent:** programme accent pixels are only `P.magenta` or `P.purple`. They appear only on the desk line, the strap tag and rule, the Reading's line and caret, the quote underline and the scenery tint. Network elements (bug, LIVE, BREAKING, LATEST) are not counted. No pink anywhere, and no magenta, purple or pink text.
10. **The Reading:** no frame shows a partial or in-between figure. The figure appears within ±2 frames of its spoken word, the line settles within 0.6 s, and the card holds still for the 1.5 s pause with the voice and bed stems silent.
11. **Music:**
    - No bed under singles, wides, the Reading or grave segments.
    - On picture cuts the bed fades in over 1.0 s and out over 1.5 s.
    - No bell, arpeggio or drum note sounds while a voice is active (checked in the engine's event log).
    - Level follows the section 3 method: at least 18 LU under the voice, and at least 22 LU in AND FINALLY.
12. **Gestures:** none of `wave`, `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up`, `facepalm` or `laugh`. At most one arm gesture per 6 s per presenter.
13. **Wall idle** (a 10 s lab capture at mid-sweep):
    - Star pixels are identical in every frame.
    - The planet's bands and position never change.
    - At most 4 planet pixels change per frame, and at least one changes every second.
    - The terminator moves 3–6 px over the 10 s.
14. **UNIT-8:** no pupils, mouth shape, blinking or coloured lights, and no cyan. The indicator updates at most 12 times per second (8 in the wide), changes width by at most 4 px per update, and never turns on and off within 120 ms.
15. **Transitions:** no stinger, sting or wipe between the open and the end card. The only dip to black comes before the end card.
16. **Runtime:** across 10 seeded offline episodes, every run lasts 70–150 s from the first frame of the cold open (or the open) to the end of the end card, with a median of 100–135 s. Every segment traces back to the writer's output.
17. **Kids' test:** shown 6 random frames, a neutral viewer never calls it a children's programme. Any cute element is a BLOCKER.
