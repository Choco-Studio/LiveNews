# COSMOS DESK: programme style

Science, space and our planet, presented by Dr Nova Reyes with UNIT-8. This builds on `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md` and does not repeat it.

**About the sources.** This was written on 2026-10-02. During this session the egress policy blocked direct fetching from Wikipedia, YouTube, nasa.gov, pbs.org, bbc.co.uk and kurzgesagt.org. Every claim about a reference therefore comes from search-engine summaries of the linked pages. No frames were inspected this time.

## 1. Identity

COSMOS DESK should feel like a late-evening observatory: dark, quiet, exact. It is for adults who read the science pages and want to be trusted with the real story.

- **Wonder comes from scale, specifics and silence,** never from adjectives.
- **Nova** is the calm expert.
- **UNIT-8** is a literal-minded instrument. Its deadpan precision is the only joke the show needs.
- **The model** is a public-service science desk (Sky at Night, NASA commentary) with Attenborough's restraint. It must never feel like a cartoon explainer.

## 2. Real references

**BBC The Sky at Night (1957–)**
- A monthly 30-minute programme, now on BBC Four. Maggie Aderin-Pocock and Chris Lintott have co-presented since 2014. Pete Lawrence's practical observing section has run since 2004, and there are occasional "Question Time" specials answering viewers ([memorabletv](https://www.memorabletv.com/news/sky-night-question-time/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sky_at_Night)).
- The theme has been Sibelius's "At the Castle Gate" since 1957: grave, orchestral and unhurried ([SecondHandSongs](https://secondhandsongs.com/performance/1303749)).
- Patrick Moore's "rapid diction" with "never a stumble" gave the show "verve and pace" ([Royal Society memoir](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsbm.2019.0029), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys2536)). Authority came from fluency, not volume.
- The 2013 relaunch's "flashy new graphics" put off some viewers ([BAA forum](https://britastro.org/forums/topic/bbc-sky-at-night)).
- **What makes it work:** regular segments, experts talking plainly, and a theme that signals seriousness.

**BBC Stargazing Live (2011–17) and The Infinite Monkey Cage**
- A scientist (Brian Cox) paired with a comedian and amateur astronomer (Dara Ó Briain), live from the Jodrell Bank control room. Links went to telescopes in Hawaii, South Africa and Norway ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargazing_Live), [Univ. of Manchester](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/brian-cox-to-host-live-astronomy-show-from-jodrell-bank/)).
- Monkey Cage bills itself as "a witty, irreverent look at the world through scientists' eyes" ([British Comedy Guide](https://www.comedy.co.uk/radio/the_infinite_monkey_cage/)).
- **What makes it work:** the double act. One person knows the science, the other voices the audience. The humour sits around the facts, never in them.

**NASA mission broadcasts (Artemis II 2026, JWST first images 2022)**
- Artemis II coverage on NASA+ ran on milestones: tanking commentary from 07:45, full coverage from 12:50, and an onsite countdown clock ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/03/30/nasas-artemis-ii-launch-mission-countdown-begins/), [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/04/01/live-artemis-ii-launch-day-updates/)).
- The far-side flyby included a planned loss of signal of about 40 minutes, framed as "normal and anticipated" ([ABC10](https://www.abc10.com/article/news/nation-world/artemis-ii-lunar-communication-loss-how-long/507-13219682-00a9-4404-9f7d-3f3b45cbfdb3)). Silence was part of the story.
- JWST's first images were "released one by one" in a televised broadcast ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-reveals-webb-telescopes-first-images-of-unseen-universe/), [SVS](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14180/)).
- Commentators:
  - Hugh Harris was known for "calm, professional commentary" and scripted his liftoff line in advance: "We have main engine start and we have liftoff…" ([WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/science-space/2023-02-15/voice-of-nasa-hugh-harris-has-died)).
  - Rob Navias was called "authoritative, prepared, distinctive, calm and stylish". He "relays facts and data with history in mind" ([JSC Roundup](https://roundupreads.jsc.nasa.gov/roundup/2244)).
- **Graphics heritage:** the 1975 Danne & Blackburn manual used Helvetica throughout, strict grids and "unity, technological precision" ([We Are The Mutants](https://wearethemutants.com/2020/01/09/unity-precision-thrust-the-nasa-graphics-standards-manual-1975/)).
- **What makes it work:** a calm voice, facts read exactly, one reveal at a time, and telemetry-like restraint.

**PBS NOVA and PBS Space Time**
- NOVA's classic open is "only 15 seconds long", yet takes the viewer "from the atomic to the cosmic scale" ([ACM SIGGRAPH](https://history.siggraph.org/artwork/wgbh-tv-design-department-nova-opening/)). One motion idea carries the whole open.
- The theme is by Walter Werzowa ([PBS credits](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/zero/credits.html)), who also wrote Intel's five-note sound logo ([Intel](https://timeline.intel.com/1995/the-intel-bong)). This is a precedent for our four-note motif.
- Space Time has been written and hosted by astrophysicist Matt O'Dowd since 2015, with episodes typically 8–10 minutes. It "trusts viewers with the real story without resorting to bad metaphors, jargon, or complex mathematics" ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/show/pbs-space-time/)).

**Vox explainers and Kurzgesagt (explanatory grammar only)**
- **Vox** mixes narration with archive and animation ([Video Consortium](https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-1-joss-fong)). Its black-hole explainer went online about an hour after the press conference, with "precise use of music" ([The Open Notebook](https://www.theopennotebook.com/2020/01/07/videogram-how-a-vox-video-explains-the-science-behind-the-first-photo-of-a-black-hole/)). Commentators describe its signature as a highlighter sweep across quoted text and kinetic type landing exactly on the narrated word ([Easy-Peasy](https://easy-peasy.ai/blog/how-to-make-vox-style-videos-with-ai)).
- **Kurzgesagt** records the narration after illustration, and the voice "provides the timing for the animation team". Each video takes about 1,200 hours, and Epic Mountain scores and sound-designs every one ([Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/what-we-do?visit=videos), [Epic Mountain](https://www.epic-mountain.com/)).
- **We borrow** the mechanics: visuals timed to words, one concept per graphic and scale comparisons. We do not borrow the bright rounded palette ([U-M Arts](https://artsatmichigan.umich.edu/ink/2019/09/27/simple-bright-beautiful-the-work-of-kurzgesagt)).

**BBC Planet Earth (narration and score)**
- Attenborough's script is written last, to the finished cut ([CinemaBlend](https://www.cinemablend.com/television/1600210/david-attenborough-still-uses-vhs-to-script-planet-earth)).
- His pace is about 110–120 words per minute ([LinkedIn analysis](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-can-we-learn-from-sir-david-attenborough-public-speaking-robin)). The famous 4.5-minute iguana chase carries just 89 words ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2026/05/04/tv/david-attenborough-voice-nature-earth-100-years)).
- Planet Earth II's composers "carved out a certain frequency" for his voice and blurred natural sound with score ([Television Academy](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/mix/earth-wind-and-choir)).
- **What makes it work:** pictures breathe, words are scarce, and the music leaves room for the voice.

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (AI writer prompt and voice prosody)

**Pace**
- Nova reads at **140–150 wpm** and slows to about **115 wpm** on the sentence carrying the key figure or the scale idea.
- UNIT-8 reads at **125–135 wpm**: even and clipped, with a narrow pitch range. It sounds robotic through monotone delivery, not through a cartoon pitch shift.

**Pauses (prosody)**
- Comma: 180 ms.
- Full stop: 350 ms.
- Before a figure: 250 ms.
- After the last sentence of a story, before the cut: 600 ms.

**Sentences**
- 10–16 words on average, 22 at most. One idea per sentence.
- One short sentence (3–6 words) per story for weight.

**Register**
- Precise verbs and units in full ("kilometres", "degrees Celsius").
- Banned words: "amazing", "incredible", "mind-blowing", "epic", "wow", "out of this world", "stellar", and space puns.

**Analogies**
- At most one per story, from Nova only. Keep them qualitative and with no numbers ("about the size of a city" is out unless the summary says so).
- Never compute conversions or comparisons. The validator drops ungrounded figures anyway.

**Humour**
- Humour lives only in chat segments and the AND FINALLY story. The joke is UNIT-8 taking an idiom literally while Nova lets it pass with a straight face.
- At most one idiom joke per episode. UNIT-8 is never wrong about a stated figure.
- On grave stories (disasters, deaths, climate harm) there is no chat, no joke and no light cue.

**Banter**
- 2 lines at most, 6–10 s, after a story and never before the lead story. It adds no new facts.
- UNIT-8 calls her "Dr Reyes". She calls it "UNIT-8", spoken as "unit eight".

**Hand-overs**
- Nova leads the show. UNIT-8 owns NUMBER OF THE DAY. Nova tosses to it by name ("UNIT-8, the number."), and UNIT-8 opens with "Number of the day:" then the figure exactly as stated.

**Numbers, names and places**
- Read figures exactly as stated: "1.3 million" is spoken as "one point three million". No scientific notation, and negative temperatures are spoken as "minus".
- Missions are spoken as words ("Artemis two").
- On first mention give the full name ("the James Webb Space Telescope"), then the short form ("Webb").
- Spell out acronyms on first use. Earth places come from the gazetteer, as "city, country".

### Structure (generated live from feeds)

| # | Segment | Source | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open: one line from the lead story over its picture or diagram | intro | 8–12 s |
| 2 | Programme open (template) | opens | 4 s |
| 3 | Greeting on the wide shot, both named, teaser of story 2 or 3 | intro | 5–8 s |
| 4 | Lead story (Nova) | story | 25–35 s |
| 5 | Optional chat reaction (UNIT-8 first) | chat | 6–10 s |
| 6 | Story 2, or NUMBER OF THE DAY if the summary supports it (UNIT-8 reads) | story + feature | 20–30 s |
| 7 | Story 3: AND FINALLY lighter, or a third straight story | story | 20–30 s |
| 8 | Close on the wide shot, then end card | outro | 6–8 s |

Ordering rules:
- The lead is the biggest story. NUMBER OF THE DAY is never the lead and is skipped when the summary is grave.
- No chat follows a grave story.
- If the lighter cannot be supported, drop it rather than inventing one.
- The total runs about 2.5–3.5 minutes.

### Set and light

- **The set is one step darker than the home look,** with neutrals of black, ink and slate, and a purple tint on the scenery at no more than 12%.
- **Light:**
  - The key light comes from upper front, camera-left, and stays neutral so skin tones are never tinted.
  - Rim light is 1 px of silver on hair, shoulders and UNIT-8's casing. Its metal is steel with a 1 px silver highlight.
- **The sides fall to black.** One magenta desk line is the only accent line.
- **Video wall, idle:**
  - A static starfield plus one planet with a soft terminator, rotating no faster than 1 px every 2 s.
  - No twinkling, and no shooting stars.
  - The planet uses two or three adjacent ramp tones in straight bands.
- **Video wall, in a story:** the story picture, or a diagram drawn in fog and silver on ink.
- **UNIT-8 is an instrument, not a toy:**
  - Eye slits are steel or white, 1×2 px, with no pupils.
  - Its speech indicator (3×1 px, white or fog) follows the voice envelope, stepping at most one palette step per frame.

### Camera and directing (runtime rules)

**Shots**
- Wide two-shot: greeting, chat and close.
- Single MCU of the speaker: story links.
- Full-screen picture: 6–10 s, Ken Burns at 2–4 px/s with eased ends.
- Diagram: 5–8 s, one move per diagram, such as a scale pull-back over 3–4 s (the NOVA idea).
- Locator map: Earth stories with a location, 3–5 s.
- Fact card or number card.

**Rhythm**
- This show is slower than the channel. The minimum shot is **4 s**, and studio singles average 6–9 s.
- Keep at least 0.6 s of picture after the voice ends before cutting back. Let the pictures breathe.

**Cut or move**
- Cut between people and between shots. The camera never moves on the set.
- Motion lives only inside full-screen pictures and diagrams.

**Gestures**
- At most one arm gesture per sentence and one every 6 s. The gesture starts 0.3 s before the stressed word (anticipation).
- Nova uses: `steeple`, `raise_hand`, `point_screen` (only when the wall shows content), `glasses`, `chin`, `nod`.
- UNIT-8 uses: `nod` (a mechanical tilt), `look_partner`, `count` (lists of figures), `lean_in`.
- Banned on COSMOS: `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up`, `facepalm`. `laugh` is allowed in chat only.
- Listener reactions (`B:nod`) only on the wide shot.

**Number of the day**
1. Cut to UNIT-8's single on "Number of the day".
2. Cut to the full-screen number card on the figure word.
3. Hold 1.5 s past the figure in silence, then cut back.

### Graphics

**Palette tokens**
- `P.magenta` is the accent, used only on bars, rules and tags. `P.purple` is its shadow and the scenery tint.
- Text is `P.white` or `P.silver` on `P.black` or `P.ink`.
- No pink or magenta text. Today `set.js` uses `text: P.pink` for the space theme, and that must change.

**Lower third**
- The channel strap (STRAP layout and STRAP_TIMING), with the kicker in caps (SPACE, CLIMATE, BIOLOGY, EARTH).
- An optional micro source line ("SOURCE: NASA").

**Number card, NASA-manual sober**
- One figure in display type (5×7 at 2×, white), a 1 px magenta rule beneath it, the label in micro type (3×5, fog) and the unit in body type (silver). Everything sits on an 8 px grid.
- Reveal with a 0.25 s left-to-right wipe. **Never count up:** the in-between values are figures the source never stated.

**Scale diagram**
- Two bars or discs, drawn only when `numbers` holds two values with the same unit. Otherwise skip it.

**Quote card**
- A 1 px magenta underline sweeps under the quoted words, synced to the word timings (the Vox highlighter translated).

**Motion timings**
- Cards come in over 0.4 s with ease-out on integer pixels and leave over 0.3 s.
- They hold for word count ÷ 3 + 2 s.
- Diagram elements build one per narrated noun, timed to the words, at least 0.5 s apart.

### Music and sound

**Bed**
- Built from the existing cosmos colour (`themes.js`, E lydian #4). The theme stays at 92 BPM; beds run slower, at 72–88 BPM.
- Instruments: soft-triangle pad, sine drone bass, and sparse bells of at most 4 notes a bar, all with long echo.
- No drums under speech.

**Frequency carve**
- Keep the pad low and the bells high so the 1–4 kHz band stays free for the voice (the Planet Earth II principle).
- The bed sits at least 18 dB below the voice.

**Where beds play, and where silence**
- Beds play under the cold open, full-screen pictures and diagrams, and AND FINALLY (lighter, a further 4 dB down).
- **Silence** under studio singles, every chat (the deadpan lands dry) and every grave story.
- For the number reveal, the bed ducks out 0.5 s before the figure and one bell note lands on the card lock.

**Stings**
- The open (existing theme).
- A NUMBER OF THE DAY sting: three motif notes on soft triangle and bell, 1.2 s.
- The channel break stingers.
- No whooshes, lasers, theremin clichés or "boing" sounds.

**UNIT-8's voice**
- Keep it near natural pitch, with a light metallic comb (10–15 ms) at about 15% wet.
- A deep pitch shift reads as comic or menacing.

### Transitions

- **Into the programme:** channel stinger, cold open, the 4 s open, then a hard cut on the downbeat to the wide shot.
- **Between stories:** a hard cut, with the wall content changing on the cut. No stingers.
- **Into NUMBER OF THE DAY:** the strap tag flips (0.3 s) and the sting plays. No full stinger.
- **To a break:** wide shot, Nova's toss, 1 s held, then the stinger.
- **From a break:** stinger, wide shot, bug glint.
- **End:** the only dip to black (0.5 s) comes before the end card.

### Do / Don't

**Do**
- Make the figure the hero: one number, white, on a quiet card.
- Let silence carry reveals.
- Use slow pans inside pictures and a still studio.
- Keep the robot deadpan with a straight-faced partner.
- Keep the planet and wall muted and two steps darker than the faces.

**Don't (childish or cheap)**
- Wavy candy stripes on planets (the current open's magenta wave band), saturated nebula gradients, or twinkling or shooting stars.
- A robot with googly eyes, a smiley mouth or bouncing. "Beep boop" lines.
- Exclamation marks, "wow", puns, emoji-like icons, or rockets with faces.
- Count-up numbers, spinning globes behind a speaking head, or bed music under chat.
- Kurzgesagt's bright palette, or more than one accent colour.

## 4. Acceptance checklist

1. The script has no banned words, no exclamation marks and no space puns. Sentences average 16 words or fewer.
2. Measured speech rate: Nova 135–155 wpm, UNIT-8 120–140 wpm.
3. NUMBER OF THE DAY is read by UNIT-8. The spoken figure exactly matches `numbers[].value`.
4. Grave stories carry no chat, no light cue and no bed.
5. No studio shot is under 4 s. Full-screen pictures run 6–10 s with a visible slow pan.
6. The camera never moves on the set. Only pictures and diagrams move.
7. In the wide shot, faces are the brightest, warmest pixels. The wall average is at or below L\* 45.
8. Accent pixels are only `P.magenta` or `P.purple`, on bars, rules and tags. There is no coloured text except the channel's yellow/black pairs.
9. The number card wipes on with no count-up and holds at least 1.5 s after the figure is spoken.
10. The bed is silent under singles and chats, ducks before the figure and sits at least 18 dB under the voice.
11. Captures show no `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up` or `facepalm` gesture on COSMOS. There is at most one arm gesture per 6 s.
12. The wall idle shows no twinkle or shooting stars. Planet motion is at most 1 px per 2 s.
13. UNIT-8 has no pupils and no mouth shape. Its indicator follows speech without flicker.
14. There are no stingers between stories. The only dip to black comes before the end card.
15. The total runtime is 2.5–3.5 min, and every segment traces back to feed data.
