# COSMOS DESK: programme style

Science, space and our planet, with Dr Nova Reyes and UNIT-8. This builds on `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md`.

**About the sources:** this session's egress policy blocked fetching Wikipedia, YouTube, nasa.gov, pbs.org, bbc.co.uk and kurzgesagt.org. Reference claims come from search summaries of the linked pages, and no frames were inspected.

## 1. Identity

A late-evening observatory: dark, quiet, exact. For adults who read the science pages and want the real story.

- **Wonder comes from scale, specifics and silence**, never adjectives.
- **Nova** is the calm expert.
- **UNIT-8** is a literal-minded instrument. Its deadpan precision is the only joke needed.
- **The model:** a public-service science desk (Sky at Night, NASA commentary) with Attenborough's restraint. Never a cartoon explainer.

## 2. Real references

**BBC The Sky at Night**
- A monthly 30-minute show. Aderin-Pocock and Lintott present, Pete Lawrence's observing section has run since 2004, and there are viewer "Question Time" specials ([memorabletv](https://www.memorabletv.com/news/sky-night-question-time/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sky_at_Night)).
- Theme: Sibelius's "At the Castle Gate" since 1957, which is grave and unhurried ([SecondHandSongs](https://secondhandsongs.com/performance/1303749)).
- Patrick Moore's authority came from fluent "rapid diction" ([Royal Society](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsbm.2019.0029)).
- The 2013 "flashy new graphics" put off some viewers ([BAA forum](https://britastro.org/forums/topic/bbc-sky-at-night)).
- **Lesson:** fixed segments, plain experts, a serious theme.

**BBC Stargazing Live and The Infinite Monkey Cage**
- A scientist (Brian Cox) plus a comedian and amateur astronomer (Dara Ó Briain), live from the Jodrell Bank control room, with telescope links abroad ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargazing_Live), [Manchester](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/brian-cox-to-host-live-astronomy-show-from-jodrell-bank/)).
- Monkey Cage: "a witty, irreverent look at the world through scientists' eyes" ([BCG](https://www.comedy.co.uk/radio/the_infinite_monkey_cage/)).
- **Lesson:** one host knows, the other voices the audience. The humour sits around the facts, never in them.

**NASA mission broadcasts**
- Artemis II coverage on NASA+ was built around milestones and an onsite countdown clock ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/03/30/nasas-artemis-ii-launch-mission-countdown-begins/)).
- The roughly 40-minute far-side loss of signal was framed as "normal and anticipated" ([ABC10](https://www.abc10.com/article/news/nation-world/artemis-ii-lunar-communication-loss-how-long/507-13219682-00a9-4404-9f7d-3f3b45cbfdb3)). Silence was part of the story.
- JWST's first images were "released one by one" ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-reveals-webb-telescopes-first-images-of-unseen-universe/)).
- Commentators:
  - Hugh Harris was known for "calm, professional commentary" and pre-scripted his liftoff line ([WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/science-space/2023-02-15/voice-of-nasa-hugh-harris-has-died)).
  - Rob Navias is "authoritative, prepared… calm and stylish" ([JSC Roundup](https://roundupreads.jsc.nasa.gov/roundup/2244)).
- The 1975 Danne & Blackburn manual set Helvetica, grids and "technological precision" ([We Are The Mutants](https://wearethemutants.com/2020/01/09/unity-precision-thrust-the-nasa-graphics-standards-manual-1975/)).
- **Lesson:** a calm voice, exact figures, one reveal at a time.

**PBS NOVA and PBS Space Time**
- NOVA's open is "only 15 seconds" and travels "from the atomic to the cosmic scale" ([SIGGRAPH](https://history.siggraph.org/artwork/wgbh-tv-design-department-nova-opening/)): one motion idea.
- Theme by Walter Werzowa ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/zero/credits.html)), also author of Intel's five-note logo ([Intel](https://timeline.intel.com/1995/the-intel-bong)). This is a precedent for our four-note motif.
- Space Time (astrophysicist Matt O'Dowd, typically 8–10 minutes) "trusts viewers with the real story without… bad metaphors, jargon" ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/show/pbs-space-time/)).

**Vox and Kurzgesagt (explanatory mechanics only)**
- Vox blends narration, archive and animation ([Video Consortium](https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-1-joss-fong)). Its black-hole video was praised for "precise use of music" ([Open Notebook](https://www.theopennotebook.com/2020/01/07/videogram-how-a-vox-video-explains-the-science-behind-the-first-photo-of-a-black-hole/)). Commentators note its highlighter sweeps and type landing on the spoken word ([Easy-Peasy](https://easy-peasy.ai/blog/how-to-make-vox-style-videos-with-ai)).
- In Kurzgesagt, narration "provides the timing for the animation team", and Epic Mountain scores every video ([Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/what-we-do?visit=videos)).
- **Take:** visuals timed to words, one concept per graphic, scale comparisons. **Not** the bright rounded palette ([U-M](https://artsatmichigan.umich.edu/ink/2019/09/27/simple-bright-beautiful-the-work-of-kurzgesagt)).

**BBC Planet Earth**
- Attenborough's script is written last, to the cut ([CinemaBlend](https://www.cinemablend.com/television/1600210/david-attenborough-still-uses-vhs-to-script-planet-earth)), at about 110–120 wpm ([analysis](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-can-we-learn-from-sir-david-attenborough-public-speaking-robin)).
- The 4.5-minute iguana chase carries 89 words ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2026/05/04/tv/david-attenborough-voice-nature-earth-100-years)).
- The Planet Earth II composers "carved out a certain frequency" for his voice ([TV Academy](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/mix/earth-wind-and-choir)).
- **Lesson:** pictures breathe, words are scarce, and the music leaves room for the voice.

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (writer prompt and prosody)

**Pace and prosody**
- Nova: 140–150 wpm, slowing to about 115 on the key-figure sentence.
- UNIT-8: 125–135 wpm, even, with a narrow pitch range. It sounds robotic by monotone, not by pitch shift.
- Pauses:
  - comma 180 ms;
  - full stop 350 ms;
  - before a figure 250 ms;
  - after a story 600 ms.

**Sentences**
- 10–16 words, 22 at most, one idea each. One 3–6-word sentence per story.

**Register**
- Units in full ("kilometres", "degrees Celsius").
- Banned: "amazing", "incredible", "mind-blowing", "epic", "wow", "out of this world", "stellar", and space puns.

**Analogies**
- At most one per story, from Nova only.
- Qualitative and number-free. Never compute conversions or comparisons.

**Humour**
- Only in chats and AND FINALLY. UNIT-8 takes an idiom literally and Nova lets it pass straight-faced.
- At most one idiom per episode. UNIT-8 is never wrong about a figure.
- Grave stories get no chat, no joke and no light cue.

**Banter**
- 2 lines at most, 6–10 s, never before the lead, no new facts.
- UNIT-8 says "Dr Reyes". Nova says "UNIT-8", spoken "unit eight".

**Hand-overs**
- Nova leads. UNIT-8 owns NUMBER OF THE DAY: "UNIT-8, the number." / "Number of the day: …", with the figure exactly as stated.

**Numbers and names**
- Read figures as stated: "1.3 million" is "one point three million". No scientific notation. Negative temperatures are "minus".
- Missions are spoken as words ("Artemis two").
- Full name first ("the James Webb Space Telescope", then "Webb"). Acronyms are spelled out on first use. Earth places as "city, country" from the gazetteer.

### Structure (built live from feeds)

| # | Segment | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open: one lead-story line over its picture | 8–12 s |
| 2 | Open (template) | 4 s |
| 3 | Wide greeting, both named, one teaser | 5–8 s |
| 4 | Lead story (Nova) | 25–35 s |
| 5 | Optional chat (UNIT-8 first) | 6–10 s |
| 6 | Story 2, or NUMBER OF THE DAY (UNIT-8) | 20–30 s |
| 7 | Story 3, AND FINALLY when supported | 20–30 s |
| 8 | Wide close, then end card | 6–8 s |

**Ordering rules**
- NUMBER OF THE DAY is never the lead and never on a grave story.
- No chat after a grave story.
- An unsupported lighter is dropped, not invented.
- Total running time: 2.5–3.5 min.

### Set and light

**Set**
- One step darker than the home look: black, ink and slate, with a purple scenery tint of no more than 12%. The sides fall to black.
- The magenta desk line is the only accent.

**Light**
- Key from upper front, camera-left, neutral: skin is never tinted.
- Rim: 1 px silver on hair, shoulders and UNIT-8's steel casing.

**Video wall**
- Idle: a static starfield and one planet with a soft terminator.
  - Straight bands in two or three adjacent ramp tones.
  - Rotation no faster than 1 px per 2 s.
  - No twinkle.
- In a story: the story picture, or a diagram in fog and silver on ink.

**UNIT-8 is an instrument, not a toy**
- 1×2 px steel or white eye slits, with no pupils.
- A 3×1 px white speech indicator that follows the voice envelope.

### Camera and directing (runtime rules)

**Shots**

| Shot | Used for | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Wide two-shot | Greeting, chat, close | — |
| Single MCU | Story links | — |
| Full-screen picture | Story pictures | 6–10 s, Ken Burns 2–4 px/s with eased ends |
| Diagram | Explanations | 5–8 s, one move (e.g. a 3–4 s scale pull-back, NOVA-style) |
| Locator map | Earth stories | 3–5 s |
| Fact or number card | Figures | See Graphics |

**Rhythm**
- Minimum shot 4 s (the channel's is 3).
- Singles average 6–9 s.
- Hold 0.6 s of picture after the voice ends.

**Cut or move**
- Cut between people and between shots. The set camera never moves.
- Motion lives only inside pictures and diagrams.

**Gestures**
- At most one arm gesture per sentence, and at most one per 6 s.
- Start 0.3 s before the stressed word.
- Nova: `steeple`, `raise_hand`, `point_screen` (only when the wall has content), `glasses`, `chin`, `nod`.
- UNIT-8: `nod` (mechanical tilt), `look_partner`, `count`, `lean_in`.
- Banned: `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up`, `facepalm`. `laugh` in chat only.
- `B:nod` only in the wide.

**Number of the day**
1. Cut to UNIT-8's single on "Number of the day".
2. Cut to the number card on the figure word.
3. Hold 1.5 s of silence.

### Graphics

**Palette tokens**
- `P.magenta` on bars, rules and tags only. `P.purple` is its shadow and the tint.
- Text is `P.white` or `P.silver` on `P.black` or `P.ink`. No pink or magenta text.
- `set.js` currently uses `text: P.pink` for the space theme; fix that.

**Strap**
- The channel STRAP and STRAP_TIMING.
- Kicker in caps: SPACE, CLIMATE, BIOLOGY, EARTH.
- Optional micro source line.

**Number card (NASA-manual sober)**
- Figure in 5×7 at 2× in white, a 1 px magenta rule, the label in 3×5 fog and the unit in silver, all on an 8 px grid.
- 0.25 s wipe on. **Never count up:** the in-between values were never stated.

**Scale diagram**
- Only when `numbers` has two values in the same unit.

**Quote card**
- A 1 px magenta underline sweeps under the words in sync with the word timings (Vox's highlighter, translated).

**Motion timings**
- In 0.4 s ease-out on integer pixels. Out 0.3 s.
- Hold for words ÷ 3 + 2 s.
- Diagram elements build one per narrated noun, at least 0.5 s apart.

### Music and sound

**Bed**
- From the cosmos colour in `themes.js` (E lydian #4), 72–88 BPM.
- Soft-triangle pad, sine drone, and bells (at most 4 notes per bar) with long echo.
- No drums under speech.

**Mix**
- Keep 1–4 kHz free for the voice: pad low, bells high.
- The bed sits at least 18 dB under the voice.

**Where beds play**
- Cold open, pictures and diagrams.
- AND FINALLY at −4 dB more.

**Silence**
- Singles, chats (the deadpan lands dry) and grave stories.
- Before the number: duck out 0.5 s before the figure, and land one bell note on the card lock.

**Stings**
- The open.
- A 1.2 s NUMBER OF THE DAY sting (three motif notes).
- The break stingers.
- No whooshes, lasers, theremin or "boing".

**UNIT-8 voice**
- Near natural pitch, with a 10–15 ms metallic comb at about 15% wet. A deep pitch shift reads as comic or menacing.

### Transitions

- **Into the show:** stinger, then cold open, then the open, then a hard cut on the downbeat to the wide.
- **Between stories:** hard cut. The wall content changes on the cut.
- **NUMBER OF THE DAY:** the strap tag flips in 0.3 s and the sting plays.
- **Into a break:** wide shot, Nova's toss, a 1 s hold, the stinger.
- **Out of a break:** stinger, wide shot, bug glint.
- **End:** a 0.5 s dip to black before the end card (the only one).

### Do / Don't

**Do**
- Make one white figure the hero, and let silence carry the reveal.
- Pair a deadpan robot with a straight-faced partner.

**Don't (childish or cheap)**
- Wavy candy stripes on planets (the current open's magenta wave), saturated nebula gradients, twinkling or shooting stars.
- A robot with googly eyes, a smile or a bounce. "Beep boop".
- Exclamation marks, "wow", puns, rockets with faces.
- Count-ups, a spinning globe behind a speaking head, or music under chat.
- Kurzgesagt's bright palette, or a second accent colour.

## 4. Acceptance checklist

1. The script has no banned words, exclamation marks or puns, and sentences average 16 words or fewer.
2. Measured pace: Nova 135–155 wpm, UNIT-8 120–140 wpm.
3. UNIT-8 reads the number, and the spoken figure equals `numbers[].value`.
4. Grave stories have no chat, light cue or bed.
5. No studio shot is under 4 s. Pictures run 6–10 s with a visible slow pan.
6. The set camera never moves. Only pictures and diagrams move.
7. Faces are the brightest, warmest pixels in the wide. The wall average is L\* 45 or below.
8. Accent pixels are only magenta or purple, on bars, rules and tags. No coloured text.
9. The number card wipes on with no count-up and holds at least 1.5 s after the spoken figure.
10. The bed is silent under singles and chats, ducks before the figure and sits at least 18 dB under the voice.
11. No `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up` or `facepalm`, and at most one arm gesture per 6 s.
12. The wall idle has no twinkle, and the planet moves at most 1 px per 2 s.
13. UNIT-8 has no pupils or mouth shape, and its indicator does not flicker.
14. There are no stingers between stories. The only dip to black is before the end card.
15. Runtime is 2.5–3.5 min, and every segment traces to feed data.
