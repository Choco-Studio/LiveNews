## 1. Identity

WORLD NOW is the channel's main news. Two people who have seen everything, and are rattled by none of it, brief the viewer.
- The room is dark and still, and the voices are unhurried.
- The graphics are white type on ink, with one red accent.
- The music is low brass and timpani, never chip arpeggios.

It is for adults who want the world in about two and a half minutes, without hype. The wit is a dry raised eyebrow, and it comes only at the end.

The whole programme should feel like the open the owner liked (`$SP/shots/opens/v2_world-now/sheet.png`):
- a navy field;
- red as the only programme accent, plus the network logo's small yellow dot;
- generous empty space;
- a lock-up that settles and holds.

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (writer prompt plus voice request)

**Writing**
- Plain public-broadcaster English, in the active voice and in declarative sentences.
- Headlines are in the present tense ("Floods force 40,000 from homes").
- Each story opens with its most striking fact, then the attribution (as `server/writer.js` already asks).
- At most 20 words per sentence, averaging 11–14, with one idea and one figure per sentence *(our rule)*. This matches the writer's existing "one idea per sentence" guidance.
- Write numbers as digits with units ("3 percent"). Round only where the source does, and never put two figures in one sentence.
- **Places:** stories set abroad name the place in the first sentence, within its first six words ("In Nairobi, ..."). Obscure cities get their country ("Goma, in eastern Congo") *(our rule)*. The map then comes on the second sentence (see Camera).
- **No question marks** in headlines, story text, tosses or the greeting. A neural voice reads "?" with a rising contour, and the flagship never sounds unsure *(our rule)*. Chat lines may hold one "?" per episode. No "!" anywhere.

**Pace.** We use the definition in `tools/voice/measure.py`: words ÷ segment duration, including planned pauses.

| Who / when | Target | How the voice pipeline gets there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paco, stories | 158–168 wpm, aim 163 *(our rule)* | Preset `speed` in `tools/voice/presets.json`, calibrated with `python3 tools/voice/measure.py rates` (its `TARGET_WPM` must change from 170 to 163) |
| Lola, stories | 166–176 wpm, aim 171 *(our rule)* | Same tool; `TARGET_WPM` lola goes from 178 to 171 |
| Round-up items (Lola) | 174–185 wpm *(our rule)* | Speed ×1.05 for those segments |
| Grave segments | About 6% below the presenter's normal pace *(our rule)* | Speed ×0.94, plus `pauseAdd` +0.10 s |
| Spoken headlines (Paco) | Articulation about 180 wpm; no inner pauses | Each line is one phrase |

- **Pauses:** WORLD NOW asks for `pauses: {comma: 0.18, sentence: 0.42, semicolon: 0.28, colon: 0.28, dash: 0.22}` *(our rule; the engine defaults are 0.15, 0.35, 0.25, 0.25 and 0.20, see `tools/voice/textnorm.py` DEFAULT_PAUSES)*.
  - Between stories, the gap is 0.7 s; before "And finally" it is 1.0 s. These are director gaps, today a fixed 0.3 s *(our rule)*.
  - The engine already stretches pauses by 1/√speed, so grave pauses grow by a further 3% or so.
- **Why the words sit at normal speed but the programme sounds slow:** a 13-word sentence at 163 wpm overall lasts 4.8 s. Take away 0.42 s at the full stop and 0.18 s at a comma, and the words themselves run at about 186 wpm. That is in line with Boyd's 180 wpm and Rodero's "normal" band. The authority comes from the pauses.
- **Why we sit below the BBC's reported 198 wpm:** our audience is international and often listens in a second language. A synthetic voice also lacks the stress a trained reader uses to carry a fast read *(our rule)*.
- **This is also the programme's pace signature:**
  - WORLD NOW is the slowest of the news shows. Tech stories, money and news-60 run 165–175, 165–175 and 165–185 wpm (their bibles).
  - It is faster than COSMOS, which runs 140–150 wpm.
  - Paco and Lola appear in no other programme, so their presets can carry this calibration directly.

**Prosody the engine cannot do.** The voice pipeline exposes speed, the pause table, `pauseAdd`, a per-phrase `pauseAfter` and voice blending (`tools/voice/engine.py`). It has no pitch control. So the old pitch rules become writing rules:
- "Falling pitch at sentence ends, no upspeak" becomes: declarative sentences only, with no "?" (above).
- "Paco's dry line at unchanged pitch" becomes: write it as a plain statement, with no "!", no "?" and no emphasis words ("really", "very", "so"). Follow it with 0.3 s of extra silence, set as a `pauseAfter` on that phrase.
- "Narrow the pitch range on grave stories" is **aspirational**. It waits for a pitch-range control in the engine. Today a grave story gets only slower speed, longer pauses and the `serious` or `sad` emotion on the face.

**Humour and banter**
- No humour in the headlines, the greeting, the lead, the main stories or the round-up.
- Wit lives only in "And finally", the chat after it and the sign-off. No puns, no "wow", and never mockery of people in a story.
- **Chat:** at most 2 chat segments (`maxChats: 2` in `config/channel.json`), each of 1–2 sentences, and only directly after "And finally". No chat after a grave story.
- **Toss:** Paco hands over with Lola's name and a full stop: "Lola." It is still one word and still dry, now with a falling read. `channel.json` chemistry and the writer's hand-over example (`"${b}?"` in `server/writer.js`) must change for WORLD NOW.
- Lola says "Thanks, Paco" at most once per episode. There is no toss after a grave story.

**Lola's remit** (`channel.json` gives her "the human angle" and has her "deflate his dryness"; these rules give that structure):
- She reads the main story with the clearest human angle: people affected, a named person, or a grounded `quote`.
- She reads the whole round-up and "And finally" *(our rule)*.
- Her chat line after "And finally" is the deflation. It answers Paco's dry line with a short, plain fact or a wry understatement, and never with a laugh, a "!" or a pun.
- She is the second voice in the greeting.

**Headlines in detail** *(our rules unless tagged)*
- **Lines:** 7–10 words each, in the present tense, naming the place where there is one. No "?", no "!", and figures only if grounded (`numbersGrounded`, `server/facts.js`).
- **Who reads:** Paco reads all three, in one voice, for authority. That is also three short phrases for the voice stage to make.
- **Timing:**
  - The open's final downbeat cuts to headline 1, and the first word comes 0.5 s after the cut.
  - Each **beat** is the line plus a 1.0 s gap, held to at least 3.8 s. A short line extends its gap.
  - Lines of 7–10 words at about 180 wpm last 2.3–3.3 s, so a beat runs 3.8–4.3 s and the whole sequence 12–14 s.
- **What the viewer sees:** one shot per beat, cut on the line's first word. No presenter is in vision. The shot is picked from the story's data, in this order:
  1. its picture, full screen, with the slow pan ART_DIRECTION allows for photos;
  2. else its locator map;
  3. else a navy title card with the category tag.
  
  Every shot carries the caption `headline` (at most 48 characters, the writer prompt's limit) in body 5x7 on an ink bar in the strap position, with the 1 px red rule under it and the source in micro type.
- **Overrides:** this beat replaces the director's `MONTAGE_FRAME = 2.6` s, which breaks the 3 s minimum, and ART_DIRECTION §5.1's 2.6–4 s.
- **Sound:** see Music. The drum hit and pips sit only in the gaps.

**Greeting**
- Greet by the studio clock (London; ART_DIRECTION §4): "Good morning" from 05:00, "Good afternoon" from 12:00, "Good evening" from 18:00. Then "I'm Paco Pixel." and Lola: "And I'm Lola Byte." *(our rule)*.
- Today the `intro` is one speaker, so Lola's line needs a new optional `intro.lineB`. Without it, Paco names both presenters.
- No aside in the greeting.

**Lengths.** These are outcomes, not targets. Under the grounding rules the writer may only use facts from the summary, so **never pad**. A thin summary makes a short story.
- **Lead:** 3–5 sentences, at most 520 characters, which is `LIMITS.text` in `server/writer.js`. That is about 88 words, or 32 s at 163 wpm.
- **Other stories:** 2–4 sentences, at most 450 characters (about 76 words, or 27 s).
- **Round-up items:** exactly 1 sentence of 12–20 words (4.0–6.9 s), so every item's shot clears 3 s *(our rule)*.
- **Budget:**
  - a typical episode: 4 + 13 + 5.5 + 25 + 2 × 20 + 26 + 20 + 1 + 8 + 6 + 3.8, plus about 3.6 s of gaps, comes to about **2:35**;
  - the ceiling is about **3:20** (every segment at its maximum, 4 round-up items and 2 chats);
  - a minimal script can run under 2:00, and that is acceptable.
- This needs `programs.world-now.stories` raised from 6 to **8** (lead, 2 main, 4 round-up items, And finally) and the new `storyLength` string (see §4). With `stories: 6`, a 3-item round-up plus And finally leaves room for only one main story.

**Fallbacks.** Air with what there is, and never stall (BRIEF, live production rule).

| Missing or late | What airs instead |
| --- | --- |
| Fewer than 3 rundown stories | 2 headlines with 2 beats. With 1 story or none, skip the headlines: the open cuts straight to the greeting. |
| `intro.headlines` missing or invalid (under 6 or over 12 words, an ungrounded figure) | The caption `headline` is spoken instead, and the 3.8 s beat hold covers the shorter line. |
| No picture for a headline or a story | The locator map. Failing that, the title card in the headlines, or the source card on the wall in a story. The beat list simply omits `full`. |
| No figure, or an ungrounded figure, for the fact card | Skip the fact beat. The Number of the day is also dropped (`writer.js` already does this). |
| 2 located candidates | A 2-item round-up. The validator rewrites the title to "Now, around the world." (no "in 30 seconds"). |
| Fewer than 2 located candidates | No round-up. Those slots become a third main story, or go unfilled. |
| No `lighter` candidate, or the last story is grave | No And finally and no chat. The sign-off is plain, and the bulletin ends after the last main story. |
| Breaking candidate | It leads, after the director's full-screen `breakingCard` (at most 3 s, as `director.js` and ART_DIRECTION §4 allow) with the ♭3 sting. |
| Neural voice late or failed for a segment | Optional segments (a round-up item, a chat, And finally) are dropped. Required ones (headlines, greeting, lead, mains, sign-off) fall back to browser TTS (`public/js/audio.js`), with shots timed by sentence. |
| No gazetteer time zone for a place | No local-time line under the map label. |
| A segment's speech is shorter than 3 s | The shot holds in silence up to 3 s, for at most 0.9 s of hold, rather than cutting early *(our rule)*. |

- **the desk backdrop:** used on a grave story or while no footage is ready. It is a dark field with a soft wash from above (never a halo round the head) and the region's dotted map, with a pulsing pin on the place.

**TWO-WAY.** Two 16:9 boxes, 176×99 at y 44, framed in fog with an accent rule under each, on a quiet ink hatch:
- the left box holds the presenter's single, its studio wall at rest (a plate cut by the box edge would show half a word);
- the right box holds the correspondent before their backdrop;
- each box is labelled in its corner: the studio's city (LONDON) and the place.

**Footage** (`server/footage.js` → `/api/vid/<id>.webm` → `public/js/footage/`)
- **What a clip may show:** the place only, never the event. The query is the place's own name; a peninsula or a region films as its country.
- **What is accepted:** only a clip whose title says it is a shot of a place (aerial, drone, street, harbour, skyline, old town, view... and the French, Spanish, Portuguese, German and Italian words Commons uploaders use; German compounds such as Hauptstraße). The place's name alone is not enough ("Germany 2019").
- **What is rejected:** clips whose title names an event, a person speaking, a production or a map; any person by role or name ("President ... greets the Chancellor": a live search for Germany returned it), children, an occasion (a festival, a strike, a visit). There is never footage on a grave story.
- **The clip itself:** free licences only, a 240p/360p VP9 transcode of a few megabytes, downloaded once, credited **FILE · author · licence** on air.
- **Limits:** Commons is polite-use: one request at a time, 2 s apart, honouring `Retry-After` on a 429 (a minute at least). Answers are cached by place for a day, across restarts.
- **Pixel style:** 192×108 shown at exactly 2x, 12 frames a second (the stepped motion of pixel animation). The grade is pixelate.js's (saturation 1.25, contrast 1.12). The palette is the shot's own (16 colours, k-means): kept for the whole shot and replaced only at a cut in the footage, so pans never flicker. Dithering is 4×4 Bayer, fixed to the screen.
- **What never airs:** the opening titles (a clip starts a fifth in), a title card or a fade to black (skipped), the closing credits (it turns back first).
- **Fallbacks:** offline (the fixture feeds) the desk is off, and links show the story's picture or map.
- **The headline montage** (round 2): a teased story with no picture of its own shows its place's footage instead of the map (never a grave one), with FILE and the clip's credit under the clock. The links' clips are found first; the montage's are prepared first on the client, because they air seconds after the open. A clip counts as ready only once it stands at its start point, so the first picture on air is never its opening frame.

**Graphics of a link** (`graphics/remote.js`)
- the correspondent's name and desk in the lower third for the first 5.5 s of their first picture, then the story's headline again;
- the place under the clock (a dateline: the story's place);
- FILE with the clip's credit whenever footage is on screen;
- the two-way's corner labels.

**IN THEIR WORDS.** When a story sentence reads the quote its source carries (`seg.quote`, grounded by the validator), that sentence goes to the quote card:
- the words, an accent quotation mark and the attribution in micro type;
- it takes the place of the cut planned for that sentence (it never adds one: the cut rate holds) and only with room to air the 4 s minimum;
- the caption does not repeat the words on the card.

It is enabled per programme by the pace profile's `shots.quoteCard` (WORLD NOW only for now).

**Pace.** Between the parts of a link the gap is `link` (0.4 s): a two-way keeps its turns close. Every shot of a link still airs the 4 s floor. The shots of each part are planned before it airs (`public/js/linkplan.js`), from its sentence times: the recorded voice's words, else the audio engine's own timeline (`audio.sentenceTimes`). Every way of opening the wanted shots on sentence starts is played through; the plan closest to them wins, with every shot holding the floor, the next part's cut included. The studio's first cut after a link never waits, so that one is a hard limit; the correspondent's words never play over the studio. As aired (Rhea, Germany): two-way 7.4 s (the hand-over and her first line), pictures 7.9 s, two-way 7.8 s (the prompt, her answer, the thanks).

### Format round 2: STILL TO COME

**STILL TO COME.** The mid-programme signpost ("Still to come: ...", a presenter's line) shows the later stories it names. The writer finds them (`normalizeBulletin`: `seg.stillToCome`, at most two, each with where its words start). It never shows a grave story, nor one already aired, whatever the provider wrote. The director (`playStillToCome`, pace `shots.stillToCome`) cuts to it at the line's start:
- **one story:** the montage's headline frame (its picture, else its place on the map) under a STILL TO COME tag, without the montage's pips;
- **two stories:** one panel, no cut. A tile for each, 168×84, the picture pixelated at the tile's own size (never a scaled-down card), else the place on a dot map with a pin. Both come in from the cut. The second waits dimmed, its headline in fog, and lights up left to right as the voice names it.

It airs only when the shot on air has held the floor and the frame will hold it too before the next story cuts back to the studio. Otherwise the line is read to camera as before. A signpost is no banter, so it also airs next to grave news, said soberly (no nod); the story it names is never a grave one. The captions go to the top (y 46–72), as over the montage. The mock no longer teases the very next story, nor adds "and our number of the day" when the number comes straight after.

### Format round 2: the boards

A story's card beat (pace `shots.numbers` and `shots.known`: WORLD NOW, and TECH BYTES since its format round, `tech-bytes.md` §3.10) shows the richest of three things:
1. **BY THE NUMBERS**, when it states two or three figures (`seg.numbers`; the writer keeps only spoken ones). These are the stack card's rows: figure at 2x, its qualifier in micro type above it, its words beside it. Never on the number of the day, which keeps its own card.
2. **WHAT WE KNOW**, when the writer gave two or three key points (`seg.known`, programme `"boards": ["known"]`). The points sit on the same black panel, one after another, each beside an accent square: at 2x when every point fits on one line, else at 1x in up to two lines.
   - Each point is held to a headline's standard (`headlineGrounded`: every content word, time span and figure is the source's), within 44 characters. No question, quote or invention ("The strike will last a month" is dropped).
   - At finalize a point whose figure the presenter does not say is dropped. The set never shows a figure we did not report.
   - The fallback writer takes them from the source's short clauses, only for hard news (the lead, a grave or breaking story, a hard-news topic). Each needs a verb of its own and never leans on another clause ("than it expected").
   - The attribution comes off a count ("the agency says about 1.2 million homes are without power") or a plain fact ("Nearby roads were closed, officials said"), never off a claim. "Geffray said the closures were a security precaution" keeps its speaker or stays off the board.
3. **The single fact card**, as before.

Boards round 3 (10 Oct) adds two more (pace `shots.timeline` and `shots.change`; programme `"boards": ["known", "timeline", "change"]` on WORLD NOW, TECH BYTES, COSMOS DESK and MONEY MINUTE). A story carries one card; the order is FROM → TO, then BY THE NUMBERS, then WHAT WE KNOW, then HOW WE GOT HERE, then the single fact.
- **FROM → TO**, when one figure moved and the story says so ("raised its main interest rate from 4.5% to 4.75%"; `seg.change` { from, to, label }). The label is the kicker. The old value (fog) lands first, an arrow draws across in the programme's accent, then the new value (white) lands at its head, with UP or DOWN by the values' scale ("$1.2 billion" to "$870 million" is DOWN). The values are as big as both fit side by side (3x, 2x, else 1x), the group centred. The writer's pair must be in one source sentence that says the figure moved ("from X to Y", "to Y from X"), the label grounded, and the presenter must say both values. The fallback writer reads the label from the object of a verb that moves it ("raised its main interest rate") or the subject of one that moves ("Inflation fell"), never a pronoun, never inside a quote.
- **HOW WE GOT HERE**, when the source dates the earlier steps that led to the story (`seg.timeline`: two or three { when, what }). A rail draws left to right; each step lands on it as the rail reaches it, its date above (2x, the month cut to three letters: "MAR 2023"; all at 1x when one would not fit) and what happened below (1x, up to three lines). Each date is a year (with its month) the source states, never past this year: a step, not a plan. Each step is held to a WHAT WE KNOW point's standard within 36 characters, put in date order, one step a date. A story with WHAT WE KNOW keeps that board. The fallback writer takes "in 2019", "in March 2023" or "on 12 March 2023" from the whole article (history sits deep in it), never a span ("since 2019", "by 2030"), a plan, a forecast or a clause that leans on another.

**Where a board goes.** It comes right after the map; a single fact still comes last. On a story handed to a correspondent the board comes first, because the hand-over owns the last sentence. The plan of such a story ends where the hand-over starts, so no beat, cap or max-hold guard cuts back to the presenter just before the two-way. A board needs 5 s of room (read once) and holds longer when there is more.

As aired (Germany, the lead): presenter 7.5 s, WHAT WE KNOW 6.2 s, the two-way on the hand-over 6.8 s, pictures 5.5 s, the two-way 7.1 s.

The figure extractor no longer takes a duration describing a noun ("a 24-hour strike") as a figure, and stops a label at "lit" ("50 metres high lit up the sky").

### Format round 2: the first run on real news

The fixture feeds are clean; the live feeds are not. WORLD NOW was run on the real feeds (4 Oct, the fallback writer, the day of Brazil's election). These faults would have aired, and are fixed for every programme and every writer:
- **A false figure.** The desk split "more than 158.7 million Brazilians" at its decimal point, took the first half for a site menu and dropped it, so "7 million Brazilians are eligible to vote" reached the writer. Sentences are now split by one shared rule (`facts.js sentencesIn`, and `transcript.js sentencePieces` for the desk's clean-up): never inside a figure, after a title or a month ("Dr.", "Sept."), or after initials ("U.S.") unless a sentence plainly starts again.
- **Broken sentences.** "warned that the choice was between democracy [and barbarism]", "a bid for a “partial", "its decision to relocate was made", "pick lawmakers, senators", "how a citizen of Oman, an Arab country…", "the country's multiethnic". A sentence cut to length (`trimClause`) now keeps a pair, a quotation, a parenthesis and a list whole. It never leaves a clause it opened without its verb, a light passive, a span of time or an adjective without what follows, and never treats an opening phrase as the clause. Otherwise the sentence is not aired. A long sentence that joins two whole clauses is told as two sentences (`splitClauses`), the way a broadcast writer shortens.
- **Cut articles and headlines.** An article cut to length ends on a real sentence end, never on "U.S.". A shortened headline is cut at a clause boundary first ("What to know about Brazil's election"). It never splits a name ("Lula and Flávio") or drops a particle ("face [off]").
- **One event, many stories.** Brazil's election came from about 20 reports. Stories sharing two people now cluster, as do reports of one country's election (the country read from names and demonyms: "the Brazilian president"). "Polls close" is no closure. The programme carries Brazil once.
- **Lines that are not news.** A promo for another outlet's interview ("…commentator Douglas Herbert shares further insights", "…speaks to analyst X about…"), an outlet's standing disclaimer ("NPR does not offer or accept money for coverage"), a bare quotation with no speaker, an opener whose "also" leans on a paragraph the viewer never heard, and a detail that tells again what the story already said.

**Places.**
- A story is placed where it happened, not in the country that acted. "US withdraws all B-1 bombers from British military base RAF Fairford" was pinned to the middle of the USA and handed to the Americas desk.
- The gazetteer knows the US states and a few territories ("off the coast of Massachusetts", Bermuda, Puerto Rico).
- A place that is only a whole country carries `scope: 'country'`: the map names it over its middle, with no pin and no coordinates. A pin in Kansas once stood for "off the US coast".

**Length.** The fallback writer now gives grave stories depth too, reads up to nine sentences of an article and splits instead of dropping. On real news WORLD NOW runs about 830 words, roughly six minutes, against the 9–10 the LLM writer is asked for. The fallback writes no more than the reporting supports: no padding.

- **Key light:** from upper front, slightly camera-left, and the same for every shot. Faces are flat in colour but directional in placement, with one shadow tone 1–2 px wide on the far side (ART_DIRECTION §3; `public/js/v2/canvas25d/character.js` already lights from camera-left). Faces stay on the true `P.skin` and `P.tan` ramps.
- **Rim light:** 1 px of `P.silver` on top of the hair and on the shoulder (ART_DIRECTION).

- **Video wall:** a `P.navy` field with ink falloff (Bayer 4x4), average L\* at or below 45 (ART_DIRECTION).

  - Breaking news goes on the strap and the 3 s card, never on the wall.

### Camera and directing (from segment data and word timings, seeded per episode)

**Shots:** the wide two-shot, a single MCU for each presenter, a full-screen picture, a map and a fact card.
- **The single:** the speaker sits in the third nearer their desk position, and the wall's picture or title fills the opposite third. This is the BBC, NBC and NHK framing in ART_DIRECTION §2 frames *(our rule, derived from those frames)*.

**Beats.** There is one beat per sentence (as `storyBeats` in `director.js` does today), in the order single → map → picture → fact card → single.
- **Story:** the first sentence is always on the speaker's single, and it names the place. The map cuts in on the first word of the second sentence.
  - This keeps ART_DIRECTION's "never cut away during a link's first sentence".
  - It overrides ART_DIRECTION §5.4's "a map opens foreign stories" for everything except the round-up.
- **Round-up:** voice-over, all on the map.
  - The title line plays over the world view, with all the item places marked dim.
  - On item 1's first word, the map zooms to place 1 (1.2 s, eased *(our rule)*).
  - Items 2–4 cut map to map on their first word.
  - Today `storyBeats` gives one-sentence items only a presenter shot, so a director change is needed.
- **Cuts:** only at sentence ends, from word timings.
  - A sentence that would make a shot shorter than 3 s is merged into the next beat.
  - The director never cuts mid-sentence. This replaces the deferred `MIN_SHOT` cut in `playStory`, which can land inside a word.
- **Hand-over:** cut to B's single on B's first word. A one-word toss and the chats stay on the wide, with the listener looking at the speaker (ART_DIRECTION §5.6).

**Shot lengths** *(our rules; not measured from broadcasts, see §2)*

| Shot | Length |
| --- | --- |
| Any shot | at least 3.0 s |
| Median across the episode | 4.5–7 s |
| Studio shot | at most 15 s |
| Full-screen picture | at most 8 s, then back to the single at the next sentence end |
| Map | holds for its sentence: 3–7.5 s, given the 20-word cap |
| Fact card | at least (words in `fact` ÷ 3) + 2 s, ending at the next sentence end. `fact` is at most 48 characters, so 3.3–4.7 s. The label ("BY THE NUMBERS", "KEY FACT") is fixed, so the hold counts the fact text. |

**Camera moves.** This **overrides ART_DIRECTION §3 "Motion budget"** ("the camera must stay still") and its Don't "Move the camera on the set", for WORLD NOW. BRIEF now asks for "slow, confident camera moves", and the canvas25d 2.5D camera re-renders every frame, so the old reason (drifting a baked bitmap jitters) no longer applies. ART_DIRECTION needs the matching edit.
- **Who moves:** only the 2.5D camera, re-rendering each frame. Never scale or scroll a baked bitmap.
- **Per shot:** at most one move, at most three per episode. Each is eased in and out, lasts at least 4 s and changes scale by at most 6% *(our rules)*.
- **Window:** a move starts at least 0.5 s after the cut and ends at least 0.5 s before the planned next cut. If the shot planned from word timings is shorter than the move plus 1.0 s, there is no move. That removes the old clash between a 4 s push and a short greeting.
- **The three allowed moves:**
  - a settling push of up to 4% on the greeting wide;
  - a 3–4% push on the lead's opening single;
  - a pull-out of up to 4% on the sign-off wide, during its 1.5 s hold.
- **Grave segments are locked off.**
- **No jitter:**
  - for a static landmark (the desk-plate edge, a wall-bezel corner), the frame-to-frame displacement during a move is monotone and at most 1 px per frame at 60 fps;
  - no pixel inside a flat panel changes colour more than once during the move, so there is no shimmer.

**Gestures** (cue names from `public/js/cues.js`)

| Moment | Allowed |
| --- | --- |
| Greeting | `nod`, once per presenter |
| Stories | 1–2 from `nod`, `lean_in`, `steeple`, `raise_hand`, plus `point_screen` only when a picture or map follows within 1.5 s |
| Hand-over on the wide | `look_partner` or `point_partner` |
| And finally and chat | additionally `shrug` and `shake_head` |
| Sign-off | `nod`, then `papers` in the 1.5 s hold |
| Grave segments | `nod` and `steeple` only |

- **Banned in WORLD NOW:** `wave`, `thumbs_up`, `wow`, `fist_pump`, `facepalm`, `laugh`, `point_camera`, `chin`, `glasses`, `count`. `money-minute.md` and `news-60.md` already ban the first six.
- **`chuckle` (owner decision 7, 10 Oct):** a soft, closed-mouth chuckle heard before a line (the speaker's own voice, under the line's level), once per programme at most, only in light banter (the sign-off after Lola's dry last word is the usual place). The cartoon `laugh` stays banned.
- **Timing:** no gesture in the first 0.5 s of a shot. On the wide, the listener looks at the speaker and nods at most once.
- **`raise_hand`** plays at its single amplitude, since ACTIONS has no size parameter.
- **Code changes these need:**
  - a per-programme allowlist, plus a grave subset, in `parseCues` or the writer's normaliser (today `cues.js` drops only the `light` actions on grave stories);
  - the writer's default intro and outro cue changes from `wave` to `nod` for WORLD NOW;
  - the director's fallback gesture for grave stories (`lean_in`) becomes `steeple`.

### Music and sound

Key of D. The WORLD NOW colour note is the 3rd (`public/js/audio/themes.js`).

The signature instrument is **low `brass` over `timpani`**, carried on from the v2 open (`themes.js`, `OPENS['world-now']`).
- No sibling bed uses it: `money-minute.md` bans brass, and the tech beds are `pulse12` arpeggios over a `pad`.
- WORLD NOW beds use no `pulse12` lead.

| Moment (tempo, BPM) | Music *(our rules)* |
| --- | --- |
| Headlines (88–96) | `tri` roots, with low `brass` chords (D3 and below while anyone speaks) moving Bm → G → D across the three lines, Lowe's minor-to-major arc. In each gap there is one `timpani` hit, then the pip sting. |
| Pip sting (each gap) | Two `bell` notes, low 5 then 1: the motif's first two notes (`MOTIF` in `themes.js`). The third sting lands on 1 and the cut to the greeting falls on the next downbeat. The bed then releases in 0.3 s. |
| Greeting, lead, main stories, Number of the day | Silence |
| Round-up (96–104) | A muted `pluck` ostinato in eighths, plus `tri` bass, with no melody and no drum under speech |
| And finally (84–92) | Sparse `softtri` and `pluck`, entering after the words "And finally" and running through the chat |
| Sign-off | The motif resolving 3 then 1, led on `brass`. The shared `signoff` cue in `themes.js` has these notes but leads on `softtri`, so WORLD NOW needs a variant. |
| Breaking | One ♭3 motif sting |
| Grave segments | No bed and no sting. The next segment has no bed either. |

**Mix.** Measure rendered stems with `measureLoudness()` from `public/js/audio/loudness.js` (BS.1770-4).
- **Under speech:** the bed's short-term loudness is at least 18 LU below the voice's over the same window. This is the channel floor that `channel-and-breaks.md`, `tech-bytes.md` and `cosmos.md` also use *(our rule)*.
  - No source found in either pass justifies one exact figure, and the siblings `money-minute.md` (18–22) and `news-60.md` (20–24) differ. The audio lead should fix one channel number.
  - The first draft's support for 18–20 dB (a production blog and an arXiv paper nobody could open) has been dropped.
- **In gaps of 0.6 s or more:** the bed may rise to −10 LU relative to the preceding speech, the same as `DUCK_LEVEL` 0.32 in `public/js/audio/synth.js`. This is where the headline drum and the pad arc are heard, so the borrowed Lowe technique survives the deep mix.
- **Stings and timpani:** they never overlap speech. Each must start at least 0.1 s after the last word ends. The loudest 400 ms momentary block must be at least 6 LU below the integrated loudness of the speech around it *(our rule)*.
- **The global duck is only −10 dB.** The 18 LU floor needs the bed levelled at least 8 dB under the voice before the duck, or a per-programme duck depth. This is an audio task.

**Banned:** saw leads, arpeggios faster than 16th notes, major-pentatonic tunes, noise beyond a quiet hat, and a `pulse12` lead in any bed.

### Transitions

- **Into the programme:** as set out in `channel-and-breaks.md`, the break's ident cuts on the downbeat to the open's navy field. `director.js` currently plays a stinger there; that is a director change owned by that bible.
- **Open to headlines:** a hard cut on the open's final downbeat.
- **Headlines to greeting:** a hard cut to the wide on the downbeat after the third sting.
- **Between stories:** always a plain cut, never a stinger or a wipe.
- **Into the round-up:** a cut to the world map on the title line's first word. The red rule draws under "AROUND THE WORLD" (`FEATURE_KICKERS` in `writer.js`) in 0.35 s.
- **Into "And finally":** a 1.0 s pause, then a cut to Lola's single.
- **Out of the programme:** the sign-off on the wide with its 1.5 s hold, then the 0.8 s stinger, then the end card. Master control then runs the break.
- **There is no mid-programme break.** `server/station.js` `advance()` inserts a break only after a whole `episode`, so there is no "More after this", and the programme tag after a break belongs to the next programme's open.

### Do / Don't

**Do:**
- keep the room still and cool and the faces warm;
- use one red accent and one red rule;
- keep silence under the news;
- name the place, then show the map;
- let the pauses carry the authority;
- save the wit for the end.

**Don't:**
- bounce, overshoot or a pulsing marker;
- sound effects, puns, "!" or "?" in story text or captions;
- the banned gestures above, including any wave or celebration;
- banter after a tragedy;
- saturated colour on set, or red glows;
- any camera motion outside the three moves;
- melodic "game level" loops or chip arpeggios, which read as a kids' show.

## 5. Acceptance checklist

Each item names how it is checked.

1. **Headlines** (word timings, cue log): 2–3 lines of 7–10 words. From the open's cut to the greeting cut takes 12–14 s. Each beat lasts at least 3.8 s. Each gap holds one timpani hit and one pip sting, both starting at least 0.1 s after the line's last word.
2. **Cuts** (director log): the open hard-cuts to headline 1, and the headlines hard-cut to the wide. Inside the programme, the only stingers are the `breakingCard` one and the one into the end card.
3. **Shots** (shot log against word timings): every shot is at least 3.0 s, and the median is 4.5–7 s. Studio shots are at most 15 s and pictures at most 8 s. Every cut falls within 0.1 s of a sentence boundary, or of a headline or round-up item's first word.
4. **Maps** (shot log, word timings): each story map cuts in on the first word of the story's second sentence, within ±0.1 s. Each round-up map cut lands on its item's first word, within ±0.1 s. Item 1 zooms instead of cutting.
5. **Beds** (tune log): no bed under the greeting, the lead, the main stories or the Number of the day. No bed or sting in a grave segment or the segment after it.
6. **Mix** (`measureLoudness` on rendered stems):
   - during speech, the bed is at least 18 LU under the voice, short-term;
   - in gaps of 0.6 s or more, it is at most −10 LU relative to the speech;
   - stings peak (momentary) at least 6 LU under the speech's integrated loudness.
7. **Pace** (word timings, `measure.py` definition):
   - each presenter's median over story segments is 158–168 wpm for Paco and 166–176 wpm for Lola;
   - round-up items run 174–185 wpm;
   - grave segments run at least 5% below that presenter's median;
   - no sentence exceeds 20 words, and round-up items are 12–20 words.
8. **Text** (string checks):
   - no "!" anywhere, and "?" only in chat lines, at most one per episode;
   - no `happy` emotion in the greeting, the lead, the main stories or the round-up;
   - at most 2 chats, all directly after And finally, and none after a grave story;
   - the toss is written "Lola.", and "Thanks, Paco" appears at most once.
   
   Whether the wit lands is a human judgement.
9. **Colour mask** (frames): red appears only in the bug, the strap tag, the desk plate, the 1 px rules, markers and BREAKING, and never as a gradient. Yellow appears only in the LATEST plate and the logo dot.
10. **Values** (frames, CIE L\*): faces are 55–73 and the brightest warm area. Head-zone backgrounds are 18–45. The wall averages 45 or less (ART_DIRECTION §3 values table).
11. **Camera** (deterministic lab frames):
    - at most one move per shot and three per episode;
    - each move is eased, at least 4 s long and at most 6% in scale, starts at least 0.5 s after its cut and ends at least 0.5 s before the next;
    - there are none in grave segments;
    - a landmark's per-frame displacement is monotone and at most 1 px, and no flat-panel pixel changes colour more than once.
12. **Graphics** (frames): graphics enter in 0.35 s, with text 0.1 s later, and leave in 0.25 s. Position is monotone, so there is no overshoot. The fact card stays within y 26–136 and holds at least (fact words ÷ 3) + 2 s.
13. **Gestures** (cue log): only allowlisted cues appear. Grave segments use only `nod` and `steeple`. There are at most 2 gestures per story, and none in a shot's first 0.5 s.
14. **Ending** (logs): And finally never follows a grave story. The sign-off plays on the wide with a 1.5 s hold and the brass 3→1, then the stinger, then **the end card**.
15. **Fallbacks** (offline channel with fixture variants that have no images, no places, no `lighter` and a failed voice): every episode airs according to the fallback table, with no stall and no shot under 3 s.
16. **Human gate** (not machine-checkable; items 8, 9 and 13 are its proxies): at 1x and at 5x, it reads as serious news in pixel art, never as a kids' show.
