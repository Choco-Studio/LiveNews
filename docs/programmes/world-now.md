# WORLD NOW: style bible

The flagship world bulletin with Paco Pixel and Lola Byte. This builds on `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md`, which is not repeated here.

Sources: this session's egress proxy blocked direct fetching of every non-GitHub site, so the claims below come from search summaries of the linked pages. "Our rule" marks a decision with no source.

## 1. Identity

WORLD NOW is the channel's ten o'clock news. The viewer is briefed by two people who have seen everything and are rattled by nothing. The room is dark and still, the voices are calm, and the graphics are red on navy.

It is for adults who want the world in four minutes, without hype. A dry raised eyebrow is fine.

The whole programme should feel like the open the owner liked: a navy field, one red accent, and a lock-up that settles and holds.

## 2. Real references

**BBC News at Ten (Studio B, 2022; BBC News channel, 2023)**
- **Set and camera:** the set was picked because it was "physical, not virtual", and it runs on full robotic camera automation ([TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/live-production/we-wanted-a-studio-that-was-physical-not-virtual-behind-the-design-and-technology-of-bbc-news-studio-b)). So moves are slow, steady and repeatable.
- **Theme:** David Lowe's theme is built on the radio pips, a bass for "strength and solidarity and depth" and a heavy drum as the "big sound for the headlines". Its chords go from minor to major: "anxiety going into calmness" ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/watch-bbc-news-theme-composer-demonstrate-how-he-made-it-25-years), [NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2015/11/03/column-david-lowe-the-man-who-changed-news-music-forever/)).
- **Reading pace:** BBC One newsreaders average 198 wpm, with a range of 152–241 ([study](https://francis-press.com/uploads/papers/pv7j7Z9uRgQyPUxnVTDTPecUAEP0HJcWWhGErVeD.pdf)). British radio favours about 180 wpm ([Broadcast Journalism](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/broadcast-journalism-6th/9780240810249/xhtml/ch17.xhtml)).

**ITV News at Ten** (added: the best headline device and closer)
- **Headlines:** Big Ben's "bongs" separate the opening headlines, so each one gets a sonic full stop ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_News_at_Ten)).
- **And Finally:** a crisply written item that "made one or both of them smile". It must end upbeat but never on a discordant note, and it is harder to script than hard news ([Broadcast](https://www.broadcastnow.co.uk/behind-the-scenes/the-importance-of-an-and-finally/5153349.article)).
- **Shot length:** a study of ITV bulletins found a median shot of 6.3 s, ranging from 2.7 to 40.8 s ([study](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Shot-types-in-the-opening-headline-sequence-of-an-ITV-news-bulletin_fig1_263129239)).

**Sky News (Studio 21)**
- **Light:** tuned to keep the architecture "as white as possible whilst retaining warmth in the skin tones" ([NCS Q&A](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2016/12/06/sky-news-glass-box-studio-21-design/)).
- **Camera:** robotic cameras on ceiling rails give "shots and jibs to create a flowing presentation" (same source).
- **Lesson:** the camera may glide, but only as a motivated robotic move. Keep the room cool and the faces warm.

**CNN International (London)**
- **Light:** soft, flat light at about 5000 K, with colour only as "pools" on the architecture ([TM Broadcast](https://tmbroadcast.com/cnn-london-lighting-workflow/)). Skin tones are checked on a waveform monitor and spectrometer ([Litepanels](https://www.litepanels.com/en/applications/cnn-london-case-study/)).
- **Music:** each show's theme carries the CNN sonic logo ([Stephen Arnold](https://stephenarnoldmusic.com/stephen-arnold-music-cnn-show-themes/)).
- **Lesson:** keep our motif in every cue, and skip CNN's EDM energy.

**Al Jazeera English (2022)**
- **Music:** 26 cues "driven by propulsive strings and suspenseful horns" ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2022/04/21/al-jazeera-theme-music/)).
- **Graphics:** a "flexible, dynamic, functional brand" with wide walls for backgrounds and timelines ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2022/04/25/al-jazeera-branding-broadcast-design/)).
- **Lesson:** in chiptune, that gravitas becomes a held pad with a repeating pulse figure.

**France 24, DW, NHK World**
- **France 24 (2024):** built from news images, in "black and white with dark blue backgrounds" and touches of cyan ([Puremédias](https://www.ozap.com/actu/france-24-revoit-son-habillage-et-adopte-le-slogan-liberte-egalite-actualite/450436)).
- **DW:** every graphic is built from geometric shapes taken from the logo ([German Design Award](https://www.german-design-award.com/en/gallery/detail/corporate-identity/dw-corporate-design-refresh-2024)).
- **NHK:** the normal register is detached and factual. Urgency is reserved for tsunami warnings ([Verification Handbook](https://datajournalism.com/read/handbook/verification-1/preparing-for-disaster-coverage/8-preparing-for-disaster-coverage/8-1-how-nhk-news-covered-and-learned-from-the-2011-japan-earthquake)).
- **Lesson:** use a dark blue field and one recurring device, and change register with severity. Ours gets slower and lower, never louder.

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (writer prompt and voice prosody)

**Writing**
- Plain public-broadcaster English in the active voice.
- Headlines in the present tense ("Floods force 40,000 from homes"). Each story leads with its most striking fact, then the attribution.
- At most 20 words per sentence, averaging 11–14, with one idea and one figure per sentence ([UF/IFAS](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/WC193)).

**Speech rate and prosody**
- Paco reads at 165–175 wpm and Lola at 172–185 wpm. Round-up items go up to 190 wpm, and grave stories are 6% slower. Listeners rate 170–190 wpm as "normal" ([Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/media/radio-news-pace-words/)); Paco sits slightly below it for authority (our rule).
- Sentences end on a falling pitch, with no upspeak. Pauses are 0.15 s at a comma, 0.35 s at a full stop, 0.6 s between stories and 0.8 s before "And finally".
- Grave stories narrow the pitch range by 20% and add 0.1 s to every pause.

**Humour and banter**
- No humour in main stories. Allow one understated aside in the greeting or sign-off, plus the wit of "And finally".
- Paco's dry line is read at an unchanged pitch, followed by 0.3 s of silence.
- No puns, no exclamation marks, no "wow", and never mockery of people in the story.
- Chat happens only next to "And finally" and the sign-off: at most 2 chats of 1–2 sentences each.
- Paco tosses with one word ("Lola?"). Lola says "Thanks, Paco" at most once per episode.
- No toss after a grave story.

**Numbers and places**
- Write numbers as digits with units ("3 percent"). Round only when the source does, and never put two figures in one sentence.
- Foreign stories name the place within the first six words ("In Nairobi, ..."). Obscure cities get their country ("Goma, in eastern Congo").

### Structure (assembled live from the selected candidates)

| # | Segment | Source | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Headlines: 3 lines of 10 words or fewer, each followed by a pip sting | top 3 headlines | 10–14 s |
| 2 | Open (existing template), hard cut on the downbeat | template | 4 s |
| 3 | Greeting with both names | intro | 5–8 s |
| 4 | Lead story: single, then map or picture, then fact card. A breaking story always leads. | biggest story | 25–40 s |
| 5 | Main stories 2–3, presenters alternating | next candidates | 20–35 s each |
| 6 | Around the world in 30 seconds: 2–4 one-sentence items | located candidates | 20–30 s |
| 7 | Number of the day (optional, never on a grave story) | `numbers` | 12–18 s |
| 8 | And finally, then at most 2 chat lines | `lighter` | 20–35 s |
| 9 | Sign-off on the wide shot, then the end card | outro | 6–8 s |

The whole bulletin runs 3–4.5 minutes. After a grave story there is no chat. "And finally" is dropped rather than following a grave story.

### Set and light: the "night bulletin" look

- **Background:** one ramp step darker than the daytime shows. Use ink and black, with a slate pool behind each head.
- **Key light:** neutral and flat, like CNN's ~5000 K. Faces stay on the true `P.skin` and `P.tan` ramps.
- **Rim light:** 1 px of `P.silver`.
- **Practicals:** none warm. The room is cool and the faces are warm, as at Sky.
- **Video wall:** a `P.navy` field dithered toward ink, at L\* 45 or below.
  - At idle it shows the open's dotted globe, turning about once every 120 s, with a 2x2 red London marker.
  - During a story it shows the picture or a locator map.
  - Breaking news goes on the strap, never on the wall.
- **Red on set:** the desk plate is the only red block.

### Camera and directing (from segment data, seeded per episode)

**Shots:** a wide two-shot, a single MCU for each presenter, a full-screen picture, a map and a fact card.

**Shot length:** median 6–7 s, minimum 3 s, studio maximum 15 s.

**When to cut**
- Cut at sentence ends, using the word timings. The one exception is the map, which cuts in on the spoken place name.
- Cut to B on B's first word, and in the round-up, cut map to map on each item's first word.

**Camera moves**
- Only the 2.5D camera moves, re-rendering each frame. Never scroll a baked bitmap.
- At most one move per shot. Each move is eased, lasts at least 4 s and changes scale by at most 6%. It starts at least 0.5 s after the cut and never spans one.
- Three moves are allowed:
  - a settling push on the wide shot after the open;
  - a 3–4% push on the lead single during its first two sentences;
  - a slow pull-out on the sign-off.
- Grave stories are locked off.

**Gestures**
- 1–2 per story, from: nod, lean_in, steeple, small raise_hand, and point_screen when a picture follows within 1.5 s.
- No gesture in the first 0.5 s of a shot.
- On the wide shot, the listener looks at the speaker and nods at most once.
- Grave stories get nods only.

### Graphics

**Colours**

| Element | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| Accent | `P.red`, with `P.darkRed` as its shadow |
| Bars | `P.ink` |
| Text | `P.white` |
| Labels | `P.fog` |
| Map sea and wall | `P.navy` / `P.ink` |
| Land | `P.slate` / `P.steel` |
| LATEST plate | `P.yellow` (only here) |

**Signature device:** a 1 px `P.red` rule under every title (strap, fact card, map label, round-up title), taken from the open's underline. It is one device, in the manner of Al Jazeera and DW.

**Type:** display (5x7 at 2x) only for fact values and the round-up title. Body 1x for straps. Micro for places, sources and times.

**Maps**
- Zoom from world to region in 1.2 s, eased.
- The marker is a 3x3 red square whose white ring expands once.
- In the round-up, show a micro local time under the place, France 24 style, but only when the gazetteer has a time zone for it.

**Fact card:** keep it inside y 26–136. Hold it for the label's word count ÷ 3 + 2 s.

**Motion:** wipe in over 0.35 s, eased out. Text follows 0.1 s later. Exit takes 0.25 s. No overshoot.

### Music and sound

Key of D. The WORLD NOW colour note is the 3rd (see `audio/themes.js`).

| Moment (tempo, BPM) | Music |
| --- | --- |
| Headlines (104–112) | `pulse12` pips, `tri` bass in quarter notes, a `pad` moving from minor to major across the three lines (Lowe's arc), `timpani` on each downbeat |
| After each headline | Pip sting: two `bell` notes, low 5 then 1 (the motif's first two notes) |
| Main stories | Silence (our rule) |
| Round-up (100–108) | A muted `pluck` ostinato plus bass, with no melody |
| And finally (84–92) | Sparse `softtri` and `pluck`, entering after the words "And finally" |
| Sign-off | Motif resolving 3 to 1 |
| Breaking | One ♭3 motif sting |
| Grave stories | Never a bed, and the next bed waits one segment |

**Mix**
- Beds sit 18–20 dB under the voice ([guide](https://pureaudioinsight.com/blogs/content-production/background-music-volume-how-loud-should-it-be)). Loud beds are a leading cause of complaints that speech is hard to follow ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.14240)). That is deeper than today's global −10 dB `DUCK_LEVEL`.
- Stings sit at least 6 dB under the voice.

**Banned:** saw leads, arpeggios faster than 16th notes, major-pentatonic tunes, and noise beyond a quiet hat.

### Transitions

- **Into the programme:** the headlines bed leads into the open, and the open hard-cuts to the wide shot.
- **Between stories:** always a plain cut, never a stinger or a wipe.
- **Into the round-up:** the red rule draws across the strap in 0.5 s, then the map takes over.
- **Into "And finally":** a 0.8 s pause, then a cut to the single.
- **Into a break:** the wide shot, the line "More after this.", then the 0.8 s stinger.
- **Out of a break:** the stinger, then the wide shot, with the programme tag shown for 8 s.

### Do / Don't

**Do:** keep the room still and cool and the faces warm. Use one red accent and one red rule. Keep silence under the news. Name the place, then show the map. Save the wit for the end.

**Don't:**
- bounce, overshoot or a pulsing marker;
- sound effects, puns or exclamation marks in captions;
- celebratory gestures outside the greeting, the sign-off and "And finally";
- banter after a tragedy;
- saturated colour on set, or red glows;
- camera drift;
- melodic "game level" loops, which read as a kids' show.

## 4. Acceptance checklist

1. The headlines run 10–14 s, with one pip after each of the 3 lines.
2. The open hard-cuts to the wide shot, and no stinger plays between stories.
3. Every shot is at least 3 s and the median is 5–8 s. No studio shot runs over 15 s.
4. Each map cuts in within ±0.3 s of the spoken place name.
5. No bed plays under main stories, or in the segment right after a grave story.
6. Beds sit at least 18 dB below speech.
7. Paco reads at 165–175 wpm and Lola at 172–185 wpm. No sentence is over 20 words.
8. Main stories contain no jokes and no "!". There are at most 2 chats, and only around "And finally" or the sign-off.
9. Red appears only on the bug, strap tag, desk plate, rule, markers and BREAKING, and never as a gradient.
10. Faces are the brightest warm area, and the wall's average L\* is 45 or lower.
11. A shot has at most one camera move, eased and at least 4 s long. Grave stories have none, and nothing jitters.
12. Graphics enter in 0.35 s and leave in 0.25 s with no overshoot. The fact card stays within y 26–136.
13. "And finally" never follows a grave story. The bulletin ends on the wide shot with the 3→1 resolution.
14. At 1x and 5x it reads as "serious news in pixel art", never as a kids' show.
