# WORLD NOW: style bible

The flagship world bulletin, presented by Paco Pixel and Lola Byte. This builds on `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md`. It does not repeat that file's set, palette and graphics rules. It adds what is specific to this programme.

Research notes, 2026-10-02: in this session the egress proxy blocked direct fetching of every non-GitHub host, including YouTube thumbnails. Claims below therefore come from search-result summaries of the linked pages, and frame observations are reused from `ART_DIRECTION.md`. Nothing is invented. Where no source exists, the text says "our rule".

## 1. Identity

WORLD NOW is the channel's late-evening hour mark: the ten o'clock news of a pixel world. The viewer should feel briefed by people who have seen everything and are not rattled by any of it. That means a dark, still, beautifully lit room, two calm voices, and a few precise graphics in red on navy.

It is for adults who want the world in four minutes without hype. They will forgive a dry raised eyebrow, but never noise.

The open the owner liked sets the bar: a navy field, one red accent, a globe that builds and a lock-up that settles and holds. The whole programme should feel like that open kept going.

## 2. Real references

**BBC News at Ten (Studio B, 2022; the merged BBC News channel, 2023)**
- **Set:** BBC chose Studio B's design because it was "physical, not virtual" ([TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/live-production/we-wanted-a-studio-that-was-physical-not-virtual-behind-the-design-and-technology-of-bbc-news-studio-b)).
- **Camera:** the studio runs with full robotic camera automation (same source). Moves are therefore slow, constant-speed and repeatable, never handheld.
- **Music:** David Lowe built the theme up from the radio pips. He added a bass for "strength and solidarity and depth" and a heavy drum as the "big sound for the headlines". The chords go from minor into major, "a sense of anxiety going into calmness" ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/watch-bbc-news-theme-composer-demonstrate-how-he-made-it-25-years), [NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2015/11/03/column-david-lowe-the-man-who-changed-news-music-forever/)). The top-of-hour "Countdown" is part of the same family ([Musiklab](https://www.musiklab.co.uk/celebrating-25-years-of-the-bbc-news-theme/)).
- **Pace:** in 23 hours of BBC One news, readers averaged 198 words per minute, with a range of 152–241 ([study](https://francis-press.com/uploads/papers/pv7j7Z9uRgQyPUxnVTDTPecUAEP0HJcWWhGErVeD.pdf)). British radio favours about 180 wpm ([Broadcast Journalism, ch. 17](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/broadcast-journalism-6th/9780240810249/xhtml/ch17.xhtml)).
- **Takeaway:** a pulse plus a bass under the headlines, minor resolving to major, then the music stops and the reading carries the bulletin.

**ITV News at Ten (added: the best headline device and closer in British news)**
- **Headlines:** Big Ben's "bongs" separate the headlines read in the opening sequence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_News_at_Ten)). Each headline gets its own sonic full stop.
- **Closer:** the "And Finally" started as a short, crisply written film clip that "made one or both of them smile". Its writer says the programme should end upbeat but never on a discordant note, and that it is harder to script than hard news ([Broadcast](https://www.broadcastnow.co.uk/behind-the-scenes/the-importance-of-an-and-finally/5153349.article)).
- **Shots:** a study of ITV bulletins measured a median shot of 6.3 s, with a minimum of 2.7 s and a maximum of 40.8 s ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Shot-types-in-the-opening-headline-sequence-of-an-ITV-news-bulletin_fig1_263129239)).

**Sky News (Studio 21 "glass box")**
- **Set and light:** a white, minimal box. The lighting was tuned so the architecture reads "as white as possible whilst retaining warmth in the skin tones" ([NCS Q&A](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2016/12/06/sky-news-glass-box-studio-21-design/)).
- **Camera:** robotic cameras flown from the ceiling give "a variety of shots and jibs to create a flowing presentation" (same source).
- **Graphics:** made in-house, with angles and a hard shadow on logo text ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2016/10/24/sky-news-debuts-glass-box-studio-new-broadcasting-center/)).
- **Takeaway:** the camera may glide, but only as a motivated, robotic move. Faces stay warm while the room stays cool.

**CNN International (London)**
- **Light:** soft, gridded sources give a "relatively flat" image at about 5000 K. Colour exists only as par "pools" on the architecture ([TM Broadcast](https://tmbroadcast.com/cnn-london-lighting-workflow/)). The lighting director checks skin tones with a waveform monitor and a spectrometer ([Litepanels](https://www.litepanels.com/en/applications/cnn-london-case-study/)).
- **Graphics:** the 2023 package is "clean and modern" ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2023/06/01/cnn-new-graphics-chryons-lower-thirds/)).
- **Music:** London shows reuse the CNN sonic logo inside each theme ([Stephen Arnold Music](https://stephenarnoldmusic.com/stephen-arnold-music-cnn-show-themes/)).
- **Takeaway:** keep the sonic logo in every cue. Do not take CNN's EDM energy, which belongs to other shows.

**Al Jazeera English**
- **Music:** the 2022 rebrand brought 26 pieces "driven by propulsive strings and suspenseful horns", recorded with live instruments ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2022/04/21/al-jazeera-theme-music/), [case study](https://stephenarnoldmusic.com/case-study-al-jazeera-rebrand/)).
- **Graphics:** the brief was a "flexible, dynamic, functional brand", with wide walls for topical backgrounds and timelines ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2022/04/25/al-jazeera-branding-broadcast-design/)).
- **Takeaway:** a world bulletin can sound like strings and horns playing an ostinato. In chiptune that becomes a held pad plus a repeating pulse figure.

**France 24, DW and NHK World (the calm public-service end)**
- **France 24:** the 2024 Gédéon package is built from news images, "black and white with dark blue backgrounds" and touches of cyan ([Puremédias](https://www.ozap.com/actu/france-24-revoit-son-habillage-et-adopte-le-slogan-liberte-egalite-actualite/450436)). More live reporting means "taking the time to explain" ([France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/france/20240302-new-programmes-more-live-reports-france-24-gets-a-fresh-look)).
- **DW:** a 6° concave LED wall mounted so it seems to float ([PresseBox](https://www.pressebox.com/pressrelease/eyevis-gmbh/DW-News-Studio-with-High-Resolution-eyevis-LED-Video-Wall/boxid/769838)). Its 2024 design builds everything from geometric shapes taken from the logo ([German Design Award](https://www.german-design-award.com/en/gallery/detail/corporate-identity/dw-corporate-design-refresh-2024)).
- **NHK Newsline:** goes from Japan to Asia to the world, with business and weather as features ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK_Newsline)). NHK's normal register is detached and factual. After 2011 it adopted urgency only for tsunami warnings ([Verification Handbook](https://datajournalism.com/read/handbook/verification-1/preparing-for-disaster-coverage/8-preparing-for-disaster-coverage/8-1-how-nhk-news-covered-and-learned-from-the-2011-japan-earthquake), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2011/04/11/135314543/japans-public-broadcaster-responds-reports-crisis)).
- **Takeaway:** use the dark blue field, let images carry stories, and change register by severity. Ours goes slower and lower, never louder.

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (writer prompt and voice prosody)

**Register and sentences**
- Plain public-broadcaster English in the active voice.
- Headlines use the present tense ("Floods force 40,000 from their homes").
- Story leads give the most striking fact, then the attribution.
- At most 20 words per sentence, averaging 11–14. One idea and at most one figure per sentence. Broadcast guides cap sentences at about 20 words and advise one number per sentence ([UF/IFAS](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/WC193)).

**Words per minute** (inside the BBC range, and inside Rodero's 170–190 "normal" band ([Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/media/radio-news-pace-words/)))

| Who / segment | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Paco | 165–175 wpm |
| Lola | 172–185 wpm |
| Round-up items | up to 190 wpm |
| Grave stories | −6% rate |

**Prosody (neural voice)**
- Statements end on a falling pitch. Never upspeak.
- Pauses are 0.15 s at commas, 0.35 s at sentence ends and 0.6 s between stories. Before "And finally", pause 0.8 s.
- Grave stories narrow the pitch range by about 20% and add 0.1 s to every pause.
- Paco's dry line is read at the same pitch as the line before, followed by 0.3 s of silence. The joke is the flatness.

**Humour**
- Never in main stories.
- At most one understated aside in the greeting or sign-off, and the wit of "And finally".
- No puns, no exclamation marks, no laughing at people in the story, no "wow".

**Banter**
- Only next to "And finally" and the sign-off, with at most two chat segments of one or two sentences each.
- Paco tosses with one word ("Lola?"). Lola picks up with "Thanks, Paco." at most once per episode.
- No tosses or banter after a grave story.

**Numbers**
- Write digits with units ("40,000 people", "3 percent").
- Round only when the source rounds. Never stack figures in one sentence.
- Years and currencies go through the text normaliser ("2 billion dollars").

**Places**
- Foreign stories name the place within the first six words ("In Nairobi, ...").
- Lesser-known cities get their country ("Goma, in eastern Congo").
- The director cuts to the map on that word's timing.

### Structure (built live from the selected candidates; no pre-planned episodes)

| # | Segment | Built from | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Headlines**: 3 lines of 10 words or fewer, each closed by a "pip" sting (ITV's bongs, BBC's pips) | top 3 stories' headlines | 10–14 s |
| 2 | **Open**: existing 4 s world open, hard cut on the downbeat | template | 4 s |
| 3 | **Greeting**: both names, at most one aside | intro | 5–8 s |
| 4 | **Lead story**: single, then map or picture, then fact card | biggest story; `breaking` always leads | 25–40 s |
| 5 | **Main stories 2–3**: alternating presenters | next candidates | 20–35 s each |
| 6 | **Around the world in 30 seconds**: 2–4 one-sentence items on the map | located candidates | 20–30 s total |
| 7 | **Number of the day** (optional, never grave) | `numbers` | 12–18 s |
| 8 | **And finally**: lighter story, then at most two chat lines | `lighter` | 20–35 s |
| 9 | **Sign-off**: wide shot, end card | outro | 6–8 s |

The whole bulletin runs 3–4.5 minutes.

If any story's emotion is `sad` or `serious` with a grave topic:
- the next segment starts with no chat;
- if that story would be the last one, "And finally" is dropped rather than following it.

### Set and light variant: "night bulletin"

**Light**
- The background sits one ramp step darker than the daytime programmes: ink and black, with a slate pool behind each head.
- The key light is neutral white, like CNN's flat ~5000 K soft key. Faces keep the true `P.skin`/`P.tan` ramps.
- Rim light is 1 px of `P.silver`.
- No warm practicals. The room is cool and the faces are warm (Sky's rule).

**The wall** (`P.navy` field dithered toward `P.ink`, at most L\* 45)

| Moment | Wall shows |
| --- | --- |
| Idle | The open's dotted globe, turning about 1 revolution per 120 s, with a red `P.red` 2x2 London marker |
| Story | The story picture, or a locator map |
| Breaking | Picture only. The red goes on the strap, never on the wall. |

The desk plate is the only red block on set.

### Camera and directing rules (applied from segment data, seeded per episode)

**Shots:** wide two-shot, single MCU per presenter, full-screen picture, map, fact card.

**Shot length:** median 6–7 s, minimum 3 s, studio maximum 15 s (from ITV's 6.3 s median).

**Cuts**
- Cut only on sentence boundaries from the word timings, or on the place-name word for maps.
- Cut to B on B's first word.
- In the round-up, cut map to map on each item's first word.

**Moves** (only where the 2.5D camera re-renders the scene; never scroll a baked bitmap)
- At most one move per shot. Eased in-out, lasting at least 4 s, with at most 6% scale change. A move starts at least 0.5 s after a cut and never crosses one.
- Allowed moves, in the robotic BBC/Sky manner:
  - a settling push on the wide shot after the open;
  - a 3–4% push-in on the lead story's single, during its first two sentences;
  - a slow pull-out on the sign-off.
- Grave stories are locked off.

**Gestures**
- One or two per story, from nod, lean_in, steeple, small raise_hand, and point_screen when the wall or a picture follows within 1.5 s.
- No gesture in the first 0.5 s after a cut.
- In the wide shot, the listener looks at the speaker and nods at most once.
- In grave stories, nod only.

### Graphics

**Colour tokens**

| Use | Token |
| --- | --- |
| Accent | `P.red` (shadow `P.darkRed`) |
| Bars | `P.ink` |
| Text | `P.white` |
| Secondary labels | `P.fog` |
| Map and wall fields | `P.navy` / `P.ink` |
| Land | `P.slate` / `P.steel` |
| "LATEST" plate only | `P.yellow` |

**Shared device:** a 1 px `P.red` rule under every title, taken from the open's underline. It appears on the strap, the fact card, map labels and the round-up title. That gives one device across the package, as Al Jazeera and DW each use one.

**Type:** display 5x7 at 2x is for fact values and the round-up title only. Straps use body 1x. Place and source use micro.

**Maps**
- Zoom from the world to the region over 1.2 s, eased.
- The marker is a 3x3 `P.red` square with one `P.white` ring that expands once and does not repeat.
- The label is micro white on an ink plate with the red rule.
- In the round-up, a micro local-time line appears under the place, France 24 style. Only show it when the gazetteer supplies a time zone.

**Fact card**
- The value at 2x white, the label in micro `P.fog`, the red rule between them.
- It stays inside y 26–136.
- Hold for the label's word count ÷ 3 + 2 s.

**Motion:** wipe in over 0.35 s, eased out, on integer pixels. Text follows 0.1 s later. Exit over 0.25 s. No bounce or overshoot.

### Music and sound (refined chiptune; key D, the WORLD NOW colour note is the 3rd as in `audio/themes.js`)

**Headlines bed**, about 104–112 BPM:
- a `pulse12` "pip" figure;
- a `tri` bass in steady quarters;
- a `pad` chord that moves from minor to major across the three headlines (Lowe's "anxiety into calmness");
- `timpani` on each headline downbeat.

**Pip sting:** two `bell` notes, low 5 then 1, after every headline.

**Main stories:** silence. Our rule: the voice carries the bulletin.

**Round-up bed**, 100–108 BPM: a muted `pluck` ostinato in the AJE manner plus bass. No melody.

**And finally bed**, 84–92 BPM: `softtri` and `pluck`, warm and sparse. It enters after the words "And finally".

**Sign-off:** the motif resolves 3 to 1.

**Grave stories:** never a bed, and the bed after them waits one segment.

**Breaking sting:** the motif with the ♭3 colour, once, at the same level as other stings.

**Mix**
- Beds sit 18–20 dB under the voice ([mixing guidance](https://pureaudioinsight.com/blogs/content-production/background-music-volume-how-loud-should-it-be)). Loud beds are a top cause of intelligibility complaints ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.14240)). That means a deeper duck than the current global `DUCK_LEVEL` of −10 dB.
- Stings may peak 6 dB under voice level.

**Banned sounds:** noise only as a quiet short-mode hat; no saw leads; no arpeggios faster than 16th notes; no major-pentatonic tunes.

### Transitions

- **Into the programme:** the headlines bed cuts to the open, and the open hard-cuts to the wide shot on the downbeat.
- **Between stories:** always a cut. Never a stinger or a wipe.
- **Into the round-up:** the red rule draws across the strap over 0.5 s, then the map takes over.
- **Into "And finally":** the 0.8 s pause, then a cut to the reader's single.
- **Into a break:** a wide shot, one line ("More after this."), the 0.8 s stinger.
- **Out of a break:** the stinger, then the wide shot. The programme tag shows for 8 s.

### Do / Don't

**Do**
- Make it still, dark and cool, with warm faces.
- Use one red accent and one red rule device.
- Use silence under the news.
- Use the same calm cadence in every bulletin.
- Name the place, then show the map.
- Save wit for the end.

**Don't**
- Bouncy or overshooting graphics, or a pulsing marker.
- Music under grave or main stories.
- Exclamation captions, sound effects, puns, "wow".
- Gestures like wave, thumbs_up, fist_pump or laugh outside the greeting, sign-off or "And finally".
- Banter after tragedy.
- Saturated colours on set, or red glows.
- A camera that drifts.
- Rapid cuts under 3 s.
- Melodic chiptune "game level" loops: they read as a children's game.

## 4. Acceptance checklist

1. The headlines run 10–14 s, with exactly one pip sting after each of the 3 lines.
2. The open hard-cuts to the wide shot. Stories never use a stinger.
3. No shot is under 3 s. The median shot is 5–8 s, and no studio shot runs over 15 s.
4. Map beats start within ±0.3 s of the spoken place name.
5. There is no music under main stories and no bed within one segment after a grave story.
6. Beds measure 18 dB or more under speech (lab loudness).
7. Measured speech rate: Paco 165–175 wpm, Lola 172–185 wpm. No sentence is over 20 words.
8. No exclamation marks, puns or jokes in main stories. At most two chats, and only next to "And finally" or the sign-off.
9. The only red is on the bug, strap tag, desk plate, rule, markers and BREAKING. There are no red gradients.
10. In the wide and single shots, faces are the brightest warm area. The wall average is L\* 45 or below.
11. Camera moves are at most 1 per shot, at least 4 s long and eased, with none in grave stories and no visible pixel jitter.
12. Graphics wipe in at 0.35 s and out at 0.25 s with no overshoot. The fact card stays within y 26–136.
13. "And finally" never follows a grave story, and the bulletin ends on the wide shot with the 3→1 resolution.
14. At 1x and 5x, a cold viewer would call it "a serious news programme in pixel art", never "a kids' show".
