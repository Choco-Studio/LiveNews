# TECH BYTES — programme style bible

Programme: technology magazine with Max Circuit (enthusiast) and Ada Volt (sceptic). Accent: cyan. Builds on `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md`. Do not repeat its rules here; where this file is silent, that file applies.

> **Research note (2026-10-02).** The egress proxy blocked every direct fetch: BBC, Wikipedia, NewscastStudio, The Verge, Made in Colour, Fast Company, MasterClass, Eye, archive.org and YouTube thumbnails. So the claims below come from search-result summaries, each one linked. We could not measure frame-level facts such as cut rhythm, per-show words per minute or exact Click colours, so none are claimed.

## 1. Identity

A late-evening tech show for adults who use technology every day and are tired of hype. It should feel like a product studio at night:

- matte charcoal surroundings, with the object lit like a commercial;
- two colleagues at a desk: one leans in, the other raises an eyebrow;
- every story answers "what does this change for me?" and then asks the question the press release avoided.

Less WORLD NOW gravitas and more conversation, but never a gadget show for kids. Think Bloomberg Technology's analysis plus MKBHD's lighting discipline plus a two-hander that likes to needle.

## 2. Real references

**BBC Click → BBC Tech Now (BBC News, 30 min)**
- Click ran from 2000 to March 2025. Tech Now replaced it with a "more global, reporter-led approach" ([tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2025/03/26/bbc-tech-now-show/), [Click](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Click_(TV_programme))).
- Episode one went from air travel to Bitcoin in rural Zambia to SXSW to a zoo lab ([TechBuzz](https://techbuzzireland.com/2025/03/24/bbc-launches-flagship-technology-programme-tech-now/)), with a 30-minute runtime ([TVmaze](https://www.tvmaze.com/shows/84203/tech-now)).
- Spencer Kelly was known for making complex subjects "engaging, entertaining, and accessible" ([agency bio](https://performingartistes.co.uk/artistes/spencer-kelly)).
- Titles were by Made in Colour for 10 years ([Made in Colour](https://madeincolour.com/click)). The electronic theme was by Bella Saer with Yoad Nevo ([SoundCloud](https://soundcloud.com/bella-saer/bbc-click-title-theme)).
- **Lesson:** a magazine shape (link, report, link) where the presenter's job is clarity plus one human touch. Wit sits beside the investigation, never instead of it.

**Bloomberg Technology (Bloomberg TV, daily, 1 h)**
- Formerly Bloomberg West. Its staples are "analysis, commentary, interviews and interactive graphics" ([Bloomberg Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_Technology)). It is co-hosted from two cities, New York and San Francisco ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/btv/series/bloomberg-technology)).
- The 2015 package gave each show one colour plus one typographic symbol (West: green "+"), with set accent lighting matched to it ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2015/10/06/bloomberg-mixes-color-coding-symbols-in-new-graphics-package/)).
- The 2021 redesign added "chart and data visuals in a nod to the Bloomberg Terminal" ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2021/05/07/bloomberg-redesign/)). The music is 50+ themes anchored to a two-note sonic logo ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/reelworld--reel2media-create-new-sonic-brand-for-bloomberg-television-and-radio-301376292.html)).
- David Lowe's 2015 brief asked for a sound that was "pacy, contemporary, positive, technological, classy" ([NCS](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2015/10/05/bloomberg-moves-to-beat-of-new-theme-music/)).
- Mark Porter calls clutter "a major problem in financial television" and set out to clean up the data "without compromising the live action" ([Mark Porter](https://markporter.com/work/bloomberg-television)).
- **Lesson:** a per-show colour and symbol, data shown as calm charts, and no screen-filling tickers.

**Marques Brownlee / MKBHD**
- His studio look is matte black and deep grey, with the product as the hero and one accent colour ([style analysis](https://www.thumix.com/thumbnail-style/mkbhd)).
- Camera moves are precise and come from a robot arm, "Colossus" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90978935/marques-brownlee-mkbhd-youtube-tech-review), [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/software/marques-brownlee-shows-us-what-camera-mounted-robot-can-do-238687)).
- Reviews are almost fully scripted, with research, motion-graphics and script-supervision roles on the team ([Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/), [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/uk/think/search-and-video/marques-brownlee-interview/)).
- Lighting is three-point, with practicals for separation, all at the same colour temperature ([MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/best-lighting-for-video-recording-tips-from-marques-brownlee)).
- **Lesson:** "beauty shots" of objects on black, slow moves that are exactly repeatable, and a conversational tone that is actually written.

**Hard Fork (NYT, 2022–2026): the two-hander**
- Roose built it on the idea that the best chat shows have hosts "with genuine chemistry". Newton brought an improv background ([Platformer](https://www.platformer.news/hard-fork-machine-gods/), [Roose](https://kevinroose.substack.com/p/leaving-the-times)).
- It has recurring segments: "HatGPT" rounds up headlines and "Hot Mess Express" rates them ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/mt/podcast/hard-fork/id1528594034)).
- The theme is by Dan Powell ([Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/episode/432Ox9zz4cxWuYJlvVfNkz)), and Vjeran Pavic overhauled the video version ([Platformer](https://www.platformer.news/hard-fork-machine-gods/)).
- **Lesson:** named recurring segments give a live show its structure. The banter works because both hosts are reporters, not foils.

**WIRED Tech Support, The Verge and CNET: editorial confidence**
- Tech Support has an expert answer internet questions one card at a time ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2020/12/15/wireds-tech-support-video-series.html)).
- The Verge's 2022 identity uses one signature colour (Ultraviolet `#5200ff`), huge display type and saturated colour tiles ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/48536/the-verge-2022-rebranding), [tokens](https://oh-my-design.kr/design-systems/theverge)). Its reviews use whole-number 0–10 scores ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2024/04/11/the-verge-review-scale)).
- Collins rebuilt CNET around a serif inspired by the "golden era of journalism" ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/30-may-6-june-2022/cnet-rebrand/)).
- **Lesson:** grown-up tech brands signal seriousness with editorial typography. Borrow the one-question card and one big figure. Reject candy colour tiles.

**Pace**
- Listeners rated 170–190 wpm "normal". Recognition was best at 170 wpm for dense items and 190 wpm for light ones ([Journalist's Resource, Rodero 2015](https://journalistsresource.org/media/radio-news-pace-words/)).
- British radio favours about 3 words per second ([Broadcast Journalism](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/broadcast-journalism-6th/9780240810249/xhtml/ch17.xhtml)).

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (writer prompt and prosody)

**Pace**
- Stories: 165–175 wpm. Tech copy is dense, with names, versions and figures.
- Chat: 180–190 wpm.
- Max runs about 10 wpm faster than Ada. His enthusiasm shows in tempo and a wider pitch range, never in volume.

**Sentences**
- Average 9–15 words, maximum 22. One idea and at most one figure per sentence.
- Every story ends on a "for you" line, but only when the summary supports it.

**Register**
- Plain, precise and adult.
- Ban: game-changer, revolutionary, mind-blowing, insane, "wow", exclamation marks and puns.
- Jargon may be quoted only so Ada can translate it flatly ("A 'reimagined experience'. A new menu.").

**Humour**
- At most one dry line per story, always in a chat segment and never inside the factual sentences.
- Targets are hype, jargon and the industry. Never the viewer, never real people.
- None near grave stories: data breaches that harm people, layoffs, deaths.

**Banter**
- An exchange is two chat segments, A then B, so it is never more than two lines.
- Ada's question must be answerable from the summary, or be left hanging ("That's the question."). Never claim what a company "hasn't said".
- The last word alternates between Max and Ada, seeded per episode, so neither is the butt.

**Hand-overs**
- Prefer topic bridges ("Ada's been reading the small print.").
- First-name tosses only before a question. Don't use "Thanks, Max" more than once per episode.

**Spoken forms**
- Write units in full: "gigabytes", "999 dollars".
- Say the letters of AI, GPU and USB-C. Spell out other acronyms the first time.
- Versions as words: "iOS nineteen".
- Give the company and product in full the first time, short after that.
- Places: city first, and the country only if the city is not world-famous.

**Prosody**
- Pause 250 ms after attribution and 400 ms before Ada's question.
- After a punchline, leave a 600–800 ms beat before the next segment.
- Statements fall at the end. Only yes/no questions rise.

### Structure (built live from feeds)

The server picks from what the candidates support and skips any slot that has no grounded material.

| # | Segment | Built from | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cold open: Max's hook, then Ada's one-line qualifier as a chat | intro + chat | 8–14 s |
| 2 | Open | template | 4 s |
| 3 | Lead story | story | 25–35 s |
| 4 | THE CATCH: Ada asks, Max answers | 2 chats | 8–14 s |
| 5 | Story 2, read by the other presenter | story | 20–30 s |
| 6 | NUMBER OF THE DAY | feature `number` | 15–20 s |
| 7 | Story 3 | story (+ optional catch) | 20–30 s |
| 8 | AND FINALLY, plus a one-line exchange | feature `lighter` + chat | 25–35 s |
| 9 | Sign-off, one line each | outro | 5–8 s |

Rules:
- At most 4 chats (`maxChats`), never after a grave story.
- A story with 2–3 `numbers` gets a BY THE NUMBERS card. A story with a `quote` gets a quote card.
- **Optional server feature `quickfire`:** 2–3 one-sentence items in the style of Hard Fork's HatGPT, as pictures without a map and with no chat. Place it after slot 5 when there are more good candidates than slots.

### Set and light: "product studio at night"

**Scenery**
- One palette step darker than WORLD NOW: `P.black` and `P.ink` fields.
- No navy on the set; navy stays WORLD NOW's wall colour.
- Blue tint at most 6%.

**Key and rim**
- Key and faces are as in ART_DIRECTION. Faces stay neutral; never cyan.
- The rim stays `P.silver`.

**Cyan**
- Only the accent line: the desk LED and the wall bezel, 1 px wide, at most 2% of pixels.

**Sides**
- One mirrored vertical `P.slate` slit per side, like a softbox edge. It is static.

**Video wall**
- Idle: near-black, with a 1-px `P.slate` circuit trace and one `P.cyan` 2×2 node. Static.
- In a story: the picture, dimmed so its average L\* is at most 40.
- Product stories: a "beauty" treatment. The image sits on `P.black` with a Bayer black-to-ink vignette, and one 1-px highlight sweeps left to right once (0.8 s) when the picture arrives.

### Camera and directing (runtime rules)

**Shot choice**
- Links go to the `close` single to lens, with the lower third 1 s after the cut.
- A story with an image: first sentence on the over-the-shoulder wall, then `full` for 4–6 s, then back to `close` for the "for you" line.
- Chats play on the `wide` two-shot:
  - the speaker turns to the partner (`look_partner`);
  - Ada delivers a deflating last line to lens while Max keeps looking at her;
  - never cut on a punchline; hold the wide for 1.2 s after the last word.

**Shot length**
- 3–12 s, averaging about 6–7 s: livelier than WORLD NOW, calmer than NEWS IN 60.

**Moves**
- With the 2.5D camera, one push-in per episode on Ada's question in `close`: 3–5% scale over 6–8 s, eased in and out, rendered in 2.5D and not by scaling sprites.
- Full-screen pictures push 2–4% or pan at most 6 px/s.
- Otherwise the camera is locked off. Cut between segments; move only within a segment.

**Gestures**
- At most 2 per segment for Max and 1 for Ada.
  - Max: `lean_in`, `raise_hand`, `point_screen` (only when introducing pictures) and `count`.
  - Ada: `steeple`, `chin`, `glasses` (before her question), a slow `shake_head` and `shrug`.
  - The listener: only `nod` and `look_partner`.
- Start a gesture 0.2–0.3 s before the stressed word, hold the apex through it and release over at least 0.4 s.
- Banned in this show: `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up`, `facepalm`. `laugh` is allowed only as a closed-mouth smile on the lighter story.

### Graphics

**Accent and symbol**
- Accent: `P.cyan`, for thin rules and the tag row (black text, via `inkOn`).
- Programme symbol, Bloomberg-style: a static 2×5 px cyan cursor block after "TECH BYTES" in the programme tag and on the cards. It blinks once at the open, never again.

**Lower third**
- Tag row: kicker such as AI, CHIPS, PRIVACY or SPACE TECH.
- White headline on `P.ink`.
- Name supers once each: "MAX CIRCUIT · TECHNOLOGY CORRESPONDENT" and "ADA VOLT · TECH ANALYST".
- No strap during chats unless the chat is about the story on screen.

**Number card**
- `P.black` field, with the figure in display type (5×7 at 2×, white).
- Label in micro 3×5 `P.fog`, and a 1 px cyan underline that draws in 0.3 s; the figure cuts in 0.1 s later.
- No count-up odometers: intermediate values are false numbers.

**BY THE NUMBERS**
- Up to 3 rows: right-aligned figures, labels in fog, with `P.slate` rules between rows.

**Charts**
- One cyan series. Others in `P.steel`, axes in `P.slate`.

**Quote card**
- White body text with a 1 px cyan bar on the left and the attribution in micro fog.

**Motion timings**
- In: 0.35 s ease-out. Out: 0.25 s ease-in. Integer pixels, no bounce.

### Music and sound

**Theme**
- Keep the network motif with the b7 dorian colour, in A, as in `themes.js`.

**Beds** (`music.cue(moment, { programId: 'tech-bytes', emotion })`)
- 100–108 bpm, felt in half time.
- Instruments:
  - soft `tri` bass on the roots;
  - a `pulse12` arpeggio at velocity 0.4 or less, with a dotted-eighth echo;
  - a `pad` on dorian i–IV (Am9 to D);
  - very soft closed hats, on light beds only.
- No kick or snare under voice. Low-pass the pulses at about 2.5 kHz while someone speaks.
- Mix: beds sit 18–22 dB under the voice.

**Where beds play**
- Under: the cold open, chats, NUMBER OF THE DAY, quickfire, AND FINALLY and the outro, which rises to the end card.
- Silence under story links, and full silence for grave stories.

**Stings**
- NUMBER OF THE DAY: the motif on `bell`, 1.2 s.
- End-of-feature button: two notes resolving to A, 0.6 s.
- No swooshes, glitches, modem or typing noises, beeps or boings.

### Transitions

- Inside the show, every change is a cut.
- When the wall changes content, a 1 px cyan scan line wipes the wall only, top to bottom in 0.4 s.
- Into a break: the "TECH BYTES" lock-up with cursor holds for 2 s, then the network stinger (0.8 s).
- Out of a break: stinger, then the wide two-shot, the bug glint and the programme tag for 8 s.

### Do / Don't

**Do**
- Light the object like an ad, on black.
- Use one cyan line.
- Let Ada ask what viewers think.
- Give exact figures.
- Hold deadpan reactions on the wide.
- Keep negative space around the number.

**Don't**
- Use glitch titles or RGB split. `set.js` still has a "glitch now and then" for tech: remove it.
- Use magenta tech palettes. `set.js` still uses them: move to cyan.
- Use matrix rain, binary code, HUD rings, circuit boards everywhere, neon gradients or Verge-style candy tiles.
- Use robots or objects with faces, or emoji icons.
- Use bouncy gestures, exclamation captions, sound effects or sitcom zooms on Max.
- Use more than one dry line per story.

## 4. Acceptance checklist

1. In a 16-frame contact sheet of a full episode, cyan covers at most 2% of the pixels in set shots and never appears on a face.
2. No magenta, purple or glitch or RGB-split frame anywhere in TECH BYTES.
3. Scenery is darker than WORLD NOW's (head-zone background L\* at most 35), and faces stay the brightest, warmest area.
4. Every chat is on the wide shot, with the listener looking at the speaker. No cut lands within 1.2 s of a punchline.
5. No studio shot is shorter than 3 s or longer than 12 s.
6. At most one push-in per episode, smooth (no 1-px jitter between frames), at most 5% scale.
7. No `wow`, `fist_pump`, `thumbs_up` or `facepalm` appears. Gesture count per segment stays within its cap (Max 2, Ada 1).
8. Script: no exclamation marks, banned hype words or puns; at most one dry line per story; no chat after a grave story.
9. Every number on screen and in speech matches the source summary exactly, with no count-up animation.
10. Measured pace: 165–175 wpm for stories and 180–190 wpm for chats. Max's rate is above Ada's.
11. No bed under story links or grave stories. Beds sit at least 18 dB under the voice, and stings come only at the NUMBER OF THE DAY and feature ends.
12. Each name super appears once per presenter. Headlines are at most 45 characters, with the kicker in the cyan tag.
13. The number card follows the spec: rule first, figure 0.1 s later, then held. The cursor blinks only at the open.
14. A neutral viewer shown 3 frames would describe the show as "a grown-up tech show", not "a kids' gadget show". Any doubt is a blocker.
