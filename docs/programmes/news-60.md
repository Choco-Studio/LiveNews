# NEWS IN 60 — style bible

Rapid headline round-up, about one minute, Sam Night solo. Builds on `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md` (studio, palette, safe areas) and only states what differs for this programme.

Research note (2026-10-02): every page fetch from this environment was blocked by the egress proxy. That included Wikipedia, YouTube and its thumbnails, BBC, Press Gazette, Ravensbourne, Typito, 1News, ABC, CNN and Sky Sports. So no on-air frames could be viewed. ## 1. Identity

The feeling is a stopwatch on a dark desk. The viewer gets five things that matter in the time it takes to boil a kettle, read by a calm, unhurried adult. They should leave feeling briefed, not rushed. The speed comes from the editing (one idea per item, hard cuts, a strap that never leaves the screen), not from fast talking or noisy graphics. It is for viewers dipping in between longer shows, and for anyone who has the stream on mute (the headline strap alone must tell the story). Compared with the rest of the channel:
- **WORLD NOW** is the warm red-and-navy flagship two-shot.
- **NEWS IN 60** is darker, sharper and single-voiced, with yellow as an instrument-dial accent.

## 2. Real references

**BBC Three "60 Seconds" (2001–2016): the direct ancestor.**
- **Format:** hourly from 7pm, five stories per bulletin, about 10 s per story. Sets of pictures ran for each story.
- **Countdown:** a countdown sat in the corner, and a line gradually crossed the screen to count down the seconds ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Seconds), [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0300707/plotsummary/)).
- **The key lesson:** it began in a "colourful studio" with a Faithless soundtrack. It was then revamped with "a more serious look": the presenter stood in a newsroom with a screen behind them, and the rave music was dropped ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Seconds)). The youth format grew up, as we must.
- **Later changes:** new graphics in 2008 and a remixed theme from Music 4 ([Music 4](http://www.music4.com/music/catalogue/540/bbc-three/bbc-three-60-seconds.html)).
- **What we take:** 5 × ~10 s items, pictures under every item, and a visible progress line.

**BBC One "BBC News Summary" (1986–2018) and today's "News in 90 Seconds".**
- **BBC News Summary:** 90 s with a sole newsreader. About a minute of national and international news, then 30 s of regional headlines and weather ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News_Summary)). It is a mainstream adult register, not a youth gimmick.
- **Current formats:** ABC News (Australia) runs "News in 90 Seconds", and one listed edition lasts 1:53 ([ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/newschannel/news-in-90-seconds)). 1News (NZ) runs a morning version made by its reporters ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/10/02/news-in-90-seconds-october-2/)). The title promises brevity; it is not a hard stopwatch.
- **What we take:** aim for 60 s and accept 55–65 s, rather than cutting a sentence mid-way.

**Sky.**
- We found no Sky News-branded "news in 90 seconds" bulletin.
- Sky's closest is "SSN in 60 Seconds" on Sky Sports News ([Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11095/11500246/sky-sports-news-in-60-seconds-all-the-latest-headlines)).
- On radio, IRN aired "IRN90": 90-second top-of-hour bulletins overnight, used by younger-audience stations. It was dropped when Sky News Radio took over IRN in 2009 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Radio_News)).

**NowThis: the item must work with the sound off.**
- NowThis moved to 15-second videos and replaced the anchor with on-screen text. "We were wasting time with people on camera" ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/nowthis-news-rebrands-mobile-social-world/)).
- One typeface (Calibre: bold, medium, medium italic). The colours are black, white and "a dash of yellow". Key words are larger and bold ([Typito](https://typito.com/blog/how-to-design-videos-like-nowthis/)).
- Digiday reported that 85% of Facebook video is watched without sound ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/)).
- **What we take:** black, white and yellow (already our accent), and a headline strap that carries each story alone. We keep Sam, but on screen less often than in other programmes.

**CBS: "60 Minutes" stopwatch, not "Up to the Minute".**
- "Up to the Minute" was a multi-hour overnight programme (1992–2015) with one anchor after 1993 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_to_the_Minute)). It is not a one-minute format; we borrow only its lone late-night anchor register.
- The CBS reference that fits is the 60 Minutes stopwatch tick, used for the open and the break bumpers. Composer Edd Kalehoff recorded it. Amplified ticking sounded like "rattling garbage can lids", so he notch-filtered it on a Moog ([Network News Music](https://www.networknewsmusic.com/60-minutes/)).
- The watch itself is in the Smithsonian ([NMAH](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1211921)).
- **What we take:** a tick is a premium sound only when filtered soft. Our open already uses a stopwatch dial (`public/js/scenes/opens/flash.js`).

**Euronews "No Comment": let one picture breathe.**
- Euronews launched in 1993 without presenters or studios. "No Comment" runs pictures with no voice-over ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronews), [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/nocomment)).
- **What we take:** one 2–3 s voiceless picture beat before the sign-off. That is the adult touch a kids' show never has.

**Radio pacing: Newsbeat and the 3-words-a-second rule.**
- **Newsbeat:** two 15-minute programmes a day plus short bulletins. In 2024, breakfast headlines went from 3 to 2 minutes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsbeat), [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2024/04/bbc-butchers-radio-1-breakfast-news-bulletins-greg-james-1235882812/)). Presenters were reportedly told to use short words and talk as if to a friend ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsbeat)).
- **Reading speed:** British radio favours 3 words/s (180 wpm), so a 20 s lead is 60 words ([Boyd, *Broadcast Journalism*](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/broadcast-journalism-6th/9780240810249/xhtml/ch17.xhtml)). Listeners rated 170–190 wpm as "normal", and faster delivery causes overload ([Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/media/radio-news-pace-words/)).
- **Shot length:** in ITV bulletins the median is 6.3 s (min 2.7 s), within a fixed structure whatever the story ([Redfern](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263129239_The_structure_of_ITV_news_bulletins)).

**Counter-references (what not to do):**
- Headline News' 2001 multi-element screen was called a "jumbled mess" and scaled back in 2005 ([CNN 2002](https://edition.cnn.com/2002/US/08/02/hln.behind.intro/index.html), [HLN summary](https://kids.kiddle.co/HLN_(TV_network))).
- MSNBC stopped scrolling its ticker in 2018 ([Variety](https://variety.com/2018/tv/news/msnbc-removes-news-ticker-1202754729/)).
- BBC Three's own "colourful studio" phase (see 60 Seconds above).

## 3. Our style

### Delivery and writing (writer prompt and prosody)
- **Words per minute:** target **175 wpm (165–185)**, measured from word timings. Calibrate Sam's neural rate to hit this. Never go above 190: urgency comes from the cuts.
- **Budget:** 120–145 spoken words per episode.
  - Intro: at most 12 words.
  - Items: 18–28 words each; the lead item at most 32 words (2 sentences).
  - Sign-off: at most 10 words.
  - Config: change `storyLength` to "1 sentence (lead up to 2), 18–28 words, max 170 characters".
- **Sentences:**
  - Start with the subject or the place: "In Lagos, ...".
  - Use active verbs and present or present perfect tense.
  - Put one fact per item, and attribute compactly at the end ("..., Reuters reports.").
  - No subordinate clause before the verb, no parentheses, no lists.
- **Register:** plain, adult public-broadcaster English, with short words (Newsbeat). No slang, no "guys", no "huge".
- **Humour:**
  - At most one dry line per episode: either the final clause of the last item, if that item is not grave, or the sign-off.
  - It must be deadpan and understatement only. No puns, no exclamation marks, no "wow".
  - Never adjacent to a grave item.
  - Solo show, so no banter and no tosses.
- **Signposts:** none between items ("meanwhile", "next up" and "in other news" are banned). The cut and the tick do that job. Entering a round-up run: "Around the world." (2 words).
- **Fixed frames, varied from programme data:**
  - Intro: "This is NEWS IN 60. I'm Sam Night."
  - Sign-off: "That's the minute. [Next programme] is next." or "That's the minute. I'm Sam Night."
- **Numbers:**
  - At most one figure per item, written in digits with its unit ("2 billion dollars", "40 percent"); the normaliser expands them.
  - No decimals beyond one place. Years are spoken naturally.
  - Never round in a way that changes precision unless the summary itself says "about".
- **Places:**
  - Name the city first, then the country if it is not world-famous ("In Daegu, South Korea, ...").
  - On map items the place is the first 1–3 words, so the pin lands on the word.
- **Prosody:**
  - Sentence-final falls; no upspeak.
  - Light stress on each item's first content word.
  - Pauses: about 0.25 s at commas, about 0.45 s between sentences, and 0.7 s between items (the cut and the tick sit in that gap).
  - Emotion: `neutral` or `serious`; `happy` only for the last item if it is light.

### Structure (built live from feeds; nothing pre-planned)
1. **Open (4 s):** the existing stopwatch open, then a hard cut on the downbeat.
2. **`intro` (3–4 s):** Sam in MCU.
3. **Lead `story` (9–13 s):** the biggest story; `breaking` goes here, with a red tag.
4. **3–4 `story` items (7–10 s each):** variety across categories (world, business, tech, science).
   - If 2–3 candidates have a gazetteer location, run them as a `roundup` (`shot: map`, at most 16 words each).
   - If fewer than 2 locations validate, drop the round-up (existing fallback).
5. **Picture beat (2–3 s, optional):** the director holds the best-scored image of the episode with the bed only and a place caption. Skip it if the run is already over 60 s or the image is missing.
6. **`outro` (3 s)**, then the **end card (2 s)**: "NEXT · PROGRAMME · HH:MM" from the rotation.

Timing: from the open's downbeat cut to the last word is 55–65 s. If the queue is late, drop items from the tail (4 is fine) rather than speed up the voice.

### Set and light variant (`flash` theme in `public/js/set.js`)
- **Background:** one step darker than WORLD NOW, using black/ink with a Bayer falloff to slate behind the head. No newsroom bokeh and no windows.
- **Key light:** neutral white (the CNN-style "about 5000 K" look) on Sam, slightly camera-left, faces in `P.skin`/`P.skinShade` untinted.
- **Rim light:** 1 px `P.silver`. The face is the brightest area.
- **Accent:** `P.yellow` only.
  - Use it on the wall-dial ticks and one desk LED line. Change `led` to a steady `P.yellow` (drop `P.white` and `P.orange`).
  - Remove the `glow` spill and the `P.cream` tint (or keep the tint at ≤ 4%).
  - Saturated pixels must be ≤ 6% of the frame, excluding wall pictures.
- **Wall:**
  - Idle: the stopwatch dial from the open (same geometry for continuity). It lights one tick per 1/60 of the episode's planned duration, a steady step, never blinking.
  - In a story: the item's picture with a 1 px `P.steel` bezel and the dial hidden. Only one counter is visible at a time; the strap rule takes over.
  - Keep the bottom 16 px dark.

### Camera and directing (runtime rules, seeded per episode)
- **Shots:**
  - **MCU-L:** Sam in the left third, with the wall picture in the right two-thirds (the over-the-shoulder box).
  - **MCU:** centred.
  - **FULL:** the picture full screen, with Sam in voice-over.
  - **MAP.**
  - No wide shot except the end card background.
- **Intro:** MCU. **Outro:** MCU, holding 1 s after the last word.
- **Lead item:** MCU-L until the first sentence ends (at least 3 s), then FULL if there is an image.
- **Other items:** cut exactly on the item's first word (±2 frames).
  - Alternate in-vision (MCU-L) and out-of-vision (FULL) by item index plus the seed, so there are never more than 2 consecutive items without Sam in vision.
  - With no image, use MCU-L with the category plate on the wall.
- **Mid-item cut:** only at a sentence boundary, and only if the item runs over 7 s.
- **Shot length:** 2.5–9 s. Cut, never dissolve or wipe.
- **Round-up run:** one MAP shot. Pan between pins in 0.7 s (ease-in-out, integer px), hold at least 2.5 s per place, and cut in and out.
- **Camera:** locked in studio shots.
  - Pictures get a slow push or pan of at most 4 px/s and at most 3% zoom per shot.
  - The camera never moves during a cut-heavy run.
- **Gestures:** at most 2 per episode, from `nod`, `lean_in` (lead item only) and `papers` (sign-off). Never `wave`, `wow`, `thumbs_up`, `fist_pump`, `laugh` or `facepalm`. Hands rest between gestures; head micro-motion only.

### Graphics
- **Strap:** up from 0.6 s into the lead and never leaves until the outro. One block for the whole minute.
  - **Tag row (11 px):** `P.yellow` fill with `P.black` 5x7 text "2/5  BUSINESS". The source is in black 3x5 micro type, right-aligned. Breaking turns the row `P.red` with `P.white` text "BREAKING".
  - **Headline bar (17 px):** `P.ink` with `P.white` 5x7 caps, **at most 36 characters** (items last only about 9 s), present tense, no articles.
  - **Progress rule:** 1 px `P.yellow` over a `P.slate` track along the bar's bottom edge, x 19→365. It grows linearly with episode time and lands at the end on the sign-off's first word. It never overflows or reverses: scale it to the planned duration.
- **Motion:**
  - Strap in: 0.35 s ease-out wipe.
  - Item change: text push-up 0.2 s with integer pixels; the bar stays.
  - Exit: 0.25 s.
  - No overshoot or bounce anywhere.
- **Bug and clock:** standard. Show the programme tag beside the bug for the whole minute.
- **Ticker:** frozen on one static item, "NEXT: [PROGRAMME] HH:MM". No flipping or scrolling during the minute.
- **Map:** `P.yellow` 3x3 pin with a 1 px `P.black` outline, and the place in `P.white` 5x7 on a `P.ink` plate. No route lines.
- **Fact card:** at most one per episode, lead item only. A display-2x `P.white` figure, a micro `P.fog` label and a 2 px yellow underline. In 0.25 s, hold at least 2.5 s, then cut out.

### Music and sound
- **Bed:** one continuous bed under the whole minute (Newsbeat-style). It changes colour, not tracks, per item.
  - Tempo 120–132 BPM.
  - Key: E minor / G major, as the existing `FLASH` package.
  - Instruments: muted `pulse25` eighth-note bass, a low-passed square pad (cutoff ≤ 1.2 kHz), and a soft filtered noise tick on off-beats (the Kalehoff lesson: dull it). No lead melody under speech.
  - Level: 20–24 dB below voice RMS while Sam speaks, with a 3 dB duck on speech onsets.
- **Item tick:** one soft tick (≤ 120 ms, low-passed) exactly on each item cut. This is the only sting between items.
- **Grave items:** the ticks and bass drop out; the pad holds alone.
- **Silence:** none mid-programme. The picture beat is bed only, no voice.
- **Signature:** the channel's 4-note signature plays only in the open and resolves on the sign-off (`Gadd9` bell chord) as the dial and the rule complete.

### Transitions
- **Into the programme:** channel ident, then the open, then a hard cut on the downbeat.
- **Between items:** a straight cut plus the strap text push plus the tick. Never a stinger, whoosh or wipe.
- **Round-up:** cut to MAP on "Around the world.", and cut out on the next item's first word.
- **Out:** outro MCU, then a 0.5 s cut to the end card, then the break ident. A stinger (0.8 s) is allowed only into the break.

### Do / Don't
**Do**
- Make the strap readable on mute.
- Keep one item per idea, cut on the item's first word, and keep the face as the brightest area.
- Use yellow as a dial colour, not a theme. Stick to black, white and one accent.
- Keep the voice at 175 wpm and the bed low.
- Keep an honest progress rule.

**Don't (childish or cheap)**
- Use a bouncing stopwatch, a stopwatch with a face, comic "TICK!" captions, or exclamation marks.
- Use rainbow item colours, flashing countdown numbers or seconds counting aloud.
- Show "Hurry!"-style copy or let the voice speed up to fit.
- Use whooshes between items, sirens, or a ticking bed under deaths.
- Fill the screen with boxes (Headline News 2001), use a scrolling ticker plus a strap plus a dial all moving, or show a wide two-shot of an empty chair.
- Use wave or thumbs-up gestures, or let the camera drift.

## 4. Acceptance checklist
1. On 10 seeded offline episodes, the time from the open's downbeat cut to the last word is 55–65 s, and the end card is ≤ 2.5 s.
2. Measured delivery is 165–185 wpm. No item is over 11 s, and the lead is ≤ 13 s.
3. There are 4–6 items, each one sentence (lead ≤ 2), with ≤ 1 dry line, no "!", and no humour next to a grave item.
4. Every item change is a straight cut within 2 frames of the first word, with zero stingers or wipes between items.
5. No shot is shorter than 2.5 s or longer than 9 s, and Sam is in vision at least every other item, including the intro and outro.
6. The strap is continuously on screen from the lead to the outro, headlines are ≤ 36 characters and never truncated, and text pushes take ≤ 0.25 s.
7. The progress rule and the wall dial reach the end on the sign-off (±0.2 s) and never reverse or overflow.
8. Yellow is ≤ 6% of studio-frame pixels (excluding pictures), red appears only in the bug or BREAKING, and accent text is black on yellow only.
9. Studio shots have 0 px camera drift between frames, and picture moves are ≤ 4 px/s.
10. The bed measures 20–24 dB under the voice, ticks are absent on grave items, and the bed resolves on the sign-off.
11. There are ≤ 2 gestures per episode, none from the light set.
12. Map items show a pin plus the place name, pans take 0.6–0.9 s, and each place holds ≥ 2.5 s.
13. Graphic easing curves never exceed 1.0 (no overshoot), the ticker is static, and at most three zones move per frame.
14. Faces are the brightest warm area, and the background is at least two palette steps darker.
15. Kids' test: a critic shown 6 random frames would not call it a children's programme. Any cute or bouncy element is a BLOCKER.
